## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Expressions vs Equations
- **tts**:
  - An expression like three x plus five is just a phrase. An equation is a full sentence with an equals sign.
  - The side on the left is called L H S, and the side on the right is called R H S.
- **on_screen**: Cards such as 3x + 5, 2x − 7 = 11 and 4y slide in; child sorts them into "Expression" and "Equation" bins, then taps the LHS and RHS of an equation.
- **check**: Is 3x + 5 an equation? — Answer: No, it has no equals sign

### step_2: The Balance Rule
- **tts**:
  - An equation is like a balanced weighing scale.
  - Whatever you do to one side, add, subtract, multiply or divide, you must do to the other side too.
- **on_screen**: Animated balance with 2x − 5 on the left pan and 9 on the right; child adds 5 to both pans, then halves both, until x = 7 remains.
- **check**: Solve 2x − 5 = 9. — Answer: x = 7

### step_3: Variables on Both Sides
- **tts**:
  - When x appears on both sides, gather the x terms on one side and the numbers on the other.
  - Remember, when a term moves across the equals sign, its sign changes.
- **on_screen**: Drag-to-move workspace for 4x + 7 = 2x + 15; when 2x is dragged left it turns into −2x, and when 7 is dragged right it turns into −7.
- **check**: Solve 4x + 7 = 2x + 15. — Answer: x = 4

### step_4: Brackets and the Tricky Minus
- **tts**:
  - Open brackets first. A minus sign outside a bracket changes the sign of every term inside.
  - So minus three times x plus one becomes minus three x minus three.
- **on_screen**: Bracket-opening animation for 2 − 3(x + 1) = 8; each term inside flashes as the −3 multiplies it. A common-mistake popup appears if the child writes +3.
- **check**: Solve 2 − 3(x + 1) = 8. — Answer: x = −3

### step_5: Equations with Fractions and Decimals
- **tts**:
  - Fractions look scary, but you can clear them by multiplying every term by the L C M of the denominators.
  - For decimals, multiply by ten or a hundred to make whole numbers.
- **on_screen**: The equation x/3 + x/6 = 5 is shown; child picks the LCM 6 from options, every term is multiplied by it, and the equation becomes 2x + x = 30.
- **check**: Solve x/3 + x/6 = 5. — Answer: x = 10

### step_6: Check by Substitution
- **tts**:
  - Always check your answer by putting it back into the original equation.
  - If the left side equals the right side, your solution is correct!
- **on_screen**: A "checker" panel: child enters a value for x and both sides are evaluated live, turning green when LHS = RHS.
- **check**: Is x = 3 a solution of 5x + 3 = 18? — Answer: Yes, 15 + 3 = 18

### step_7: Turning Word Problems into Equations
- **tts**:
  - Read the problem, choose one unknown and call it x, then write every other quantity using x.
  - Ages, coins, perimeters and consecutive numbers can all be solved this way.
- **on_screen**: Story card: "Aarav's father is three times Aarav's age and their ages add up to 52." Child fills a table (Aarav = x, Father = 3x), builds x + 3x = 52 and solves.
- **check**: Solve x + 3x = 52 to find Aarav's age. — Answer: 13 years

## Practice Set A

### Q01
- **stem**: Which of these is an equation?
- **options**:
  - A) 3x + 5
  - B) 2x − 7 = 11
  - C) 4y
  - D) x + y
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: An equation must have an equals sign with an LHS and an RHS, and the others are only expressions.
- **skill**: equation_basics
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q02
- **stem**: Solve: x + 9 = 4
- **options**:
  - A) 13
  - B) -13
  - C) 5
  - D) -5
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: Subtracting 9 from both sides gives x = 4 − 9 = −5, while 13 wrongly adds 9.
- **skill**: solve_basic
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q03
- **stem**: Solve: 3x = −21

- **skill**: solve_basic
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q20
