- **options**:
  - A) ₹1,470
  - B) ₹1,440
  - C) ₹1,575
  - D) ₹1,512
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: 20% off ₹2,000 leaves ₹1,600. The extra 10% is taken off ₹1,600, which is ₹160, leaving ₹1,440. GST of 5% on ₹1,440 is ₹72, so he pays ₹1,512. ₹1,470 is tempting because it adds the two discounts to make 30% off ₹2,000, but the second discount is 10% of the already reduced price, so together they take off less than 30%.
- **hint**: Apply the offers on Chaitanya's tag one at a time; each later percentage is worked out on the price left after the step before.

### Q03
- **id**: g8-maths-ch03-ach-x-03
- **stem**: At Anaya's local shop in Kochi, the price of a kilogram of cashews went up by 20% in June, came down by 25% in July and went up by 10% in August. Taking all three changes together, what happened to the price?
- **options**:
  - A) a 1% decrease
  - B) a 5% increase
  - C) a 1% increase
  - D) a 5% decrease
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: Each change acts on the price left by the one before. Start from ₹100 for easy counting: up 20% gives ₹120; down 25% of ₹120 gives ₹90; up 10% of ₹90 gives ₹99. So the price ends ₹1 lower on every ₹100: a 1% decrease. Choosing a 5% increase is tempting because it adds and subtracts the percentages as 20 − 25 + 10 = 5, but each percentage is taken of a different price.
- **hint**: Pretend Anaya's cashews started at a round price and apply each month's change in turn to the newest price.
- **skill**: chain_of_percentage_changes
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q04
- **id**: g8-maths-ch03-ach-x-04
- **stem**: Namita pays ₹2,950 for a microwave oven. This includes 18% GST charged on the shop's selling price. The shop had sold it at 20% off its marked price and still made a 25% profit on what it paid for the oven. How much higher was the marked price than the shop's cost price?
- **options**:
  - A) ₹625
  - B) ₹1,125
  - C) ₹500
  - D) ₹1,250
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Without GST the selling price is 2,950 ÷ 1.18 = ₹2,500. That is 125% of the cost price, so the cost price is 2,500 ÷ 1.25 = ₹2,000. It is also 80% of the marked price, so the marked price is 2,500 ÷ 0.8 = ₹3,125. The marked price is 3,125 − 2,000 = ₹1,125 more than the cost price. ₹625 is tempting because it is the gap between the marked price and the selling price, the discount, but the question compares the marked price with the cost price.
- **hint**: Remove the GST from Namita's bill first, then work back separately to the shop's cost price and to its marked price.
- **skill**: working_back_from_a_gst_bill_to_cost_and_marked_price
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q05
- **id**: g8-maths-ch03-ach-x-05
- **stem**: In Lucknow, the price of mustard oil rises from ₹160 to ₹200 per litre. Dhruvi's family wants to spend exactly the same amount on oil each month as before. By what percentage must they reduce the amount of oil they use?
- **options**:
  - A) 25%
  - B) 40%
  - C) 15%
  - D) 20%
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: Suppose the family spent ₹800 a month: that bought 800 ÷ 160 = 5 L before and buys only 800 ÷ 200 = 4 L now. Using 1 L less out of 5 L is a cut of 1/5 = 20%. 25% is tempting because it is the rise in price (40 out of 160), but the cut in oil is measured against the old amount used, and paying ₹200 instead of ₹160 means each rupee buys 4/5 as much.
- **hint**: Pick a convenient monthly amount Dhruvi's family spends and find how many litres it buys at each price.
- **skill**: cutting_use_to_keep_spending_fixed
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
