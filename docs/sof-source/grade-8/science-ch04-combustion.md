# Chapter 4: Combustion and Flame

## Meta
- Grade: 8
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: combustion, flame, fuel, ignition temperature, fire triangle, calorific value, pollution
- Source basis: NCERT Class 8 themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Distinguish combustible from non-combustible substances and give everyday examples of each.
- Explain ignition temperature and why some fuels catch fire more easily than others.
- Describe rapid, spontaneous, and explosive combustion, and list the three conditions of the fire triangle.
- Identify the zones of a candle flame and link colour to temperature.
- Compare fuels using calorific value, and name harmful products of burning fossil fuels.
- Suggest safe ways to control fire and choose cleaner fuels such as LPG and CNG for common uses.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Will it burn?
- **Visual:** A sorting table with wood, paper, coal, iron nail, glass, stone, and kerosene. A virtual match approaches each sample.
- **TTS:**
  - "A substance that burns in air to give heat and light is called combustible."
  - "Wood, coal, kerosene, and LPG are combustible. Iron, glass, and stone are non-combustible."
  - "Burning with heat and light is combustion. The substance that burns is the fuel."
- **Interaction:** Drag each sample into Combustible or Non-combustible. Correct drops glow; wrong ones bounce back with a hint.

### Scene 2: Ignition temperature and types of combustion
- **Visual:** Three clips play: a match lighting paper (rapid), a heap of oily rags slowly smoking then flaming (spontaneous), and a firecracker bursting (explosion). A thermometer graphic shows ignition temperature.
- **TTS:**
  - "Ignition temperature is the lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire."
  - "Rapid combustion gives heat and light quickly, like LPG in a stove."
  - "Spontaneous combustion starts on its own, without an external flame, when a substance slowly heats up."
  - "An explosion is a sudden reaction that releases a large amount of gas and heat with a bang."
- **Interaction:** Match each video clip to Rapid, Spontaneous, or Explosion. Feedback names one more real-life example.

### Scene 3: The fire triangle and putting fires out
- **Visual:** A triangle labelled Fuel, Oxygen (air), and Heat. Removing any side collapses the fire. Extinguishers appear: water for paper/wood, CO2 or dry powder for oil and electrical fires, a fire blanket for a small kitchen blaze.
- **TTS:**
  - "Fire needs fuel, oxygen, and heat. Remove any one and the fire goes out."
  - "Water cools the fuel below its ignition temperature, but never pour water on oil or electrical fires."
  - "Carbon dioxide and dry powder cut off the air supply."
  - "A fire blanket smothers flames by blocking oxygen."
- **Interaction:** For each fire scene, pick the safest control method. Wrong choices flash a safety tip.

### Scene 4: Zones of a candle flame
- **Visual:** A labelled candle flame with three zones: dark innermost (unburnt wax vapour), middle yellow luminous zone, and outermost blue non-luminous zone. A thin wire held across shows which zone is hottest.
- **TTS:**
  - "A candle flame has three main zones."
  - "The innermost zone is dark and coolest. It has unburnt vapour."
  - "The middle luminous zone is yellow because of glowing carbon particles. It gives most of the light."
  - "The outermost non-luminous zone is blue and hottest. Complete combustion happens here."
- **Interaction:** Tap each zone to hear its job, then place a matchstick tip in the hottest zone to blacken or ignite as predicted.

### Scene 5: Fuels, calorific value, and clean choices
- **Visual:** A fuel comparison board showing wood, coal, petrol, LPG, and CNG with bars for calorific value. A city skyline shows smoke, acid rain, and a cleaner CNG bus.
- **TTS:**
  - "Calorific value is the heat produced when one kilogram of fuel burns completely. It is measured in kilojoules per kilogram."
  - "A good fuel has a high calorific value, burns without much smoke, and is easy and safe to store."
  - "Burning coal and petrol can release carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide, and oxides of nitrogen."
  - "These gases can cause acid rain and add to global warming."
  - "LPG and CNG burn more cleanly and are preferred for homes and many vehicles."
- **Interaction:** Rank fuels by calorific value, then match each pollution problem to the gas that mainly causes it. A recap card lists key ideas: combustible fuels, ignition temperature, types of combustion, fire triangle, flame zones, and clean fuels.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** A substance that burns in air to produce heat and light is called a —
**Options:**
A) Non-combustible substance
B) Combustible substance
C) Coolant
D) Insulator
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Combustible substances catch fire and burn in air, giving heat and light. Non-combustible substances do not burn.

### Q2
**Stem:** Which of these is a non-combustible substance?
**Options:**
A) Wood
B) Paper
C) Kerosene
D) Glass
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Glass does not burn in air. Wood, paper, and kerosene are fuels that can burn.

### Q3
**Stem:** The lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire is called its —
**Options:**
A) Boiling point
B) Melting point
C) Ignition temperature
D) Freezing point
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Ignition temperature is the lowest temperature at which a substance starts burning.

### Q4
**Stem:** LPG burns with a blue flame on a stove almost as soon as the burner is lit. This is an example of —
**Options:**
A) Spontaneous combustion
B) Rapid combustion
C) Explosion only
D) Smouldering without oxygen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Rapid combustion produces heat and light quickly. LPG and CNG show rapid combustion.

### Q5
**Stem:** Oily rags left in a warm cupboard slowly heat up and catch fire without a match. This is —
**Options:**
A) Rapid combustion
B) Spontaneous combustion
C) Complete combustion only
D) An acid–base reaction
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Spontaneous combustion begins on its own when a substance slowly reaches its ignition temperature.

### Q6
**Stem:** A firecracker bursting with a loud bang is an example of —
**Options:**
A) Spontaneous combustion
B) An explosion
C) Slow rusting
D) Photosynthesis
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** An explosion is a sudden combustion that releases a large amount of gas and heat with sound.

### Q7
**Stem:** The three essential conditions for a fire are fuel, heat, and —
**Options:**
A) Nitrogen
B) Oxygen (air)
C) Carbon dioxide
D) Argon
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fire needs fuel, oxygen from air, and enough heat. Removing any one puts the fire out.

### Q8
**Stem:** Why should you never pour water on a burning oil pan?
**Options:**
A) Water raises the ignition temperature of oil
B) Water sinks under oil and the oil can splash and spread the fire
C) Water turns oil into LPG
D) Water increases the calorific value of oil
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Oil floats on water. Water can vaporise under the oil and throw burning oil around, spreading the fire.

### Q9
**Stem:** Carbon dioxide fire extinguishers put out fire mainly by —
**Options:**
A) Raising the fuel's ignition temperature
B) Cutting off the supply of air (oxygen)
C) Adding more fuel
D) Increasing the flame temperature
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** CO2 is denser than air and forms a blanket that cuts off oxygen from the fuel.

### Q10
**Stem:** In a candle flame, the outermost blue zone is —
**Options:**
A) The coolest zone
B) The hottest zone with complete combustion
C) Made only of unburnt wax
D) Where no oxygen reaches
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The outermost non-luminous zone has plenty of air, so combustion is complete and the temperature is highest.

### Q11
**Stem:** The yellow luminous zone of a candle flame gives light mainly because of —
**Options:**
A) Glowing unburnt carbon particles
B) Completely burnt water vapour only
C) Cold air from the room
D) Nitrogen gas
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Incomplete burning in the middle zone produces hot carbon particles that glow and give yellow light.

### Q12
**Stem:** The innermost dark zone of a candle flame contains mainly —
**Options:**
A) Completely burnt ash
B) Unburnt wax vapour
C) Liquid water
D) Solid wax only
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Wax vapour rises into the innermost zone before it mixes well with air, so little burning happens there.

### Q13
**Stem:** Calorific value of a fuel is the heat produced when —
**Options:**
A) One gram of fuel melts
B) One kilogram of fuel burns completely
C) One litre of water freezes
D) One mole of oxygen is cooled
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Calorific value is heat from complete burning of 1 kg of fuel, usually in kJ/kg.

### Q14
**Stem:** Which unit is commonly used for calorific value?
**Options:**
A) newton
B) pascal
C) kilojoule per kilogram (kJ/kg)
D) metre per second
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Calorific value is heat energy per unit mass, so kJ/kg is the usual unit in school science.

### Q15
**Stem:** Which of these is generally considered a cleaner fuel for city buses?
**Options:**
A) Raw coal only
B) Wet wood
C) CNG
D) Damp cow-dung cakes
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Compressed natural gas (CNG) burns more cleanly with less smoke than coal or wood.

### Q16
**Stem:** Burning coal that contains sulphur can produce sulphur dioxide, which may lead to —
**Options:**
A) Acid rain
B) An increase in soil nitrogen only
C) Colder winters only
D) More oxygen in air
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Sulphur dioxide dissolves in rainwater and forms acids, contributing to acid rain.

### Q17
**Stem:** Which gas released by burning fossil fuels is most linked to global warming?
**Options:**
A) Oxygen
B) Nitrogen
C) Carbon dioxide
D) Argon
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Extra carbon dioxide traps heat in the atmosphere and contributes to global warming.

### Q18
**Stem:** A good domestic fuel should —
**Options:**
A) Have a very low calorific value and produce thick smoke
B) Be hard to light and leave a lot of ash
C) Have a high calorific value and burn with little smoke
D) Be stored as an open powder near a flame
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Useful fuels give plenty of heat, light easily in a controlled way, and produce little harmful smoke.

### Q19
**Stem:** White phosphorus catches fire in air at room temperature because —
**Options:**
A) Its ignition temperature is below room temperature
B) It is non-combustible
C) It needs no oxygen
D) It is colder than ice
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** If the ignition temperature is lower than the surroundings, the substance can catch fire easily.

### Q20
**Stem:** Forest fires can start when dry leaves catch fire from the heat of the Sun because dry leaves have —
**Options:**
A) A very high ignition temperature
B) A low ignition temperature
C) No carbon
D) Only nitrogen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Dry leaves ignite at a relatively low temperature, so strong heating can start a fire.

### Q21
**Stem:** The fuel used in many homes for cooking, stored in cylinders, is —
**Options:**
A) LPG
B) Solid iron
C) Glass wool
D) Pure nitrogen
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) is a common cooking fuel stored under pressure in cylinders.

### Q22
**Stem:** Incomplete combustion of a carbon fuel often produces —
**Options:**
A) Only pure oxygen
B) Carbon monoxide, which is poisonous
C) More nitrogen for breathing
D) Only harmless steam
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** When there is not enough air, carbon burns to carbon monoxide, a dangerous gas.

### Q23
**Stem:** Water puts out a wood fire mainly by —
**Options:**
A) Adding more oxygen
B) Cooling the fuel below its ignition temperature and limiting air contact
C) Turning wood into coal
D) Raising the flame temperature
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Water cools the burning material and the steam formed can also help keep air away.

### Q24
**Stem:** Which statement about combustion is correct?
**Options:**
A) Combustion never needs oxygen
B) Combustion always happens below freezing point
C) Combustion is a chemical process that needs a combustible substance and usually oxygen
D) Only metals can burn
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Combustion is the burning of a fuel, usually in oxygen, that releases heat and often light.
## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** Which pair correctly matches a substance with whether it is combustible?
**Options:**
A) Stone — combustible; coal — non-combustible
B) Iron nail — combustible; paper — non-combustible
C) Kerosene — combustible; glass — non-combustible
D) Water — combustible; wood — non-combustible
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Kerosene burns; glass does not. Stone, iron, and water are not fuels in ordinary air.

### Q2
**Stem:** A matchstick tip has chemicals with a low ignition temperature so that —
**Options:**
A) The stick never burns
B) Friction heat can light the tip easily
C) The tip becomes an insulator
D) Oxygen is removed from air
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The tip chemicals ignite with the small heat from rubbing, then light the wood of the stick.

### Q3
**Stem:** Spontaneous combustion differs from rapid combustion because it —
**Options:**
A) Always needs a match flame at the start
B) Can start without an external flame when heat builds up slowly
C) Never produces heat
D) Happens only under water
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In spontaneous combustion, slow heating reaches the ignition temperature without a separate flame.

### Q4
**Stem:** During an explosion, a large volume of gas forms suddenly. This mainly causes —
**Options:**
A) A quiet cooling effect
B) A sudden increase in pressure and a loud sound
C) The fuel to freeze
D) Air to turn into liquid oxygen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Rapid gas production raises pressure violently, which we hear and feel as an explosion.

### Q5
**Stem:** Cutting off air from a small fire with a wet blanket works because —
**Options:**
A) The blanket increases oxygen
B) Oxygen needed for burning is blocked
C) The fuel's calorific value rises
D) Ignition temperature becomes zero
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Without oxygen, the fire triangle is broken and burning stops.

### Q6
**Stem:** For an electrical fire in a socket, the safer first action is usually to —
**Options:**
A) Spray plenty of water on the wires
B) Switch off the power if safe, and use a CO2 or dry-powder extinguisher — not water
C) Pour kerosene to dilute the fire
D) Blow with a fan to add more air
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Water conducts electricity and can cause shock. Cut power and use a suitable extinguisher.

### Q7
**Stem:** A thin copper wire held across a candle flame gets hottest in the —
**Options:**
A) Innermost dark zone
B) Middle yellow zone only at the wick
C) Outermost blue zone
D) Cold air far below the candle
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The outer non-luminous zone has complete combustion and the highest temperature.

### Q8
**Stem:** Goldsmiths often use the outermost zone of a flame when heating gold because —
**Options:**
A) It is the coolest and darkest
B) It gives the highest temperature for melting and working the metal
C) It contains only unburnt wax
D) It has no oxygen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The outer zone burns completely and is hottest, which helps melt or work metals.

### Q9
**Stem:** Which fuel usually has a higher calorific value than wood?
**Options:**
A) Wet leaves
B) Damp cow dung
C) LPG
D) Green twigs
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** LPG releases much more heat per kilogram than wood or wet biomass fuels.

### Q10
**Stem:** Acid rain is linked to oxides of sulphur and nitrogen mainly because they —
**Options:**
A) Make rain alkaline like soap
B) Dissolve in rainwater to form acids
C) Turn rain into pure oxygen
D) Freeze all clouds instantly
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** These oxides react with water in the air to form acids that fall as acid rain.

### Q11
**Stem:** Choosing CNG over petrol for many vehicles helps the environment mainly by —
**Options:**
A) Producing more thick black smoke
B) Reducing harmful exhaust gases compared with petrol or diesel
C) Removing all oxygen from cities
D) Increasing sulphur in the air
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** CNG burns more cleanly, so vehicles release fewer pollutants.

### Q12
**Stem:** A substance with a very high ignition temperature is —
**Options:**
A) Easier to set on fire with a small spark
B) Harder to set on fire under normal conditions
C) Always explosive
D) Always a liquid
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** High ignition temperature means you must heat it a lot before it catches fire.

### Q13
**Stem:** Inflammable substances such as petrol and alcohol —
**Options:**
A) Have high ignition temperatures and never burn
B) Have low ignition temperatures and catch fire easily
C) Cannot be fuels
D) Burn only in pure nitrogen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Inflammable liquids ignite easily because their ignition temperatures are low.

### Q14
**Stem:** The middle zone of a candle flame is luminous mainly due to —
**Options:**
A) Complete combustion of all carbon
B) Partial combustion leaving hot carbon particles
C) Ice crystals in the flame
D) Only ultraviolet light
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Limited air in the middle zone leaves glowing carbon particles that emit yellow light.

### Q15
**Stem:** Which product of incomplete combustion is especially dangerous in closed rooms?
**Options:**
A) Water vapour only
B) Carbon monoxide
C) Argon
D) Pure nitrogen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Carbon monoxide binds strongly in blood and can be fatal in poorly ventilated spaces.

### Q16
**Stem:** The fire triangle collapses if you remove —
**Options:**
A) Only the colour of the flame
B) Any one of fuel, oxygen, or heat
C) Only the sound of burning
D) Gravity
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** All three conditions are needed. Take away fuel, air, or heat and burning stops.

### Q17
**Stem:** Calorific value helps us compare fuels because it tells —
**Options:**
A) How heavy the fuel cylinder is
B) How much heat one kilogram of fuel can give when burnt completely
C) The colour of the flame only
D) The melting point of glass
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fuels with higher calorific value give more heat per kilogram.

### Q18
**Stem:** Burning plastics and some synthetic materials can be especially harmful because they may release —
**Options:**
A) Only oxygen
B) Toxic gases and smoke
C) Pure drinking water
D) Extra nitrogen for plants only
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Many plastics produce poisonous gases and thick smoke when they burn.

### Q19
**Stem:** A candle flame goes out when covered with a glass because —
**Options:**
A) The wax becomes non-combustible instantly
B) Oxygen inside is used up and not replaced
C) The ignition temperature rises to infinity
D) Glass adds nitrogen fuel
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The flame uses the trapped oxygen; when oxygen is gone, combustion stops.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which is the best description of a fuel?
**Options:**
A) Any substance that never reacts with oxygen
B) A combustible substance that is burnt to obtain heat (and often light)
C) Only solid metals
D) Only water and sand
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fuels are materials we burn on purpose for useful heat or light.

### Q21
**Stem:** Unburnt carbon particles from smoky fuels can cause —
**Options:**
A) Clearer lungs
B) Respiratory problems and blackening of buildings
C) More ozone at ground level that always heals lungs
D) Only a sweet smell
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Soot and smoke irritate the lungs and dirty walls and monuments.

### Q22
**Stem:** Dry powder extinguishers are useful for oil fires because they —
**Options:**
A) Add water under the oil
B) Cut off oxygen without spreading burning oil the way water can
C) Increase the oil's calorific value
D) Turn oil into LPG
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Powder blankets the burning oil and stops air reaching it.

### Q23
**Stem:** Why is the innermost zone of a flame least hot?
**Options:**
A) It has the most oxygen and complete burning
B) Fuel vapour there has not mixed well with air, so little combustion occurs
C) It is made of solid diamond
D) It is outside the flame entirely
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Without enough oxygen mixed in, burning is minimal and the zone stays cooler.

### Q24
**Stem:** Global warming concerns from fossil fuels are mainly about —
**Options:**
A) Extra carbon dioxide trapping heat
B) Extra argon making rain pink
C) Less nitrogen in fertilisers only
D) Colder ocean water from more oxygen
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** CO2 from burning coal, oil, and gas adds to the greenhouse effect.
