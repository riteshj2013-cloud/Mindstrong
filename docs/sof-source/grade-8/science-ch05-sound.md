# Chapter 5: Sound

## Meta
- Grade: 8
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: sound, vibration, frequency, amplitude, pitch, loudness, ultrasound, echo, ear, noise
- Source basis: NCERT Class 8 themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Explain that sound is produced by vibrating objects and needs a material medium to travel.
- Relate amplitude to loudness and frequency to pitch, and use hertz as the unit of frequency.
- State the approximate audible range for humans and give examples of infrasound and ultrasound.
- Describe how the human ear receives sound, in simple school-level steps.
- Distinguish music from noise and list harms and controls for noise pollution.
- Explain echo and reverberation, and name uses of ultrasound such as SONAR and medical scans.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Sound needs vibration
- **Visual:** A tuning fork struck beside a hanging ping-pong ball; a guitar string; a drum skin with rice grains dancing. Slow-motion shows to-and-fro motion.
- **TTS:**
  - "Sound is produced when objects vibrate."
  - "Vibration means a rapid to-and-fro motion."
  - "If vibrations stop, the sound stops."
- **Interaction:** Tap each object to start or stop vibration and watch the linked sound wave appear or fade.

### Scene 2: Sound needs a medium
- **Visual:** A bell jar with a ringing electric bell. Air is pumped out and the sound fades, though the hammer still moves.
- **TTS:**
  - "Sound needs a material medium — solid, liquid, or gas."
  - "Sound cannot travel through vacuum."
  - "In space, astronauts use radios because there is almost no air to carry sound."
- **Interaction:** Pump air out and in while watching a loudness meter fall and rise.

### Scene 3: Loudness, pitch, and the wave picture
- **Visual:** Two wave graphs. Tall waves mean large amplitude and loud sound. Waves packed closer mean higher frequency and higher pitch. A slider changes each property.
- **TTS:**
  - "Amplitude is the maximum distance a vibrating particle moves from its rest position."
  - "Larger amplitude means louder sound."
  - "Frequency is the number of vibrations in one second. Its unit is hertz (Hz)."
  - "Higher frequency means higher pitch."
- **Interaction:** Match four wave sketches to soft/low, soft/high, loud/low, and loud/high.

### Scene 4: Audible range, ultra and infra, and the ear
- **Visual:** A frequency number line from 1 Hz to 100,000 Hz with bands for infrasound, audible sound, and ultrasound. Beside it, a simple ear diagram: pinna, ear canal, eardrum, middle-ear bones, inner ear.
- **TTS:**
  - "Humans usually hear from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz."
  - "Sounds below 20 Hz are infrasound. Sounds above 20,000 Hz are ultrasound."
  - "Dogs and bats can hear some ultrasound that we cannot."
  - "The pinna collects sound. The eardrum vibrates. Tiny bones pass the vibration inward so we hear."
- **Interaction:** Place animal and device cards on the correct band, then tap ear parts in order from outside to inside.

### Scene 5: Echo, noise, and useful ultrasound
- **Visual:** A shout toward a cliff returns as an echo. A noisy street contrasts with a quiet classroom. A ship uses SONAR; a clinic shows an ultrasound scan icon.
- **TTS:**
  - "An echo is a reflected sound that returns after a short delay."
  - "For a clear echo, the reflecting surface must be far enough for the delay to be noticed."
  - "Unpleasant or unwanted sound is noise. Loud noise can harm hearing and concentration."
  - "Ultrasound helps in SONAR to find depth or objects under water, and in medical imaging."
- **Interaction:** Solve a simple echo time puzzle, then sort actions into Reduce noise or Makes more noise. Recap: vibration, medium, amplitude, frequency, ear, echo, and ultrasound uses.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** Sound is produced by —
**Options:**
A) Objects at complete rest only
B) Vibrating objects
C) Only hot objects
D) Only magnetic objects
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Vibrating objects disturb the surrounding medium and produce sound.

### Q2
**Stem:** Vibration means —
**Options:**
A) A slow one-way motion only
B) A rapid to-and-fro motion about a mean position
C) A chemical change only
D) A change of colour
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In vibration, a particle or object moves back and forth repeatedly.

### Q3
**Stem:** Sound cannot travel through —
**Options:**
A) Air
B) Water
C) Iron
D) Vacuum
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Sound needs a material medium. There are almost no particles in a vacuum to carry the vibration.

### Q4
**Stem:** In which medium does sound generally travel fastest among these?
**Options:**
A) Air
B) Water
C) Steel
D) Vacuum
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sound is usually fastest in solids, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases. It does not travel in vacuum.

### Q5
**Stem:** The maximum displacement of a vibrating particle from its rest position is called —
**Options:**
A) Frequency
B) Amplitude
C) Pitch
D) Wavelength only as loudness
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Amplitude measures how far the particle moves from its mean position.

### Q6
**Stem:** Loudness of sound mainly depends on —
**Options:**
A) Amplitude
B) Colour of the object
C) Only the listener's height
D) Magnetic field only
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Greater amplitude means more energy in the wave and a louder sound.

### Q7
**Stem:** The number of oscillations per second is called —
**Options:**
A) Amplitude
B) Frequency
C) Loudness
D) Echo time
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Frequency counts how many vibrations happen each second.

### Q8
**Stem:** The SI unit of frequency is —
**Options:**
A) metre
B) hertz (Hz)
C) joule
D) pascal
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** One hertz means one vibration per second.

### Q9
**Stem:** Pitch of a sound depends mainly on its —
**Options:**
A) Frequency
B) Only on amplitude
C) Only on the colour of the source
D) Weight of the listener
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Higher frequency sounds are heard as higher pitch; lower frequency as lower pitch.

### Q10
**Stem:** The approximate audible frequency range for a healthy young human is —
**Options:**
A) 1 Hz to 10 Hz
B) 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz
C) 25,000 Hz to 100,000 Hz only
D) 0 Hz to 5 Hz only
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Most humans hear roughly from 20 Hz up to about 20 kHz.

### Q11
**Stem:** Sounds of frequency less than 20 Hz are called —
**Options:**
A) Ultrasound
B) Infrasound
C) Audible music only
D) Light waves
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Infrasound is below the lower limit of human hearing.

### Q12
**Stem:** Sounds of frequency greater than 20,000 Hz are called —
**Options:**
A) Infrasound
B) Ultrasound
C) Thunder only
D) Visible light
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Ultrasound is above the upper limit of normal human hearing.

### Q13
**Stem:** Which animal is well known for using ultrasound for navigation?
**Options:**
A) Earthworm
B) Bat
C) Snail
D) Goldfish only in silence
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Bats emit ultrasound and use returning echoes to find their way and hunt.

### Q14
**Stem:** The thin membrane in the human ear that vibrates first when sound arrives is the —
**Options:**
A) Pinna
B) Eardrum (tympanic membrane)
C) Cochlea fluid only
D) Outer skin of the cheek
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Sound makes the eardrum vibrate; that vibration is then passed inward.

### Q15
**Stem:** The outer part of the ear that collects sound is the —
**Options:**
A) Eardrum
B) Pinna
C) Stirrup bone only
D) Auditory nerve only
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The pinna funnels sound into the ear canal toward the eardrum.

### Q16
**Stem:** An echo is —
**Options:**
A) Sound that is absorbed completely
B) Reflected sound heard after a noticeable delay
C) Light bouncing from a mirror
D) A smell travelling in air
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** When sound reflects from a distant surface and returns, we may hear it as an echo.

### Q17
**Stem:** For a distinct echo to be heard, the reflecting surface should generally be —
**Options:**
A) Very close, less than a few centimetres
B) Far enough for a clear time gap (about 17 m or more in air at room conditions in school problems)
C) Inside the ear only
D) Made of vacuum
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** School science uses a minimum distance so the reflected sound arrives after the original has ended.

### Q18
**Stem:** Unwanted or unpleasant sound is called —
**Options:**
A) Music
B) Noise
C) Ultrasound always
D) Silence
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Noise is sound that is unwanted, harsh, or disturbing.

### Q19
**Stem:** Prolonged exposure to very loud noise can —
**Options:**
A) Improve hearing forever
B) Damage hearing and cause stress
C) Turn sound into light
D) Stop all vibrations in nature
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Loud noise can harm the ear and affect health and concentration.

### Q20
**Stem:** SONAR is used mainly to —
**Options:**
A) Cook food with sound
B) Detect objects and find distances under water using ultrasound
C) Grow plants faster
D) Measure only room temperature
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** SONAR sends ultrasound pulses and times the echoes to locate underwater objects or depth.

### Q21
**Stem:** Which is a practical way to reduce noise near a busy road?
**Options:**
A) Planting trees or using barriers along the road
B) Removing all silencers from vehicles
C) Playing louder horns
D) Breaking more glass
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Trees, walls, and quieter machines help absorb or block noise.

### Q22
**Stem:** Music is generally described as sound that is —
**Options:**
A) Always harmful
B) Pleasant and has a regular pattern
C) Below 1 Hz only
D) Unable to travel in air
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Musical sounds are organised and usually pleasant, unlike irregular noise.

### Q23
**Stem:** If the amplitude of a sound wave is halved, the sound becomes —
**Options:**
A) Louder
B) Softer
C) Higher in pitch only for that reason
D) Unable to travel
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Smaller amplitude means less loudness, so the sound is softer.

### Q24
**Stem:** A shrill whistle has a higher pitch than a drum beat mainly because the whistle has —
**Options:**
A) Lower frequency
B) Higher frequency
C) Zero amplitude
D) No vibrations
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pitch rises with frequency. Shrill sounds have higher frequencies.
## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** A stretched rubber band produces sound when plucked because it —
**Options:**
A) Becomes magnetic
B) Vibrates
C) Turns into a liquid
D) Stops all motion
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Plucking makes the rubber band vibrate, and those vibrations create sound.

### Q2
**Stem:** You can hear a friend swimming and calling underwater (nearby) because sound —
**Options:**
A) Needs vacuum
B) Can travel through liquids
C) Travels only in solids
D) Is only light
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Water is a material medium, so sound waves can pass through it.

### Q3
**Stem:** Astronauts on the Moon cannot talk to each other by ordinary air sound because —
**Options:**
A) The Moon is too bright
B) There is no air (almost vacuum) to carry sound
C) Sound is faster than light there
D) Ears do not work in low gravity alone
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Without a material medium, ordinary sound cannot travel between them.

### Q4
**Stem:** Two sounds have the same frequency but different amplitudes. They differ mainly in —
**Options:**
A) Pitch
B) Loudness
C) Colour
D) Chemical formula
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Same frequency means similar pitch; different amplitude means different loudness.

### Q5
**Stem:** A sound of 30,000 Hz is —
**Options:**
A) Audible to all humans
B) Ultrasound for humans
C) Infrasound
D) Visible light
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** 30 kHz is above 20 kHz, so it is ultrasound for a typical human ear.

### Q6
**Stem:** A sound of 10 Hz is —
**Options:**
A) Ultrasound
B) Infrasound
C) Always musical
D) A radio wave only
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** 10 Hz is below 20 Hz, so it is infrasound.

### Q7
**Stem:** Dogs can often hear a whistle that humans cannot because —
**Options:**
A) Dogs see better colours
B) Their audible range can include higher frequencies than ours
C) Whistles produce only light
D) Dogs do not use ears
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Many dogs hear higher frequencies, including some ultrasound.

### Q8
**Stem:** The tiny bones of the middle ear help to —
**Options:**
A) Produce saliva
B) Transmit eardrum vibrations toward the inner ear
C) Pump blood
D) Focus light on the retina
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The hammer, anvil, and stirrup pass vibrations from the eardrum inward.

### Q9
**Stem:** Reverberation in a hall is —
**Options:**
A) Complete silence
B) Persistence of sound due to multiple reflections
C) Sound travelling in vacuum
D) A type of smell
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Repeated reflections make sound linger; soft materials are used to control it.

### Q10
**Stem:** To hear an echo from a wall, you need —
**Options:**
A) No reflecting surface
B) A reflecting surface and enough distance for a delay
C) Only ultraviolet light
D) A vacuum between you and the wall
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Sound must bounce back and arrive after a noticeable time gap.

### Q11
**Stem:** Ultrasound scans in hospitals are useful because ultrasound can —
**Options:**
A) Cook tissue with visible light only
B) Help form images of internal organs without using ordinary sound we hear
C) Replace all X-rays for broken bones always
D) Remove gravity
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** High-frequency ultrasound echoes from tissues are processed into images.

### Q12
**Stem:** Which action increases noise pollution?
**Options:**
A) Using silencers on vehicles
B) Unnecessary honking and very loud loudspeakers
C) Planting trees near roads
D) Speaking softly in libraries
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Extra loud, unwanted sound from horns and speakers adds to noise pollution.

### Q13
**Stem:** The speed of sound is greatest in which of these at usual school conditions?
**Options:**
A) Air at room temperature
B) Water
C) A metal rod
D) Outer space vacuum
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Particles in solids are closer, so sound generally travels fastest in solids.

### Q14
**Stem:** If frequency doubles and amplitude stays the same, the sound becomes —
**Options:**
A) Higher in pitch but similarly loud (amplitude unchanged)
B) Lower in pitch and much louder
C) Silent
D) Only brighter light
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pitch follows frequency; loudness mainly follows amplitude.

### Q15
**Stem:** Soft curtains and carpets in a room help reduce echoes because they —
**Options:**
A) Reflect all sound perfectly
B) Absorb a good part of the sound energy
C) Create vacuum
D) Increase amplitude of every wave
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Soft, porous materials absorb sound and cut unwanted reflections.

### Q16
**Stem:** The unit hertz means —
**Options:**
A) One joule per kilogram
B) One vibration per second
C) One metre per second squared
D) One newton per metre
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Frequency in hertz counts cycles (vibrations) each second.

### Q17
**Stem:** Which part of the ear is damaged by very loud sounds over time?
**Options:**
A) Sensitive structures inside the ear involved in hearing
B) Only the fingernails
C) Only the teeth enamel
D) Only the hair on the head
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Loud noise can harm the eardrum or delicate inner-ear cells and nerves.

### Q18
**Stem:** A ship measures ocean depth with SONAR by —
**Options:**
A) Sending ultrasound and timing the echo from the seabed
B) Using only a thermometer
C) Burning fuel underwater
D) Measuring salt by taste
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Distance relates to echo time and the known speed of sound in water.

### Q19
**Stem:** Which statement is true?
**Options:**
A) Sound is a form of energy produced by vibrations
B) Sound is a type of smell
C) Sound needs no energy to be produced
D) Sound always travels fastest in vacuum
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Vibrating sources transfer energy through the medium as sound.

### Q20
**Stem:** A low-pitched drum sound compared with a high-pitched flute note has —
**Options:**
A) Higher frequency
B) Lower frequency
C) No amplitude
D) Only ultrasonic waves
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Low pitch means lower frequency.

**Options:**
A) Recordings remove all amplitude
B) Bone conduction and air paths differ when we speak versus when we only listen

### Q22

**Options:**
A) Only changing hair colour

C) Making plants produce only ultrasound

**Answer:** B

**Options:**
A) Vacuum between bell and ear
B) Air (or another material medium) between bell and ear

**Answer:** B

### Q24
**Stem:** Bats and dolphins both benefit from ultrasound mainly for —
**Options:**
A) Photosynthesis
B) Echolocation — sensing surroundings with echoes
C) Producing magnetic fields
D) Changing seasons
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** They send high-frequency sounds and interpret returning echoes.
