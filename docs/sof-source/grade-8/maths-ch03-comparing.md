## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Ratios and Percentages
- **tts**:
  - A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind, written in the same units.
  - Percent means per hundred, so seventy-five percent means seventy-five out of every hundred.
- **on_screen**: A 10 × 10 grid; child shades squares to show 3/4 as 75%. Then a unit-converter card turns 40 cm : 2 m into 40 : 200 = 1 : 5.
- **check**: Write 3/4 as a percentage. — Answer: 75%

### step_2: Percentage Increase and Decrease
- **tts**:
  - Percentage change is always worked out on the original amount, not the new one.
  - Change divided by original, times one hundred, gives the percent change.
- **on_screen**: A price tag changes from ₹50 to ₹60; the child drags "change = ₹10" over "original = ₹50" to build the formula, and a warning appears if ₹60 is used.
- **check**: A price rises from ₹50 to ₹60. What is the percentage increase? — Answer: 20%

### step_3: Finding the Original Amount
- **tts**:
  - If a salary goes up by twenty percent, the new salary is one point two times the old one.
  - To go back, divide by one point two. Do not just subtract twenty percent of the new amount!
- **on_screen**: Reverse-machine animation: ₹18,000 enters a "÷ 1.2" box and ₹15,000 comes out; a side panel shows why 18,000 − 20% of 18,000 gives the wrong answer.
- **check**: After a 20% raise a salary is ₹18,000. What was it before? — Answer: ₹15,000

### step_4: Profit and Loss
- **tts**:
  - Cost price is what the shopkeeper pays, and selling price is what the customer pays.
  - Profit percent and loss percent are always calculated on the cost price.
- **on_screen**: A stationery-shop simulation; the child sets CP and SP for items, and a meter shows profit or loss with the percent calculated on CP.
- **check**: CP is ₹1,250 and SP is ₹1,000. What is the loss percent? — Answer: 20%

### step_5: Discounts and GST
- **tts**:
  - A discount is taken off the marked price. GST is added on top of the selling price.
  - So first subtract the discount, then add the tax.
- **on_screen**: A bill builder: marked price ₹1,600 → 15% discount → ₹1,360 → add 5% GST → final bill. Each step lights up in order.
- **check**: Marked price ₹1,600 with a 15% discount. What is the selling price? — Answer: ₹1,360

### step_6: Simple Interest
- **tts**:
  - Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by one hundred.
  - Amount is the principal plus the interest.
- **on_screen**: Sliders for P, R and T update I = PRT/100 live; a "solve for" toggle lets the child find P, R or T when the others are given.
- **check**: Find the SI on ₹2,000 at 5% per year for 3 years. — Answer: ₹300

### step_7: Compound Interest
- **tts**:
  - In compound interest, each year's interest is added to the principal, so next year you earn interest on interest too.
  - Amount equals P times one plus R by one hundred, raised to the number of years.
- **on_screen**: Side-by-side bar charts for ₹10,000 at 10% over 2 years: SI bars grow by ₹1,000 each year, while CI bars grow by ₹1,000 and then ₹1,100.
- **check**: Find the CI on ₹10,000 at 10% per year for 2 years. — Answer: ₹2,100

### step_8: Successive Percentage Changes
- **tts**:
  - A twenty percent rise followed by a twenty percent fall does not bring you back to the start!
  - Multiply the factors: one point two times zero point eight is zero point nine six, which is a four percent drop.
- **on_screen**: A price of ₹100 goes up 20% to ₹120 and then down 20% to ₹96; the child predicts the final price before the animation plays.
- **check**: A price goes up 20% and then down 20%. What is the net change? — Answer: 4% decrease
