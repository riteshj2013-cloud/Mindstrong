### Q02
- **id**: g8-maths-ch01-ach-x-02
- **stem**: At a khadi shop in Jaipur, Mihika pays ₹363 for 2 3/4 m of cloth. At the same rate, how much would 1 1/3 m of the same cloth cost?
- **options**:
  - A) ₹484
  - B) ₹132
  - C) ₹198
  - D) ₹176
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: One metre costs 363 ÷ 2 3/4 = 363 × 4/11 = ₹132. So 1 1/3 m costs 132 × 4/3 = ₹176. ₹484 is tempting because it works out 363 × 4/3, scaling the whole bill by 1 1/3 as if ₹363 were the price of one metre, but ₹363 paid for 2 3/4 m.
- **hint**: First find what one metre of Mihika's cloth costs by dividing by the mixed number, then work out the shorter length.
- **skill**: unit_rate_with_mixed_numbers
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q04
- **id**: g8-maths-ch01-ach-x-04
- **stem**: The average of the four fractions on Aadvik's slate is −1/8. Three of them are 7/10, −11/10 and 1/5. Which fraction is missing?
- **options**:
  - A) 3/40
  - B) 3/10
  - C) −3/10
  - D) −7/10
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: An average of −1/8 for four fractions means they add up to 4 × (−1/8) = −1/2. The three known fractions add up to 7/10 − 11/10 + 2/10 = −2/10 = −1/5. So the missing fraction is −1/2 − (−1/5) = −5/10 + 2/10 = −3/10. 3/40 is tempting because it works out −1/8 − (−1/5), using the average as if it were the total, but the average must first be multiplied by 4 to give the sum of all four fractions.
- **hint**: Turn the average of Aadvik's fractions into their total first, then take away the fractions you already know.
- **skill**: missing_rational_from_a_mean
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q05
- **id**: g8-maths-ch01-ach-x-05
- **stem**: Gauransh writes a list of numbers. The first number is 27/8, and each number after that is the one before it multiplied by −2/3. What is the fifth number in his list?
- **options**:
  - A) 2/3
  - B) −1
  - C) −2/3
  - D) −4/9
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: Multiply by −2/3 again and again: 27/8, then −9/4, then 3/2, then −1, then 2/3. The fifth number is 2/3 (four multiplications by −2/3 give a positive result). Choosing −1 is tempting because it is the fourth number in the list, reached after only three multiplications; the first number is already in the list, so the fifth number needs four.
- **hint**: Gauransh's list starts at 27/8; keep multiplying by the same fraction and count carefully which place in the list you have reached.
- **skill**: repeated_multiplication_by_a_negative_rational
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
