## Interactive lesson outline
- L1: Welcome. Today we put grammar under the microscope and look at how sentences actually work, not just at the rules.
- L2: Start with time. English tenses don't only say when something happened. They show how actions relate to each other.
- L3: The perfect tenses connect two moments. "I have finished" links the past to now. "I had finished" points to a moment before another past moment.
- L4: Add "been" plus "-ing," and you get the perfect continuous. It stresses how long something was going on: "We had been waiting for an hour."
- L5: Next, voice. In active voice, the doer comes first. In passive voice, the action or the receiver takes the spotlight: "The bridge was built in 1990."
- L6: (optional) Passive voice is useful when the doer is unknown or unimportant. It's not wrong, just a different focus.
- L7: Reported speech is how we retell what someone said. Usually the tense steps back: "is" becomes "was," and "will" becomes "would."
- L8: Modals like must, should, might and could carry attitude: certainty, duty, possibility or permission.
- L9: Let's work through one together. Direct speech: Meera said, "I have lost my library card."
- L10: First, the reporting verb is in the past, so the tense steps back: "have lost" becomes "had lost."
- L11: Next, the pronoun shifts to match the speaker: "I" becomes "she," and "my" becomes "her."
- L12: Result: Meera said that she had lost her library card. Clean, accurate, and every change has a reason.
- L13: (optional) One exception to remember: universal truths keep their present tense. "The Earth is round" stays "is."
- L14: For error correction, read the sentence aloud and check the subject and verb, the tense and its time words, and the prepositions.
- L15: Now it's your turn. Before choosing an answer, name the rule it depends on. That habit turns guessing into knowing.
- L16: Great focus. Grammar is the engineering behind every clear sentence, and you're becoming a fine engineer. Let's begin.

## Passage bank (for quizzes)

### P1 — The Water Clock (narrative)
By the time the judges reached our table, Arnav and I had been rehearsing our explanation for nearly two hours. We had built a water clock from three plastic bottles, a length of rubber tubing and a ruler marked in minutes. It had taken us a month to get the drip rate right; the first version had flooded my mother's kitchen twice.

The chief judge, a retired engineer who had taught physics for thirty years, studied the clock in silence. "Who designed the float?" she asked. Arnav pointed at me. "She did. I only cut the bottles."

The judge smiled. "Then you have both learnt something important. Every machine is designed by one person and improved by another." She asked us whether the clock would work at a hill station, where water boils at a lower temperature. We hadn't considered that. "It should still work," I said slowly, "because the drip depends mainly on gravity, not on boiling."

She wrote something in her notebook. We did not win first prize, but we were invited to present the clock at the district exhibition next month.

  - A: He was unwell, so he went to school.
  - B: Because he was unwell, he went to school.
  - C: Though he was unwell, he went to school.
  - D: He was unwell unless he went to school.
- answer: C
- explanation: |
  Going to school despite being unwell is a contrast, so "though" fits. "So" and "because" wrongly suggest that illness caused the trip.

  - B: Many

  The singular verb "has" is the clue. "Each of the + plural noun" takes a singular verb; all, many and few would need "have."

### Q23
- id: g8-eng-ch02-b-23
- skill: sentence-transformation
- difficulty: medium
- stem: |
  Rewrite using the superlative degree: "No other planet in the solar system is as large as Jupiter."
- options:
  - A: Jupiter is larger planet in the solar system.
  - B: Jupiter is as large as other planets in the solar system.
  - C: Jupiter is the larger planet in the solar system.
  - D: Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system.
- answer: D
- explanation: |
  "No other... as large as" means Jupiter beats all the others. The superlative needs "the" + -est: the largest.
