# Chapter 2: Force and Pressure

## Meta
- Grade: 8
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: force, pressure, contact force, non-contact force, friction, gravity, magnetism, electrostatic, atmosphere, liquid pressure
- Source basis: NCERT Class 8 / typical NSO themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Define force as a push or a pull with both magnitude and direction. Find the net force when two forces act along the same line.
- List what a force can do: start or stop motion, change speed, change direction, and change shape.
- Sort forces into contact forces (muscular, friction, normal force) and non-contact forces (gravitational, magnetic, electrostatic), with everyday examples.
- Define pressure as force per unit area. Use P = F / A with newtons (N), square metres (m²), and pascals (Pa).
- Explain everyday design choices using pressure: pointed nails, sharp knives, wide bag straps, broad camel feet, and caterpillar tracks.
- Explain that liquid pressure increases with depth and acts in all directions. Use atmospheric pressure to explain straws, syringes, rubber suckers, and the Magdeburg hemispheres.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: What is a force?
- **Visual:** A cartoon market. A child pushes a vegetable cart, and a shopkeeper pulls open a shutter. Arrows appear on each action: the arrow's length shows how strong the force is, and its tip shows the direction. Then a tug-of-war rope appears with two teams.
- **TTS:**
  - "A force is a push or a pull."
  - "Every force has a size, called its magnitude, and a direction."
  - "We measure force in newtons. The short form is N."
  - "When two forces push the same way, they add up."
  - "When they push in opposite directions, the net force is their difference."
  - "If the two teams pull equally hard, the net force is zero and the rope does not move."
- **Interaction:** Slider game. The child sets Team Left's pull and Team Right's pull, and the rope moves toward the larger force. A prompt asks, "What is the net force?" and the child types the answer before the rope moves.

### Scene 2: What can a force do?
- **Visual:** Four mini-clips play in a grid: a football at rest is kicked, a moving cycle is braked, a cricket ball is hit and changes direction, and a ball of clay is pressed flat.
- **TTS:**
  - "A force can make a resting object start moving."
  - "It can speed up a moving object or slow it down and stop it."
  - "It can change the direction of motion."
  - "It can even change an object's shape, like pressing clay."
  - "But a force cannot change how much matter an object has. Its mass stays the same."
- **Interaction:** Match-the-clip. The child drags the labels (start motion, change speed, change direction, change shape) onto the four clips.

### Scene 3: Contact and non-contact forces
- **Visual:** Two zones, "Touching" and "No touching needed". Animations: a hand lifting a bucket, a skidding shoe on a floor, a book resting on a table with an upward arrow from the table, a mango falling from a tree, a magnet pulling an iron pin from a distance, and a rubbed balloon lifting bits of paper.
- **TTS:**
  - "Some forces act only when objects touch. These are contact forces."
  - "Muscular force comes from our muscles. Friction acts between surfaces and opposes motion."
  - "When a book rests on a table, the table pushes up on it. This push is called the normal force."
  - "Other forces act from a distance. These are non-contact forces."
  - "Gravity pulls objects toward the Earth. A magnet pulls iron without touching it."
  - "A charged object, like a rubbed balloon, can attract or repel things. This is electrostatic force."
- **Interaction:** Sorting bin. Six force cards fall one by one, and the child flicks each into the "Contact" or "Non-contact" bin. A wrong flick bounces back with a one-line hint.

### Scene 4: Pressure — force on an area
- **Visual:** A nail is pushed into wood, point first and then head first. Next, a child carries a school bag with thin straps and then with wide padded straps, and pressure "glow" spots on the shoulders shrink. Last, a tractor with tyres sinks into mud while a tank on caterpillar tracks rolls over it. The formula card reads: Pressure = Force ÷ Area.
- **TTS:**
  - "Pressure is the force acting on each unit of area."
  - "Pressure equals force divided by area."
  - "Force is in newtons, and area is in square metres. Pressure is in pascals. One pascal is one newton on one square metre."
  - "The same force on a smaller area gives more pressure. That is why a nail has a sharp point."
  - "The same force on a larger area gives less pressure. That is why bag straps are wide and tanks have broad tracks."
- **Interaction:** Calculator challenge. A 100 N box is placed on a 2 m² mat, and the child enters the pressure (50 Pa). Then the mat shrinks to 0.5 m² and the child predicts whether pressure goes up or down before checking.

### Scene 5: Pressure in liquids and air
- **Visual:** A tall plastic bottle with three holes at different heights. Water spurts out fastest from the lowest hole. A dam diagram shows the wall thicker at the base. Then the scene switches to air: a child sips juice through a straw, a rubber sucker sticks to a tile, and two metal hemispheres with the air pumped out resist teams of horses pulling them apart.
- **TTS:**
  - "Liquids push on the walls and floor of their container."
  - "The deeper you go, the greater the liquid pressure. So water shoots out hardest from the lowest hole."
  - "That is why dam walls are made thicker at the bottom."
  - "Air also presses on everything around us. This is atmospheric pressure."
  - "When you suck on a straw, you lower the air pressure inside it. The air outside pushes the juice up."
  - "Long ago, scientists pumped the air out of two joined metal halves. Even horses could not pull them apart, because the outside air pressed them together."
- **Interaction:** Predict-and-pour. The child taps the hole they think will spray farthest, then watches the water flow. A final recap card lists four ideas: push or pull, contact or non-contact, pressure equals force over area, and pressure in liquids and air.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** In science, a force is best described as —
**Options:**
A) only a push
B) a push or a pull
C) only a pull
D) the speed of a moving object
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A force is any push or pull on an object. Speed describes motion. It is not a force.

### Q2
**Stem:** What is the SI unit of force?
**Options:**
A) Pascal
B) Joule
C) Newton
D) Kilogram
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Force is measured in newtons (N). The pascal is the unit of pressure, and the kilogram is the unit of mass.

### Q3
**Stem:** Which of these is a non-contact force?
**Options:**
A) The pull of a magnet on an iron pin held a little distance away
B) The friction between a shoe and the floor
C) The muscular force used to lift a bucket
D) The push of your hand opening a door
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A magnet can pull an iron pin without touching it, so magnetic force is a non-contact force. The other three act only when the objects touch.

### Q4
**Stem:** Which of these can a force NOT change?
**Options:**
A) The speed of a moving object
B) The direction of a moving object
C) The shape of an object
D) The mass of an object
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A force can start or stop motion and change speed, direction, or shape. It does not change how much matter, or mass, the object contains.

### Q6
**Stem:** In a tug-of-war, both teams pull the rope with exactly equal force in opposite directions. What happens to the rope?
**Options:**
A) It stays where it is.
B) It moves toward the left team.
C) It moves toward the right team.
D) It keeps speeding up.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Equal and opposite forces cancel, so the net force is zero. A rope at rest stays at rest.

### Q7
**Stem:** A ball rolling on a grassy field slowly comes to a stop. Which force mainly stops it?
**Options:**
A) Magnetic force
B) Friction
C) Electrostatic force
D) Muscular force
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Friction between the ball and the grass opposes the ball's motion and gradually stops it.

**Stem:** School bags P and Q hold the same 5 kg load. Why is bag Q more comfortable to carry?
**Options:**
A) Its straps make the bag heavier
B) Its straps increase the pressure on the shoulders

### Q9
**Stem:** Pressure is defined as —
**Options:**
A) force acting per unit area
B) force multiplied by area
C) area per unit force
D) mass per unit volume
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pressure is force divided by the area it acts on. Mass per unit volume is density, which is a different quantity.

### Q10
**Stem:** A balloon rubbed on dry hair can pick up tiny bits of paper. Which force is at work?
**Options:**
A) Magnetic force
B) Gravitational force
C) Friction
D) Electrostatic force
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Rubbing gives the balloon an electric charge. A charged object attracts light objects like paper bits through electrostatic force.

### Q11
**Stem:** As you go deeper into a swimming pool, the water pressure —
**Options:**
A) decreases
B) increases
C) stays the same
D) becomes zero
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Deeper water has more water above it pressing down, so liquid pressure increases with depth.

### Q12
**Stem:** When you sip juice through a straw, the juice rises because —
**Options:**
A) your mouth pulls the juice up directly with muscular force
B) gravity pushes the juice upward
C) sucking lowers the air pressure in the straw, and atmospheric pressure on the juice pushes it up
D) juice is lighter than air
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sucking removes some air from the straw. The greater air pressure outside, acting on the juice surface, then pushes the juice up the straw.

### Q13
**Stem:** A force of 200 N acts evenly on an area of 4 m². What is the pressure?
**Options:**
A) 800 Pa
B) 204 Pa
C) 196 Pa
D) 50 Pa
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Pressure = force ÷ area = 200 N ÷ 4 m² = 50 N/m² = 50 Pa.

### Q20
**Stem:** A rubber sucker pressed onto a smooth tile stays stuck. Why?
**Options:**
