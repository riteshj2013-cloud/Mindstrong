# Chapter 2: Force and Pressure

## Meta
- Grade: 8
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: force, pressure, contact force, non-contact force, friction, gravity, magnetism, electrostatic, atmosphere, liquid pressure
- Source basis: NCERT Class 8 / typical NSO themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Define force as a push or a pull with both magnitude and direction. Find the net force when two forces act along the same line.
- List what a force can do: start or stop motion, change speed, change direction, and change shape.
- Sort forces into contact forces (muscular, friction, normal force) and non-contact forces (gravitational, magnetic, electrostatic), with everyday examples.
- Define pressure as force per unit area. Use P = F / A with newtons (N), square metres (m²), and pascals (Pa).
- Explain everyday design choices using pressure: pointed nails, sharp knives, wide bag straps, broad camel feet, and caterpillar tracks.
- Explain that liquid pressure increases with depth and acts in all directions. Use atmospheric pressure to explain straws, syringes, rubber suckers, and the Magdeburg hemispheres.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: What is a force?
- **Visual:** A cartoon market. A child pushes a vegetable cart, and a shopkeeper pulls open a shutter. Arrows appear on each action: the arrow's length shows how strong the force is, and its tip shows the direction. Then a tug-of-war rope appears with two teams.
- **TTS:**
  - "A force is a push or a pull."
  - "Every force has a size, called its magnitude, and a direction."
  - "We measure force in newtons. The short form is N."
  - "When two forces push the same way, they add up."
  - "When they push in opposite directions, the net force is their difference."
  - "If the two teams pull equally hard, the net force is zero and the rope does not move."
- **Interaction:** Slider game. The child sets Team Left's pull and Team Right's pull, and the rope moves toward the larger force. A prompt asks, "What is the net force?" and the child types the answer before the rope moves.

### Scene 2: What can a force do?
- **Visual:** Four mini-clips play in a grid: a football at rest is kicked, a moving cycle is braked, a cricket ball is hit and changes direction, and a ball of clay is pressed flat.
- **TTS:**
  - "A force can make a resting object start moving."
  - "It can speed up a moving object or slow it down and stop it."
  - "It can change the direction of motion."
  - "It can even change an object's shape, like pressing clay."
  - "But a force cannot change how much matter an object has. Its mass stays the same."
- **Interaction:** Match-the-clip. The child drags the labels (start motion, change speed, change direction, change shape) onto the four clips.

### Scene 3: Contact and non-contact forces
- **Visual:** Two zones, "Touching" and "No touching needed". Animations: a hand lifting a bucket, a skidding shoe on a floor, a book resting on a table with an upward arrow from the table, a mango falling from a tree, a magnet pulling an iron pin from a distance, and a rubbed balloon lifting bits of paper.
- **TTS:**
  - "Some forces act only when objects touch. These are contact forces."
  - "Muscular force comes from our muscles. Friction acts between surfaces and opposes motion."
  - "When a book rests on a table, the table pushes up on it. This push is called the normal force."
  - "Other forces act from a distance. These are non-contact forces."
  - "Gravity pulls objects toward the Earth. A magnet pulls iron without touching it."
  - "A charged object, like a rubbed balloon, can attract or repel things. This is electrostatic force."
- **Interaction:** Sorting bin. Six force cards fall one by one, and the child flicks each into the "Contact" or "Non-contact" bin. A wrong flick bounces back with a one-line hint.

### Scene 4: Pressure — force on an area
- **Visual:** A nail is pushed into wood, point first and then head first. Next, a child carries a school bag with thin straps and then with wide padded straps, and pressure "glow" spots on the shoulders shrink. Last, a tractor with tyres sinks into mud while a tank on caterpillar tracks rolls over it. The formula card reads: Pressure = Force ÷ Area.
- **TTS:**
  - "Pressure is the force acting on each unit of area."
  - "Pressure equals force divided by area."
  - "Force is in newtons, and area is in square metres. Pressure is in pascals. One pascal is one newton on one square metre."
  - "The same force on a smaller area gives more pressure. That is why a nail has a sharp point."
  - "The same force on a larger area gives less pressure. That is why bag straps are wide and tanks have broad tracks."
- **Interaction:** Calculator challenge. A 100 N box is placed on a 2 m² mat, and the child enters the pressure (50 Pa). Then the mat shrinks to 0.5 m² and the child predicts whether pressure goes up or down before checking.

### Scene 5: Pressure in liquids and air
- **Visual:** A tall plastic bottle with three holes at different heights. Water spurts out fastest from the lowest hole. A dam diagram shows the wall thicker at the base. Then the scene switches to air: a child sips juice through a straw, a rubber sucker sticks to a tile, and two metal hemispheres with the air pumped out resist teams of horses pulling them apart.
- **TTS:**
  - "Liquids push on the walls and floor of their container."
  - "The deeper you go, the greater the liquid pressure. So water shoots out hardest from the lowest hole."
  - "That is why dam walls are made thicker at the bottom."
  - "Air also presses on everything around us. This is atmospheric pressure."
  - "When you suck on a straw, you lower the air pressure inside it. The air outside pushes the juice up."
  - "Long ago, scientists pumped the air out of two joined metal halves. Even horses could not pull them apart, because the outside air pressed them together."
- **Interaction:** Predict-and-pour. The child taps the hole they think will spray farthest, then watches the water flow. A final recap card lists four ideas: push or pull, contact or non-contact, pressure equals force over area, and pressure in liquids and air.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** In science, a force is best described as —
**Options:**
A) only a push
B) a push or a pull
C) only a pull
D) the speed of a moving object
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A force is any push or pull on an object. Speed describes motion. It is not a force.

### Q2
**Stem:** What is the SI unit of force?
**Options:**
A) Pascal
B) Joule
C) Newton
D) Kilogram
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Force is measured in newtons (N). The pascal is the unit of pressure, and the kilogram is the unit of mass.

### Q3
**Stem:** Which of these is a non-contact force?
**Options:**
A) The pull of a magnet on an iron pin held a little distance away
B) The friction between a shoe and the floor
C) The muscular force used to lift a bucket
D) The push of your hand opening a door
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A magnet can pull an iron pin without touching it, so magnetic force is a non-contact force. The other three act only when the objects touch.

### Q4
**Stem:** Which of these can a force NOT change?
**Options:**
A) The speed of a moving object
B) The direction of a moving object
C) The shape of an object
D) The mass of an object
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A force can start or stop motion and change speed, direction, or shape. It does not change how much matter, or mass, the object contains.

### Q5
**Stem:** Riya pushes a box to the right with a force of 30 N. Aman pushes the same box to the right with 20 N. What is the net force on the box?
**Options:**
A) 10 N to the right
B) 20 N to the right
C) 50 N to the right
D) 600 N to the right
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Forces acting in the same direction add up: 30 N + 20 N = 50 N, to the right.

### Q6
**Stem:** In a tug-of-war, both teams pull the rope with exactly equal force in opposite directions. What happens to the rope?
**Options:**
A) It stays where it is.
B) It moves toward the left team.
C) It moves toward the right team.
D) It keeps speeding up.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Equal and opposite forces cancel, so the net force is zero. A rope at rest stays at rest.

### Q7
**Stem:** A ball rolling on a grassy field slowly comes to a stop. Which force mainly stops it?
**Options:**
A) Magnetic force
B) Friction
C) Electrostatic force
D) Muscular force
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Friction between the ball and the grass opposes the ball's motion and gradually stops it.

### Q8
**Stem:** Why do good school bags have wide shoulder straps?
**Options:**
A) To make the bag heavier
B) To increase the pressure on the shoulders
C) To make the bag look attractive
D) To spread the weight over a larger area and reduce pressure
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Pressure = force ÷ area. A wider strap gives a larger area, so the same weight produces less pressure and the bag feels more comfortable.

### Q9
**Stem:** Pressure is defined as —
**Options:**
A) force acting per unit area
B) force multiplied by area
C) area per unit force
D) mass per unit volume
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pressure is force divided by the area it acts on. Mass per unit volume is density, which is a different quantity.

### Q10
**Stem:** A balloon rubbed on dry hair can pick up tiny bits of paper. Which force is at work?
**Options:**
A) Magnetic force
B) Gravitational force
C) Friction
D) Electrostatic force
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Rubbing gives the balloon an electric charge. A charged object attracts light objects like paper bits through electrostatic force.

### Q11
**Stem:** As you go deeper into a swimming pool, the water pressure —
**Options:**
A) decreases
B) increases
C) stays the same
D) becomes zero
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Deeper water has more water above it pressing down, so liquid pressure increases with depth.

### Q12
**Stem:** When you sip juice through a straw, the juice rises because —
**Options:**
A) your mouth pulls the juice up directly with muscular force
B) gravity pushes the juice upward
C) sucking lowers the air pressure in the straw, and atmospheric pressure on the juice pushes it up
D) juice is lighter than air
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sucking removes some air from the straw. The greater air pressure outside, acting on the juice surface, then pushes the juice up the straw.

### Q13
**Stem:** A force of 200 N acts evenly on an area of 4 m². What is the pressure?
**Options:**
A) 800 Pa
B) 204 Pa
C) 196 Pa
D) 50 Pa
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Pressure = force ÷ area = 200 N ÷ 4 m² = 50 N/m² = 50 Pa.

### Q14
**Stem:** A tall water bottle has three small holes, one near the top, one in the middle, and one near the bottom. From which hole does water come out with the greatest force?
**Options:**
A) The hole near the bottom
B) The hole in the middle
C) The hole near the top
D) All three holes equally
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The bottom hole has the most water above it. Pressure is greatest there, so water rushes out with the most force.

### Q15
**Stem:** Why are the walls of a dam made thicker at the bottom than at the top?
**Options:**
A) So that the dam looks stronger
B) Because water is colder at the bottom
C) Because water pressure is greatest at the bottom
D) To stop fish from swimming through
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Water pressure increases with depth, so the base of the dam must withstand the largest push. A thicker base makes it stronger there.

### Q16
**Stem:** An iron nail has a sharp pointed tip. The main reason is that —
**Options:**
A) the pointed end makes the nail lighter
B) the small area of the tip produces a large pressure for the same force
C) the pointed end increases friction with the hammer
D) the pointed tip attracts the wood
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** For the same hammer force, a tiny tip area gives a very high pressure, which lets the nail push into wood easily.

### Q17
**Stem:** A brick weighing 30 N measures 0.3 m × 0.1 m × 0.05 m. On which face should it rest to exert the GREATEST pressure on the ground?
**Options:**
A) The 0.1 m × 0.05 m face
B) The 0.3 m × 0.1 m face
C) The 0.3 m × 0.05 m face
D) The pressure is the same on every face.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The weight stays 30 N, so pressure is largest on the smallest area. The 0.1 m × 0.05 m face is only 0.005 m², which gives 30 ÷ 0.005 = 6000 Pa.

### Q18
**Stem:** Which pair contains ONLY contact forces?
**Options:**
A) Gravity and friction
B) Magnetic force and muscular force
C) Friction and muscular force
D) Electrostatic force and gravity
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Friction and muscular force both need the objects to touch. Gravity, magnetic force, and electrostatic force can act from a distance.

### Q19
**Stem:** Three containers have very different shapes: one wide, one narrow, and one cone-shaped. Each has the same base area and is filled with water to the same height. How does the water pressure at the bottom compare?
**Options:**
A) It is greatest in the widest container.
B) It is greatest in the narrowest container.
C) It is greatest in the container holding the most water.
D) It is the same in all three containers.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Liquid pressure depends on the depth of the liquid, not on the container's shape or the total amount of water. Equal depth means equal pressure at the bottom.

### Q20
**Stem:** A rubber sucker pressed onto a smooth tile stays stuck. Why?
**Options:**
A) The rubber has glue on it.
B) Pressing pushes air out from under it, and the atmospheric pressure outside holds it against the tile.
C) The tile pulls the sucker with magnetic force.
D) Air trapped inside the sucker pulls the tile.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Very little air is left under the cup, so the pressure there is low. The greater air pressure outside presses the sucker onto the tile.

### Q21
**Stem:** Compared with sea level, the atmospheric pressure at the top of a high mountain is —
**Options:**
A) higher
B) the same
C) lower
D) zero
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** At a height there is less air above you pressing down, so atmospheric pressure is lower. It is still not zero.

### Q22
**Stem:** Atmospheric pressure is about 100,000 Pa. What force does the air exert on a page of area 0.0225 m² (15 cm × 15 cm)?
**Options:**
A) 225 N
B) 22.5 N
C) 22,500 N
D) 2,250 N
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Force = pressure × area = 100,000 Pa × 0.0225 m² = 2,250 N. The page is not crushed because air pushes on it equally from both sides.

### Q23
**Stem:** In a famous old experiment, two metal hemispheres were joined and the air between them was pumped out. Teams of horses could not pull them apart. Why?
**Options:**
A) The atmospheric pressure outside pressed the hemispheres together because there was almost no air inside to push back.
B) The hemispheres were strong magnets.
C) The hemispheres were glued together.
D) Friction from the horses' hooves slowed them down.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** With the air removed, nothing pushed outward from inside. The large atmospheric pressure acting on the outside held the halves tightly together.

### Q24
**Stem:** A girl weighing 500 N stands on both feet, which have a total contact area of 0.05 m². She then lifts one foot and stands on the other. What pressure does she now exert on the floor?
**Options:**
A) 5,000 Pa
B) 20,000 Pa
C) 10,000 Pa
D) 25 Pa
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** One foot has half the area: 0.05 ÷ 2 = 0.025 m². Pressure = 500 N ÷ 0.025 m² = 20,000 Pa, which is double the 10,000 Pa she exerts on both feet.

## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** A footballer kicks a moving ball sideways, and it goes off in a new direction. This shows that a force can —
**Options:**
A) change the mass of the ball
B) change the colour of the ball
C) change the direction of motion
D) change only the ball's temperature
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The kick changed the direction in which the ball was moving. Changing direction is one of the main effects of a force.

### Q2
**Stem:** To describe a force completely, we must state its —
**Options:**
A) magnitude and direction
B) magnitude only
C) direction only
D) colour and size
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A force has both a size (magnitude) and a direction. A 10 N push to the left has a different effect from a 10 N push to the right.

### Q3
**Stem:** Which of these is NOT a contact force?
**Options:**
A) Friction
B) Muscular force
C) Normal force from a table
D) Gravitational force
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Gravity acts without touching. For example, it pulls a falling mango toward the Earth through the air. The other three need contact.

### Q4
**Stem:** Which instrument is used to measure force?
**Options:**
A) Thermometer
B) Spring balance
C) Barometer
D) Odometer
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A spring balance measures force in newtons. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, and a thermometer measures temperature.

### Q5
**Stem:** When you press a ball of clay between your palms, it becomes flat. This shows that force can —
**Options:**
A) change the shape of an object
B) change the mass of an object
C) have no effect on an object
D) turn a solid into a liquid
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pressing the clay changed its shape. The amount of clay, or its mass, stayed the same.

### Q6
**Stem:** One pascal is equal to —
**Options:**
A) 1 N × 1 m
B) 1 kg per m²
C) 1 N per cm²
D) 1 N per m²
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A pressure of one pascal means a force of one newton acting on one square metre.

### Q7
**Stem:** A book rests on a table. The table pushes up on the book. What is this upward push called?
**Options:**
A) Gravitational force
B) Magnetic force
C) Normal force
D) Frictional force
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The normal force is the push of a surface on an object resting on it. It acts at right angles to the surface, here straight up.

### Q8
**Stem:** Kabir pushes a cart east with 40 N. His friend pushes the same cart west with 15 N. What is the net force?
**Options:**
A) 55 N east
B) 25 N east
C) 25 N west
D) 55 N west
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Opposite forces subtract: 40 N − 15 N = 25 N. The net force points in the direction of the larger force, which is east.

### Q9
**Stem:** Camels can walk easily on soft desert sand because they have —
**Options:**
A) long necks
B) humps that store fat
C) hard, pointed hooves
D) broad, flat feet that spread their weight over a large area
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Broad feet increase the contact area, which lowers the pressure on the sand, so the camel's feet do not sink in deeply.

### Q10
**Stem:** Two plastic straws are rubbed with the same piece of paper. When one is brought near the other hanging straw, the hanging straw moves away. What does this show?
**Options:**
A) Magnetic force between the straws
B) Gravitational force between the straws
C) Electrostatic force, because similarly charged objects repel
D) Friction between the straws
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Both straws pick up the same kind of charge. Like charges repel, so the hanging straw is pushed away without being touched.

### Q11
**Stem:** Army tanks and bulldozers move on wide caterpillar tracks instead of narrow wheels. This is mainly to —
**Options:**
A) spread their weight over a larger area and reduce pressure on the ground
B) increase their top speed
C) increase their pressure on the ground for digging
D) reduce their weight
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Tracks cover a large area, so the heavy vehicle exerts less pressure and does not sink into soft or muddy ground.

### Q12
**Stem:** A ripe mango falls from a tree to the ground. Which force pulls it down?
**Options:**
A) Magnetic force
B) Gravitational force
C) Electrostatic force
D) Muscular force
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Earth's gravity pulls all objects toward it. Gravity is a non-contact force.

### Q13
**Stem:** Which knife cuts vegetables more easily, and why?
**Options:**
A) A blunt knife, because its edge has a larger area
B) A sharp knife, because its thin edge has a smaller area and produces greater pressure
C) Both cut equally well with the same force.
D) A blunt knife, because it produces more friction
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The same force on the sharp knife's very thin edge gives a much higher pressure, which cuts through the vegetable more easily.

### Q14
**Stem:** A water tank is on the roof of a four-storey building. Water flows faster from a tap on the ground floor than from a tap on the top floor. Why?
**Options:**
A) Taps on the ground floor are always wider.
B) Water on the ground floor is warmer.
C) There is less friction in pipes on the ground floor.
D) The ground-floor tap has a greater height of water above it, so the pressure is higher.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Liquid pressure increases with depth. The ground-floor tap is much farther below the water level in the tank, so the pressure there is greater.

### Q15
**Stem:** A nurse fills a syringe by dipping the needle into medicine and pulling the plunger back. The liquid enters the syringe because —
**Options:**
A) pulling the plunger lowers the pressure inside, and atmospheric pressure pushes the liquid in
B) the plunger pulls the liquid with magnetic force
C) gravity pulls the liquid upward
D) the liquid moves into empty spaces all by itself
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pulling the plunger makes a low-pressure space inside the syringe. The higher air pressure on the medicine's surface pushes the liquid up into it.

### Q16
**Stem:** Air presses on our bodies with a very large force. Why are we not crushed?
**Options:**
A) Air has no weight.
B) Atmospheric pressure acts only on buildings.
C) The pressure of fluids inside our bodies balances the air pressure outside.
D) Our skin repels air.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The fluids inside our bodies push outward with a pressure about equal to the atmospheric pressure. The inside and outside pressures balance.

### Q17
**Stem:** A sealed packet of chips bought in the plains looks puffed up when it is carried to a hill station. Why?
**Options:**
A) The chips expand in the cold.
B) More air leaks into the packet.
C) Gravity is stronger on hills.
D) The outside air pressure is lower at a height, so the air inside pushes the packet outward.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** At a height, atmospheric pressure is lower. The air sealed inside is now at a higher pressure than the air outside, so it pushes the packet outward.

### Q18
**Stem:** A magnet will attract which of these?
**Options:**
A) Aluminium foil
B) Iron pins
C) Copper wire
D) A plastic scale
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Magnets attract magnetic materials such as iron. Aluminium, copper, and plastic are not attracted.

### Q19
**Stem:** You have a bar magnet and an iron bar that may or may not be a magnet. Which observation PROVES that the iron bar is itself a magnet?
**Options:**
A) One end of the iron bar is attracted to the magnet's north pole.
B) The iron bar sticks to the side of the magnet.
C) One end of the iron bar is pushed away (repelled) by the magnet's north pole.
D) The iron bar feels heavy.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A magnet also attracts plain, unmagnetised iron, so attraction proves nothing. Only another magnet's like pole can repel it, so repulsion is the sure test.

### Q20
**Stem:** A woman weighing 600 N stands on stiletto heels with a total contact area of 0.0002 m². An elephant weighing 40,000 N stands on four feet with a total area of 0.4 m². Who exerts more pressure on the floor?
**Options:**
A) The woman, about 30 times more than the elephant
B) The elephant, because it is much heavier
C) Both exert equal pressure.
D) The elephant, about 30 times more than the woman
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The woman exerts 600 ÷ 0.0002 = 3,000,000 Pa. The elephant exerts 40,000 ÷ 0.4 = 100,000 Pa. Her tiny heel area makes her pressure about 30 times greater.

### Q21
**Stem:** At a given depth inside a liquid, the liquid pressure acts —
**Options:**
A) only downward
B) only sideways
C) only upward
D) equally in all directions
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** At any one depth, a liquid pushes equally in every direction: down, up, and on the side walls of its container.

### Q22
**Stem:** In the sea, water pressure rises by about 1 atmosphere for every 10 m of depth. The air at the surface already presses with about 1 atmosphere. What is the total pressure on a diver 30 m deep?
**Options:**
A) About 4 atmospheres
B) About 3 atmospheres
C) About 30 atmospheres
D) About 1 atmosphere
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** 30 m of water adds about 3 atmospheres. Adding the 1 atmosphere of air above the water gives a total of about 4 atmospheres.

### Q23
**Stem:** A person has fallen through thin ice on a pond. Rescuers are told to crawl or lie flat on the ice instead of walking. Why?
**Options:**
A) To keep themselves warm
B) To increase friction with the ice
C) To spread their weight over a larger area so the pressure is less and the ice does not crack
D) To move faster across the ice
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Lying flat greatly increases the contact area. For the same weight, the pressure on the ice drops, so the ice is less likely to break.

### Q24
**Stem:** Water is poured into a U-shaped glass tube with one wide arm and one narrow arm. When the water settles, how do the water levels in the two arms compare?
**Options:**
A) Higher in the wide arm
B) The same level in both arms
C) Higher in the narrow arm
D) Higher in whichever arm the water was poured into
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Liquid pressure depends only on depth, not on the width of the tube. The water settles when the pressure at the bottom is equal from both sides, which happens when both levels are the same height.
