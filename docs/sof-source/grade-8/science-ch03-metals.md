# Chapter 3: Metals and Non-Metals

## Meta
- Grade: 8
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: metals, non-metals, physical properties, chemical properties, reactivity, rusting, displacement, uses
- Source basis: NCERT Class 8 / typical NSO themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Compare metals and non-metals by their physical properties: lustre, hardness, malleability, ductility, sonority, and conduction of heat and electricity.
- Recognise important exceptions, such as soft sodium, liquid mercury, liquid bromine, lustrous iodine, and the two forms of carbon (diamond and graphite).
- Describe how metals and non-metals react with oxygen, water, acids, and bases, and classify metal oxides as basic and non-metal oxides as acidic.
- Use a simple reactivity series to predict displacement reactions, such as iron or zinc placed in copper sulphate solution.
- Explain the conditions needed for rusting of iron and the common methods used to prevent it.
- Link the properties of metals and non-metals to their uses in daily life.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Feel the difference — physical properties
- **Visual:** A lab bench with samples: an iron nail, copper wire, aluminium foil, a lump of sulphur, a piece of coal, and a pencil lead. A virtual hammer, a wire-drawing machine, a bell striker, and a battery-bulb circuit sit beside them.
- **TTS:**
  - "Metals are usually shiny. This shine is called lustre."
  - "Metals can be beaten into thin sheets. This is malleability."
  - "Metals can be drawn into thin wires. This is ductility."
  - "Metals make a ringing sound when struck. This is sonority."
  - "Metals are good conductors of heat and electricity."
  - "Non-metals like sulphur and coal are dull and brittle. They break into pieces when hammered."
- **Interaction:** Test each sample with each tool. The hammer flattens the metals and shatters sulphur. The circuit bulb glows for the metals. Results fill a two-column table: Metal or Non-metal.

### Scene 2: The rule breakers — exceptions
- **Visual:** A "Surprise Shelf" with cards: sodium being cut by a knife, a bead of mercury rolling in a dish, a bottle of reddish-brown bromine, shiny purple-black iodine crystals, a sparkling diamond, and a grey graphite pencil lead lighting a bulb.
- **TTS:**
  - "Not every element follows the rules."
  - "Sodium and potassium are metals, but they are so soft you can cut them with a knife."
  - "Mercury is a metal, yet it is a liquid at room temperature."
  - "Bromine is a non-metal that is also a liquid."
  - "Iodine is a non-metal, but it has lustre."
  - "Diamond and graphite are both forms of carbon, a non-metal."
  - "Diamond is extremely hard and does not conduct electricity. Graphite is soft and slippery, and it does conduct electricity."
- **Interaction:** Odd-one-out cards. The learner taps the property that breaks the usual rule for each element. Feedback names the exception.

### Scene 3: Reactions with oxygen, water, acids, and bases
- **Visual:** Four stations. Station 1: magnesium ribbon burns with a dazzling white flame; its ash is dissolved in water and tested with litmus. Station 2: sulphur burns in a jar; the gas is dissolved in water and tested with litmus. Station 3: a small piece of sodium fizzes and darts on water. Station 4: zinc granules bubble in dilute hydrochloric acid, and a burning splint near the gas gives a pop.
- **TTS:**
  - "Metals burn in oxygen to form metal oxides. These oxides are basic. Their solutions turn red litmus blue."
  - "Non-metals like sulphur form oxides that are acidic. Their solutions turn blue litmus red."
  - "Sodium reacts so strongly with water that it is stored in kerosene."
  - "White phosphorus catches fire in air, so it is stored in water."
  - "Many metals react with dilute acids to give hydrogen gas. Hydrogen burns with a pop sound."
  - "Non-metals generally do not react with dilute acids."
  - "Some metals, like aluminium, also react with sodium hydroxide to give hydrogen."
- **Interaction:** Predict-then-test. Before each reaction, the learner chooses the litmus colour or the gas formed. A wrong guess replays the reaction slowly with a hint.

### Scene 4: Who is stronger? — Reactivity and displacement
- **Visual:** A ladder labelled with metals from top to bottom: potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, zinc, iron, lead, (hydrogen), copper, mercury, silver, gold. Beside it, three beakers: an iron nail in blue copper sulphate, a zinc strip in copper sulphate, and a copper strip in green iron sulphate.
- **TTS:**
  - "Metals higher on the reactivity ladder are more reactive."
  - "A more reactive metal can push a less reactive metal out of its salt solution. This is a displacement reaction."
  - "Iron pushes copper out of copper sulphate. The blue solution turns light green, and a reddish-brown coating forms on the nail."
  - "Zinc does the same. The blue solution becomes colourless."
  - "Copper is less reactive than iron, so nothing happens when copper is placed in iron sulphate."
- **Interaction:** Drag a metal strip into a salt solution. The learner first predicts "Reaction" or "No reaction" using the ladder. The beaker then shows the colour change or stays the same.

### Scene 5: Rusting and everyday uses
- **Visual:** Three test tubes with iron nails: Tube A in dry air with calcium chloride, Tube B in boiled water with an oil layer on top, Tube C in ordinary tap water open to air. After a week, only Tube C shows rust. Then a city street appears with a painted gate, a galvanised bucket, copper wires, aluminium foil, a gold ring, a chlorine tablet in a water tank, and a fertiliser bag.
- **TTS:**
  - "Iron rusts only when both oxygen and water are present."
  - "Rust is a reddish-brown flaky substance, mainly hydrated iron oxide."
  - "Salty, moist air near the sea makes iron rust faster."
  - "We prevent rusting by painting, oiling, greasing, or galvanising. Galvanising means coating iron with zinc."
  - "Copper and aluminium make electric wires. Aluminium foil wraps food. Gold and silver make jewellery."
  - "Non-metals are useful too. We breathe oxygen. Nitrogen and phosphorus are used in fertilisers. Chlorine purifies water. Iodine solution is an antiseptic."
- **Interaction:** First, the learner taps the tube that will rust and explains why using a choice card. Then a matching game links each object on the street to the property or element behind it.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** The property of metals that allows them to be beaten into thin sheets is called —
**Options:**
A) Ductility
B) Malleability
C) Sonority
D) Lustre
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Malleability is the ability to be hammered into thin sheets. Ductility is about being drawn into wires.

### Q2
**Stem:** Which metal is a liquid at room temperature?
**Options:**
A) Sodium
B) Aluminium
C) Bromine
D) Mercury
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Mercury is the metal that is liquid at room temperature. Bromine is also a liquid, but it is a non-metal.

### Q3
**Stem:** Which of these non-metallic substances is a good conductor of electricity?
**Options:**
A) Graphite
B) Sulphur
C) Diamond
D) Iodine
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Graphite is a form of carbon that conducts electricity, which is why it is used in electrodes. Diamond, also carbon, does not conduct.

### Q4
**Stem:** A temple bell gives a ringing sound when struck. This property of metals is called —
**Options:**
A) Malleability
B) Ductility
C) Sonority
D) Brittleness
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sonority is the property of producing a ringing sound when struck. Most metals are sonorous.

### Q5
**Stem:** Which metal is kept under kerosene in the laboratory?
**Options:**
A) Copper
B) Iron
C) Sodium
D) Zinc
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sodium reacts strongly with oxygen and with moisture in the air. Kerosene keeps air and water away from it.

### Q6
**Stem:** Electric wires are commonly made of copper because copper is —
**Options:**
A) A good conductor of electricity and ductile
B) Brittle and lustrous
C) A poor conductor of heat
D) Soft enough to cut with a knife
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Copper conducts electricity very well and can be drawn into thin wires. Both properties make it ideal for wiring.

### Q7
**Stem:** Magnesium ribbon is burnt and the white ash is dissolved in water. The solution will —
**Options:**
A) Turn blue litmus red
B) Have no effect on litmus
C) Turn red litmus colourless
D) Turn red litmus blue
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The ash is magnesium oxide, a basic oxide. In water it forms magnesium hydroxide, which turns red litmus blue.

### Q8
**Stem:** For iron to rust, which conditions must both be present?
**Options:**
A) Oxygen only
B) Oxygen and water
C) Water only
D) Carbon dioxide and nitrogen
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Rusting needs both oxygen (air) and water (moisture). If either one is missing, iron does not rust.

### Q9
**Stem:** Galvanisation is a method of protecting iron by coating it with a layer of —
**Options:**
A) Zinc
B) Copper
C) Paint
D) Grease
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Galvanising means coating iron or steel with zinc. Painting and greasing also prevent rust, but they are not galvanisation.

### Q10
**Stem:** Zinc granules are added to dilute hydrochloric acid. The gas given off is —
**Options:**
A) Oxygen
B) Carbon dioxide
C) Hydrogen
D) Chlorine
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to form zinc chloride and hydrogen gas. The hydrogen burns with a pop sound.

### Q11
**Stem:** An iron nail is left in blue copper sulphate solution for some time. What colour change is seen in the solution?
**Options:**
A) Colourless to blue
B) Blue to light green
C) Light green to blue
D) No change at all
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Iron is more reactive than copper and displaces it. Iron sulphate forms, which is light green, and copper deposits on the nail.

### Q12
**Stem:** Which non-metal is stored under water because it catches fire when exposed to air?
**Options:**
A) Sulphur
B) Carbon
C) Iodine
D) White phosphorus
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** White phosphorus is very reactive and burns in air. Storing it under water keeps air away.

### Q13
**Stem:** Sulphur is burnt and the gas formed is dissolved in water. The solution will —
**Options:**
A) Turn red litmus blue
B) Be basic in nature
C) Be neutral to litmus
D) Turn blue litmus red
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Burning sulphur forms sulphur dioxide, an acidic oxide. In water it forms sulphurous acid, which turns blue litmus red.

### Q14
**Stem:** Which of these non-metals has a shiny appearance (lustre)?
**Options:**
A) Sulphur
B) Iodine
C) Phosphorus
D) Charcoal
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Iodine is a non-metal, yet its crystals are shiny. It is an exception to the rule that non-metals are dull.

### Q15
**Stem:** Which is the hardest natural substance known?
**Options:**
A) Iron
B) Graphite
C) Diamond
D) Copper
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Diamond, a form of carbon, is the hardest natural substance. Graphite is also carbon, but it is soft.

### Q16
**Stem:** Which metal is soft enough to be cut with a knife?
**Options:**
A) Sodium
B) Iron
C) Copper
D) Aluminium
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Sodium and potassium are very soft metals. This is an exception, as most metals are hard.

### Q17
**Stem:** In which of these will NO displacement reaction take place?
**Options:**
A) Zinc strip in copper sulphate solution
B) Copper strip in iron sulphate solution
C) Iron nail in copper sulphate solution
D) Magnesium ribbon in zinc sulphate solution
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A metal displaces another only if it is more reactive. Copper is less reactive than iron, so it cannot displace iron.

### Q18
**Stem:** Aluminium foil is used to wrap food mainly because aluminium is —
**Options:**
A) Highly malleable
B) Highly sonorous
C) A poor conductor of heat
D) A liquid at room temperature
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Aluminium is very malleable, so it can be beaten into thin, flexible foil that wraps food easily.

### Q19
**Stem:** A copper vessel left in moist air slowly gets a dull green coating. This coating is —
**Options:**
A) Rust
B) Copper sulphate
C) Pure copper oxide
D) A mixture of copper hydroxide and copper carbonate
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Copper reacts with moist air and carbon dioxide to form a green mixture of copper hydroxide and copper carbonate. Rust forms only on iron.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which statement is generally true for non-metals?
**Options:**
A) They are malleable.
B) They are sonorous.
C) Solid non-metals are brittle.
D) They are good conductors of heat.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Solid non-metals such as sulphur and coal break into pieces when hammered. Malleability, sonority, and good conduction are metal properties.

### Q21
**Stem:** Metal P displaces metal Q from its salt solution. Metal Q displaces metal R from its salt solution. What is the correct order of reactivity?
**Options:**
A) R > Q > P
B) Q > P > R
C) P > Q > R
D) P > R > Q
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A metal displaces only a less reactive metal. P is more reactive than Q, and Q is more reactive than R, so P > Q > R.

### Q22
**Stem:** Aluminium foil is warmed with sodium hydroxide solution. A gas is given off that burns with a pop sound. The gas is —
**Options:**
A) Oxygen
B) Nitrogen
C) Carbon dioxide
D) Hydrogen
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Aluminium reacts with sodium hydroxide to form hydrogen gas. A pop sound with a burning splint is the test for hydrogen.

### Q23
**Stem:** Why should copper sulphate solution NOT be stored in an iron container?
**Options:**
A) Iron displaces copper from the solution, so the container gets eaten away.
B) Copper displaces iron, making the container stronger.
C) Iron is less reactive than copper.
D) Copper sulphate solution boils on contact with iron.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Iron is more reactive than copper. It slowly displaces copper and forms iron sulphate, so the container is damaged.

### Q24
**Stem:** Element X is dull and breaks easily when hammered. Its oxide dissolves in water to give a solution that turns blue litmus red. X is most likely —
**Options:**
A) Magnesium
B) Sulphur
C) Sodium
D) Copper
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Dull, brittle, and forming an acidic oxide are all signs of a non-metal. Sulphur fits. The other three are metals that form basic oxides.

## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** The property of metals that allows them to be drawn into thin wires is called —
**Options:**
A) Malleability
B) Sonority
C) Ductility
D) Lustre
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Ductility is the ability to be drawn into wires. Gold and silver are among the most ductile metals.

### Q2
**Stem:** A freshly cut surface of a metal looks shiny. This shine is called —
**Options:**
A) Lustre
B) Ductility
C) Hardness
D) Sonority
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Lustre is the shine of a metal surface. Metals often look dull over time because a coating forms on them in air.

### Q3
**Stem:** Which non-metal is a liquid at room temperature?
**Options:**
A) Mercury
B) Sulphur
C) Iodine
D) Bromine
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Bromine is a reddish-brown liquid non-metal. Mercury is also a liquid, but it is a metal.

### Q4
**Stem:** Which non-metal is a key part of many fertilisers that help plants grow?
**Options:**
A) Oxygen
B) Nitrogen
C) Chlorine
D) Iodine
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Nitrogen is needed by plants to make proteins. Many fertilisers, such as urea, supply nitrogen.

### Q5
**Stem:** Which non-metal is used to kill germs in drinking water supplies?
**Options:**
A) Chlorine
B) Sulphur
C) Carbon
D) Nitrogen
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Chlorine is added in small amounts to water to kill harmful germs. This makes the water safe to drink.

### Q6
**Stem:** Which metal was traditionally used in clinical thermometers?
**Options:**
A) Iron
B) Sodium
C) Mercury
D) Aluminium
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Mercury is a liquid metal that expands evenly when heated. This made it useful in thermometers.

### Q7
**Stem:** A solution of which non-metal is applied on cuts and wounds as an antiseptic?
**Options:**
A) Sulphur
B) Iodine
C) Phosphorus
D) Bromine
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Tincture of iodine is a solution of iodine in alcohol. It is used as an antiseptic on wounds.

### Q8
**Stem:** When metals react with oxygen, they generally form —
**Options:**
A) Acidic oxides
B) Neutral salts
C) Acids
D) Basic oxides
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Metal oxides are basic. When they dissolve in water, they form bases that turn red litmus blue.

### Q9
**Stem:** Which gas is formed when sulphur burns in air?
**Options:**
A) Hydrogen
B) Oxygen
C) Sulphur dioxide
D) Carbon dioxide
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sulphur combines with oxygen to form sulphur dioxide. It has a sharp, choking smell.

### Q10
**Stem:** Cooking pans are made of metals mainly because metals —
**Options:**
A) Are good conductors of heat
B) Are sonorous
C) Are brittle
D) Have low melting points
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Metals carry heat quickly from the flame to the food. Most metals also have high melting points.

### Q11
**Stem:** Which of these metals reacts most vigorously with cold water?
**Options:**
A) Copper
B) Iron
C) Gold
D) Sodium
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Sodium is high in the reactivity series and reacts violently with cold water. Copper and gold do not react with water.

### Q12
**Stem:** Copper does not give hydrogen gas with dilute hydrochloric acid because copper is —
**Options:**
A) More reactive than hydrogen
B) Less reactive than hydrogen
C) A non-metal
D) A liquid
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Copper lies below hydrogen in the reactivity series. It cannot displace hydrogen from dilute acids.

### Q13
**Stem:** The reddish-brown substance called rust is mainly —
**Options:**
A) Iron sulphate
B) Hydrated iron oxide
C) Iron carbonate
D) Iron chloride
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Rust forms when iron reacts with oxygen and water. It is mainly hydrated iron oxide.

### Q14
**Stem:** Which of these does NOT help prevent an iron gate from rusting?
**Options:**
A) Painting it
B) Applying grease
C) Washing it often with salt water
D) Galvanising it
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Salt water speeds up rusting. Painting, greasing, and galvanising keep air and moisture away from the iron.

### Q15
**Stem:** A zinc strip is placed in blue copper sulphate solution. What is observed after some time?
**Options:**
A) The solution becomes colourless and a reddish-brown layer forms on the zinc.
B) The solution turns green and the zinc becomes shiny.
C) The solution turns deeper blue and nothing forms on the zinc.
D) No change is seen.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Zinc is more reactive than copper and displaces it. Zinc sulphate is colourless, and reddish-brown copper deposits on the strip.

### Q16
**Stem:** Diamond and graphite are both forms of carbon. Which statement about them is correct?
**Options:**
A) Both conduct electricity well.
B) Diamond conducts electricity, but graphite does not.
C) Both are very soft.
D) Graphite conducts electricity, while diamond is very hard and does not.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Graphite is soft and conducts electricity. Diamond is the hardest natural substance and does not conduct electricity.

### Q17
**Stem:** Magnesium oxide dissolves in water to form magnesium hydroxide. This solution is —
**Options:**
A) Basic
B) Acidic
C) Neutral
D) A strong acid
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Magnesium oxide is a metal oxide. Its solution in water is basic and turns red litmus blue.

### Q18
**Stem:** Iron railings in coastal towns rust faster than those in dry inland towns. The main reason is that —
**Options:**
A) Sea air has no oxygen.
B) Salt stops water from touching the iron.
C) Sea sand coats and protects the iron.
D) Moist, salty air speeds up rusting.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Near the sea, the air has more moisture and salt. Salt speeds up the reaction of iron with oxygen and water.

### Q19
**Stem:** School bells are made of metal because metals are —
**Options:**
A) Lustrous
B) Ductile
C) Sonorous
D) Brittle
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Sonorous materials produce a ringing sound when struck. This property makes metals ideal for bells.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which of these is a non-metal?
**Options:**
A) Zinc
B) Carbon
C) Mercury
D) Aluminium
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Carbon is a non-metal. Zinc, mercury, and aluminium are metals, though mercury is a liquid.

### Q21
**Stem:** An iron nail is placed in boiled water, and a layer of oil is poured over the water. The nail does not rust. Why?
**Options:**
A) There is no water in the tube.
B) The water is too cold.
C) The oil reacts with the iron.
D) The boiled water has almost no dissolved air, and the oil stops air from entering.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Boiling removes dissolved air, and the oil layer stops fresh air from dissolving. Without oxygen, iron cannot rust.

### Q22
**Stem:** Metal X gives hydrogen with dilute hydrochloric acid and displaces copper from copper sulphate solution. It does NOT displace zinc from zinc sulphate solution. X could be —
**Options:**
A) Iron
B) Magnesium
C) Silver
D) Gold
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** X lies above hydrogen and copper but below zinc. Iron fits. Magnesium would displace zinc, and silver and gold do not react with dilute acids.

### Q23
**Stem:** Gold is widely used for making jewellery mainly because it —
**Options:**
A) Reacts quickly with air
B) Is the hardest metal
C) Is lustrous, malleable, and does not corrode easily
D) Is the best conductor of heat
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Gold is very unreactive, so it keeps its shine. It is also malleable and ductile, so it can be shaped into fine designs.

### Q24
**Stem:** Charcoal is burnt and the gas formed is bubbled through water. The solution will —
**Options:**
A) Turn red litmus blue
B) Turn blue litmus slightly red
C) Turn red litmus deep red
D) Show no effect on any indicator
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Burning carbon forms carbon dioxide, an acidic oxide. It dissolves in water to form weak carbonic acid, which turns blue litmus slightly red.
