## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: The Number Family Tree
- **tts**:
  - Natural numbers start at one, whole numbers add zero, and integers bring in the negatives.
  - A rational number is any number we can write as p by q, where p and q are integers and q is not zero.
- **on_screen**: Nested circles labelled Natural ⊂ Whole ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational. Child drags cards (7, 0, -4, 3/5, -2/9) into the smallest circle each one fits.
- **check**: Where does -4 go first: whole numbers or integers? — Answer: Integers

### step_2: Equivalent and Standard Forms
- **tts**:
  - Multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same non-zero number and the value stays the same.
  - A rational number is in standard form when the denominator is positive and the top and bottom share no common factor except one.
- **on_screen**: A "simplify machine" takes 9/-12; the child first moves the minus sign to the numerator, then divides by 3 to get -3/4.
- **check**: Write 9/-12 in standard form. — Answer: -3/4

### step_3: Rational Numbers on the Number Line
- **tts**:
  - Positive rationals sit to the right of zero, and negative rationals sit to the left.
  - To place minus five by three, split each unit into thirds and count five thirds to the left of zero.
- **on_screen**: A number line from -2 to 2 with zoom; each unit splits into thirds. Child drags a marker to -5/3.
- **check**: Between which two integers does -5/3 lie? — Answer: -2 and -1

### step_4: Comparing and Ordering
- **tts**:
  - Make the denominators the same, and then compare the numerators.
  - Be careful with negatives! Minus fourteen is greater than minus fifteen, because it is closer to zero.
- **on_screen**: Two cards, -5/6 and -7/9, are converted to -15/18 and -14/18 on a number line; child taps the greater one.
- **check**: Which is greater, -5/6 or -7/9? — Answer: -7/9

### step_5: Adding and Subtracting
- **tts**:
  - To add rational numbers, first make the denominators the same, then add the numerators.
  - Subtracting a number is the same as adding its additive inverse, so minus a negative becomes plus.
- **on_screen**: Step-by-step workspace for 2/3 − (−5/6): the "change to add the inverse" button flips it to 2/3 + 5/6, then the LCM 6 is found.
- **check**: What is 2/3 − (−5/6)? — Answer: 3/2

### step_6: Multiplying, Dividing and Reciprocals
- **tts**:
  - Multiply tops together and bottoms together, and then fix the sign.
  - To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second number. Zero is the only number with no reciprocal.
- **on_screen**: Sign-rule grid (+×+, +×−, −×−) beside a calculator; child solves (−7/12) ÷ (14/9) by flipping 14/9 to 9/14.
- **check**: What is the reciprocal of -2/7? — Answer: -7/2

### step_7: Properties of Rational Numbers
- **tts**:
  - Rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication. Order does not matter for adding or multiplying.
  - Zero is the additive identity, one is the multiplicative identity, and every number has an additive inverse.
- **on_screen**: Property-matching game: equations such as a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c slide in, and the child matches each to Closure, Commutative, Associative, Distributive, Identity or Inverse.
- **check**: Which property does 3/4 × (2/5 + 1/3) = 3/4 × 2/5 + 3/4 × 1/3 show? — Answer: Distributivity

### step_8: Finding Rationals Between Two Rationals
- **tts**:
  - Between any two different rational numbers there are endless rational numbers.
  - Rewrite both with a bigger common denominator, and you will see many numbers fit in between.
- **on_screen**: Zoomable number line between -1/3 and 1/4; child rewrites them as -4/12 and 3/12 and taps to place rationals like -1/12, 0 and 1/12.
- **check**: Name one rational number between -1/3 and 1/4. — Answer: 0 (or -1/12, 1/12, etc.)

## Practice Set A

### Q01
- **stem**: Which of these is NOT a rational number?
- **options**:
  - A) -3/7
  - B) 0
  - C) 5/0
  - D) 2
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: A rational number p/q needs q ≠ 0, so 5/0 is not defined and is not rational.
- **skill**: definition
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q02
- **stem**: What is the standard form of 9/-12?
- **options**:
  - A) -3/4
  - B) 3/-4
  - C) 3/4
  - D) -9/12
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: Standard form needs a positive denominator and no common factor, so 9/-12 becomes -9/12 and then -3/4.
- **skill**: standard_form
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q03
- **stem**: What is the additive inverse of -5/8?
- **options**:
  - A) -8/5
  - B) 8/5
  - C) -5/8
  - D) 5/8
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: The additive inverse is the number that gives 0 when added, and -5/8 + 5/8 = 0.
- **skill**: inverse
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q04
- **stem**: What is the multiplicative inverse of -2/7?

  - B) -7/2
  - C) 2/7

- **explanation**: (-2/7) × (-7/2) = 1, so the reciprocal keeps the negative sign.
- **skill**: inverse
- **difficulty**: easy

### Q05
