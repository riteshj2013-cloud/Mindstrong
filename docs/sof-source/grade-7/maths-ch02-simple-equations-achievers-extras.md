  - A) 3 kg

  - C) 6 kg
  - D) 4 kg
- **answer**: D

### Q02
- **id**: g7-maths-ch02-ach-x-02
- **stem**: Tank P on Hemang's terrace holds 500 L and is being emptied at 15 L per minute. At the same time Tank Q holds 200 L and is being filled at 10 L per minute. After how many minutes will both tanks hold the same amount?
- **options**:
  - A) 12 min
  - B) 60 min
  - C) 20 min
  - D) 30 min
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: After t minutes Tank P holds 500 − 15t litres and Tank Q holds 200 + 10t litres. Setting them equal: 500 − 15t = 200 + 10t, so 300 = 25t and t = 12 min (both hold 320 L). 60 min is tempting because it divides the 300 L gap by 15 − 10 = 5, but one tank is losing water while the other gains, so the gap closes by 15 + 10 = 25 L every minute.
- **hint**: Write an expression for the water in each of Hemang's tanks after some minutes, and set the two expressions equal.
- **skill**: emptying_and_filling_tanks_meet
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

- **alt**: A rectangle. The long top side is labelled (x + 5) cm and the short right side is labelled (x − 2) cm.
- **options**:
  - A) 10 cm
  - B) 15 cm
  - C) 8 cm
  - D) 18 cm
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: The perimeter is 2 × [(x + 5) + (x − 2)] = 2 × (2x + 3) = 4x + 6. So 4x + 6 = 46, 4x = 40 and x = 10. The longer side is x + 5 = 15 cm (the shorter is 8 cm, and 2 × (15 + 8) = 46). 10 cm is tempting because it is the value of x, but x is not a side; the longer side is x + 5.
- **hint**: Add all four sides of Nishka's frame in terms of x, set the total equal to the perimeter, then work out the longer side.
- **skill**: perimeter_equation_from_labelled_sides
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: pictorial
- **pictorial**: yes

### Q04
- **id**: g7-maths-ch02-ach-x-04
- **stem**: At a planetarium in Kolkata, an adult ticket costs ₹40 more than a child ticket. Sohan's family of 2 adults and 3 children pays ₹580 in all. What does one child ticket cost?
- **options**:
  - A) ₹108
  - B) ₹116
  - C) ₹100
  - D) ₹140
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: Let a child ticket cost ₹c, so an adult ticket costs ₹(c + 40). Then 2(c + 40) + 3c = 580, so 5c + 80 = 580, 5c = 500 and c = 100. A child ticket costs ₹100 (an adult ticket ₹140). ₹108 is tempting because it writes 5c + 40 = 580, but each of the 2 adult tickets carries the extra ₹40, so the extra is ₹80.
- **hint**: Let a child ticket cost some amount, write each adult ticket in terms of it, and remember Sohan's family has two adults.
- **skill**: two_ticket_prices_equation
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q05
- **id**: g7-maths-ch02-ach-x-05
- **stem**: An auto-rickshaw in Pune charges ₹25 for the first kilometre and ₹15 for every kilometre after that. Eshani's ride cost ₹205. How long was the ride?
- **options**:
  - A) 13 km
  - B) 12 km
  - C) 14 km
  - D) 11 km
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: If the ride was d km, the fare is 25 + 15(d − 1) = 205. So 15(d − 1) = 180, d − 1 = 12 and d = 13 km. 12 km is tempting because it is the number of kilometres charged at ₹15, but the first kilometre, charged at ₹25, has to be added back.
- **hint**: Take off the charge for Eshani's opening kilometre, then see how many more kilometres the rest of the fare pays for.
- **skill**: fare_equation_after_first_unit
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
