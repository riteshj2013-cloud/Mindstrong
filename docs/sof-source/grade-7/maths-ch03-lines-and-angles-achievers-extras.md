### Q02
- **id**: g7-maths-ch03-ach-x-02
- **stem**: Two straight railway tracks near Chaitali's town are parallel, and a straight road crosses both. On the same side of the road, the two interior angles it makes with the tracks are (3x + 20)° and (x + 40)°. What is the larger of these two angles?
- **options**:
  - A) 50°
  - B) 110°
  - C) 70°
  - D) 30°
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Interior angles on the same side of a line crossing two parallel lines are co-interior, so they add up to 180°: (3x + 20) + (x + 40) = 180, so 4x + 60 = 180 and x = 30. The angles are 110° and 70°, and the larger is 110°. 50° is tempting because it sets the two angles equal (3x + 20 = x + 40 gives x = 10), but only corresponding or alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles add up to 180°.
- **hint**: The two interior angles on one side of the road between Chaitali's parallel tracks are co-interior; decide what they add up to.
- **skill**: co_interior_angles_equation
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q03
- **id**: g7-maths-ch03-ach-x-03
- **stem**: Sarika splits a round rangoli into four parts with four lines drawn out from its centre. The four angles at the centre are x°, (x + 20)°, 2x° and (2x − 20)°. What is the largest of the four angles?
- **options**:
  - A) 60°
  - B) 100°
  - C) 80°
  - D) 120°
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: The angles at the centre make a full turn: x + (x + 20) + 2x + (2x − 20) = 360, so 6x = 360 and x = 60. The angles are 60°, 80°, 120° and 100°, and the largest is 120°. 60° is tempting because it uses 180° instead of 360° (6x = 180 gives x = 30 and a largest angle of 60°); it is also the value of x itself, but the angles round a full point add up to 360°.
- **hint**: The angles at the centre of Sarika's rangoli make a full turn; add the four expressions and solve for x.
- **skill**: angles_round_a_point_equation
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q05
- **id**: g7-maths-ch03-ach-x-05
- **stem**: Near Aniruddh's school, two parallel kerbs l and m of a road are crossed by a straight zebra-crossing line at P on l and Q on m. One interior angle at P is 64°. Rays PR and QR bisect the two interior angles on that same side of the crossing line and meet at R. What is ∠PRQ?
- **options**:
  - A) 122°
  - B) 58°
  - C) 90°
  - D) 64°
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: The interior angles at P and Q on the same side are co-interior, so the one at Q is 180° − 64° = 116°. The bisectors cut them into 32° and 58°, which are two angles of triangle PQR. So ∠PRQ = 180° − 32° − 58° = 90°. 122° is tempting because it takes away only the 58° at Q from 180°, but the triangle also has the 32° angle at P.
- **hint**: The two interior angles on one side of Aniruddh's crossing line are co-interior; halve each, then use the angle sum of triangle PQR.
- **skill**: bisectors_of_co_interior_angles
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
