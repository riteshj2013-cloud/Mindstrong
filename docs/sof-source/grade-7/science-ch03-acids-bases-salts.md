## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** Acids taste ____ (never taste unknown lab chemicals!).
**Options:**
A) Sour
B) Sweet
C) Salty always
D) Bitter always
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Edible acids like lemon taste sour; lab tasting is unsafe.

### Q2
**Stem:** Bases taste ____ and feel soapy (do not taste/touch unknowns!).
**Options:**
A) Bitter
B) Sour
C) Sweet always
D) Metallic always
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Bases are typically bitter and soapy to touch — but never test unknowns that way.

### Q3
**Stem:** Litmus in acid turns…
**Options:**
A) Red
B) Blue
C) Green
D) Black
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Blue litmus turns red in acid.

### Q4
**Stem:** Litmus in base turns…
**Options:**
A) Blue
B) Red
C) Yellow
D) Orange
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Red litmus turns blue in base.

### Q5
**Stem:** A neutral solution on litmus…
**Options:**
A) Does not change litmus colour
B) Always turns purple permanently as acid
C) Always bleaches it
D) Turns it to metal
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Neutral solutions do not affect litmus.

### Q6
**Stem:** The pH of a neutral solution is…
**Options:**
A) 7
B) 0
C) 14
D) 1
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** pH 7 is neutral at standard conditions.

### Q7
**Stem:** Acids have pH…
**Options:**
A) Less than 7
B) Exactly 7
C) Greater than 7 only
D) Exactly 14
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Acidic solutions have pH < 7.

### Q8
**Stem:** Bases have pH…
**Options:**
A) Greater than 7
B) Less than 7
C) Exactly 0
D) Exactly 7 only
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Basic/alkaline solutions have pH > 7.

### Q9
**Stem:** Which is a natural acid indicator?
**Options:**
A) Turmeric
B) Common salt
C) Sugar
D) Sand
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Turmeric is a natural indicator (yellow → reddish-brown in base).

### Q10
**Stem:** China rose indicator turns ____ in acid.
**Options:**
A) Dark pink / magenta
B) Green
C) Blue always
D) Black
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** China rose gives dark pink in acid and green in base.

### Q11
**Stem:** Hydrochloric acid’s formula is…
**Options:**
A) HCl
B) H₂SO₄
C) HNO₃
D) NaOH
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** HCl is hydrochloric acid.

### Q12
**Stem:** Sulphuric acid’s formula is…
**Options:**
A) H₂SO₄
B) HCl
C) NaCl
D) Ca(OH)₂
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** H₂SO₄ is sulphuric acid.

### Q13
**Stem:** Sodium hydroxide is a…
**Options:**
A) Base (alkali)
B) Acid
C) Salt only
D) Indicator
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** NaOH is a strong base/alkali.

### Q14
**Stem:** The reaction of acid + base produces…
**Options:**
A) Salt and water
B) Only oxygen
C) Only hydrogen always without salt
D) Only nitrogen
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water.

### Q15
**Stem:** Neutralisation is used in treating ant bites because ant venom is acidic and…
**Options:**
A) A mild base can neutralise it
B) A strong acid helps more
C) Salt removes oxygen
D) Indicators cure bites
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Mild baking soda (base) can neutralise acidic ant venom.

### Q16
**Stem:** Acids react with many metals to liberate…
**Options:**
A) Hydrogen gas
B) Nitrogen gas
C) Neon
D) Ozone
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Active metals + acid often give H₂.

### Q17
**Stem:** Carbon dioxide turns limewater…
**Options:**
A) Milky
B) Blue
C) Green
D) Black
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** CO₂ + limewater → milky calcium carbonate.

### Q18
**Stem:** Baking soda’s chemical name is…
**Options:**
A) Sodium hydrogen carbonate
B) Sodium hydroxide
C) Calcium oxide
D) Potassium nitrate
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** NaHCO₃ is sodium hydrogen carbonate (baking soda).

### Q19
**Stem:** Slaked lime is…
**Options:**
A) Calcium hydroxide
B) Sodium chloride
C) Hydrochloric acid
D) Sugar
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Ca(OH)₂ is slaked lime.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which salt is used to make food tasty (common salt)?
**Options:**
A) Sodium chloride
B) Copper sulphate
C) Calcium carbonate only as table salt
D) Potassium permanganate
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** NaCl is common salt.

### Q21
**Stem:** Acids are ____ in water (handle with care; dilution rule).
**Options:**
A) Corrosive / can be made into aqueous solutions
B) Insoluble always
C) Magnetic
D) Solid metals
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Many acids form aqueous solutions and can be corrosive.

### Q22
**Stem:** An alkali is…
**Options:**
A) A base that dissolves in water
B) Any salt
C) Any acid
D) An indicator
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Alkalis are water-soluble bases.

### Q23
**Stem:** Phenolphthalein in acid is…
**Options:**
A) Colourless
B) Pink
C) Blue
D) Green
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Phenolphthalein is colourless in acid and pink in base.

### Q24
**Stem:** Phenolphthalein in base is…
**Options:**
A) Pink
B) Colourless
C) Red litmus only
D) Milky
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** It turns pink in basic solutions.

## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** Tooth decay is promoted by acids from bacteria; toothpaste is usually…
**Options:**
A) Mildly basic to neutralise acids
B) Strongly acidic
C) Pure sulphuric acid
D) Only sugar
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Basic toothpaste helps neutralise mouth acids.

### Q2
**Stem:** Factory waste acids are often treated with…
**Options:**
A) Bases (neutralisation) before release
B) More acid
C) Only sugar
D) Only oxygen gas
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Neutralisation reduces environmental harm.

### Q3
**Stem:** Ammonia solution is…
**Options:**
A) Basic
B) Acidic
C) Neutral salt only
D) An indicator dye only
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Aqueous ammonia is a base.

### Q4
**Stem:** Which is an olfactory indicator?
**Options:**
A) Onion / vanilla (odour changes in acid/base)
B) Blue litmus paper only
C) Thermometer
D) Magnet
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Olfactory indicators change smell in acid/base.

### Q5
**Stem:** The salt formed from HCl + NaOH is…
**Options:**
A) NaCl
B) Na₂SO₄
C) CaCO₃
D) KNO₃
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O.

### Q6
**Stem:** Acids turn methyl orange…
**Options:**
A) Red / pinkish
B) Yellow
C) Green
D) Blue
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Methyl orange is red in acid and yellow in base.

### Q7
**Stem:** Bases turn methyl orange…
**Options:**
A) Yellow
B) Red
C) Black
D) Colourless always
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Methyl orange is yellow in basic medium.

### Q8
**Stem:** Curd and citrus fruits contain…
**Options:**
A) Acids (lactic / citric etc.)
B) Only strong bases
C) Only pure metals
D) Only indicators
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Food acids give sour taste.

### Q9
**Stem:** Soap solution is generally…
**Options:**
A) Basic
B) Strongly acidic like HCl
C) pH 7 exactly always
D) A salt without ions
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Soap solutions are usually alkaline.

### Q10
**Stem:** Which gas is released when an acid reacts with a metal carbonate?
**Options:**
A) Carbon dioxide
B) Hydrogen only always
C) Nitrogen
D) Neon
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂.

### Q11
**Stem:** Distilled water is…
**Options:**
A) Neutral
B) Strongly acidic
C) Strongly basic
D) A salt crystal
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pure water is neutral (pH 7).

### Q12
**Stem:** A pH of 2 indicates a…
**Options:**
A) Strongly acidic solution
B) Neutral solution
C) Strongly basic solution
D) Salt with pH 7
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Far below 7 means strongly acidic.

### Q13
**Stem:** A pH of 13 indicates a…
**Options:**
A) Strongly basic solution
B) Neutral solution
C) Strongly acidic solution
D) Pure sugar
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Far above 7 means strongly basic.

### Q14
**Stem:** Neutralisation can be represented as…
**Options:**
A) H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O
B) Na + Cl → only heat
C) CO₂ → O₂
D) N₂ + O₂ → sugar
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions form water.

### Q15
**Stem:** Plaster of Paris is related to…
**Options:**
A) Calcium sulphate chemistry (salts)
B) Sodium hydroxide acid
C) Only nitrogen gas
D) Chlorophyll
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** PoP comes from gypsum (a salt) chemistry.

### Q16
**Stem:** Bleaching powder is associated with…
**Options:**
A) Chlorine chemistry used for bleaching/disinfecting
B) Only sugar refining as its only use named here
C) Making chilli milder
D) Photosynthesis
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Bleaching powder releases chlorine for bleaching/disinfection.

### Q17
**Stem:** Which is NOT a property of acids?
**Options:**
A) They turn red litmus blue
B) They taste sour (edible ones)
C) They can react with metals
D) They have pH < 7
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Acids turn blue litmus red, not red litmus blue.

### Q18
**Stem:** Which is NOT a property of bases?
**Options:**
A) They turn blue litmus red
B) They feel soapy
C) They have pH > 7
D) They can neutralise acids
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Bases turn red litmus blue; they do not turn blue litmus red.

### Q19
**Stem:** Universal indicator shows…
**Options:**
A) Different colours across the pH range
B) Only one colour forever
C) Temperature only
D) Mass only
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Universal indicator maps pH to a colour chart.

### Q20
**Stem:** Diluting a strong acid should be done by…
**Options:**
A) Adding acid slowly to water (with care)
B) Adding water to concentrated acid quickly
C) Tasting first
D) Heating in a closed bottle
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Safety: acid into water, slowly, with stirring — never the reverse casually.

### Q21
**Stem:** Soil that is too acidic for plants may be treated with…
**Options:**
A) Mild bases like slaked lime
B) More strong acid
C) Only sugar
D) Only plastic
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Lime can neutralise excess soil acidity.

### Q22
**Stem:** Which ion do acids commonly release in water?
**Options:**
A) H⁺ (hydrogen ions)
B) OH⁻ only
C) Only Na⁺
D) Only Cl₂ gas always
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Acids furnish H⁺ in aqueous solution.

### Q23
**Stem:** Which ion do alkalis commonly release in water?
**Options:**
A) OH⁻ (hydroxide ions)
B) H⁺ only
C) Only CO₃²⁻ always
D) Only neon
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Alkalis furnish OH⁻ in water.

### Q24
**Stem:** Common salt, baking soda, and washing soda are all examples of…
**Options:**
A) Salts (useful compounds)
B) Strong acids only
C) Indicators only
D) Metals only
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** They are important salts used at home and industry.
