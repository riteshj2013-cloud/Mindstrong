## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Collecting Data and Tally Marks
- **tts**:
  - Data is information we collect, like how many of each thing we count. A tally is a quick way to count: draw one upright stick for each thing.
  - When you reach five, draw the fifth mark as a line across the four upright sticks. That bundle stands for 5, so you can count in fives.
- **on_screen**: Paper boats float past one by one, and a stick appears for each. On the fifth boat a red line slides across the four sticks to make a bundle.
- **check**: A tally has 2 bundles and 3 single sticks. What number does it show? — Answer: 13

### step_2: Tables
- **tts**:
  - A table puts data in rows and columns so it is easy to read. Read across a row to find one thing's number.
  - From a table you can find the most, the least, how many more, and the total by adding the numbers.
- **on_screen**: A small table of days and numbers appears. A finger slides along each row and the smallest number glows.
- **check**: A table shows Monday 8, Tuesday 5 and Wednesday 7. Which day has the least? — Answer: Tuesday

### step_3: Pictographs and Keys
- **tts**:
  - A pictograph uses symbols to show data. The key tells you how many each symbol stands for, such as 1 symbol = 2 or 1 symbol = 10.
  - To read a row, count the symbols and use the key. A half symbol stands for half of the key number.
- **on_screen**: A row of round symbols appears with the key '1 symbol = 2'. Each symbol splits into two dots while a counter adds up; a half symbol adds just one dot.
- **check**: The key is 1 symbol = 10. A row has 3 whole symbols and a half symbol. What does it show? — Answer: 35

### step_4: Bar Graphs and Scales
- **tts**:
  - A bar graph shows each amount as a bar. The scale beside the bars goes up in equal steps, such as 1, 2, 5 or 10.
  - Read a bar by following its top across to the scale. If the top is halfway between two lines, the amount is halfway between their numbers.
- **on_screen**: Four bars grow upward. A dotted line runs from the top of one bar across to the scale; then a bar stops between 20 and 30 and the number 25 appears.
- **check**: The lines on a scale go 0, 5, 10, 15. A bar stops at the third line above 0. What does it show? — Answer: 15

### step_5: Comparing: Most, Least and How Many More
- **tts**:
  - The longest row or tallest bar shows the most, and the shortest shows the least.
  - To find how many more, read both amounts and subtract the smaller from the bigger. To find a total, add the amounts.
- **on_screen**: Two bars stand side by side; the shorter one is copied onto the taller one and the gap at the top lights up and is counted.
- **check**: A bar for Monday shows 12 and a bar for Tuesday shows 8. How many more on Monday? — Answer: 4

### step_6: Changing One Display into Another
- **tts**:
  - The same data can be shown as a tally, a table, a pictograph or a bar graph. Each one must show the same numbers.
  - To check a display, read every row or bar and compare it with the numbers it should show.
- **on_screen**: A tally row turns into a number in a table, then into symbols in a pictograph, then into a bar, with the same number glowing each time.
- **check**: A tally shows 1 bundle and 4 single sticks. The key is 1 symbol = 3. How many symbols are needed? — Answer: 3

### step_7: Two-Step Data Puzzles
- **tts**:
  - Some questions need two steps: first read two amounts and add them, then compare with another amount.
  - If you know the total, you can find a missing amount: add the amounts you know and take that away from the total.
- **on_screen**: A bar graph shows three bars and a dashed empty space; the known bars stack up beside the total and the gap left over becomes the missing bar.
- **check**: Four days have a total of 30. Three days show 8, 7 and 9. What is the fourth day? — Answer: 6

### Q02
- **id**: g3-maths-ch09-a-02
- **stem**: Chinmay's uncle has sold 14 tender coconuts since morning. Chinmay wants to record this number with tally marks. What should he draw?
- **options**:
  - A) 1 bundle and 4 single sticks
  - B) 2 bundles and 4 single sticks
  - C) 3 bundles and 1 single stick
  - D) 2 bundles and 2 single sticks
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Each bundle shows 5, so 2 bundles show 10, and 4 more sticks make 14. 1 bundle and 4 single sticks is tempting because it copies the digits of 14, but it shows only 5 + 4 = 9.
- **hint**: Every bundle Chinmay draws stands for five coconuts; see how many full bundles fit into the coconuts sold, then add sticks.
- **skill**: make_tally_for_number
- **difficulty**: easy
