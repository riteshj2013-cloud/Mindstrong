## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Coins and Notes
- **tts**:
  - Indian money comes as coins and notes. Coins are ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10 and ₹20, and there is also a small 50-paise coin.
  - Notes are ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200 and ₹500. A bigger number on a coin or note means more money, not a bigger size.
- **on_screen**: Simple drawn coins and notes float onto a counter one by one, and each says its value aloud. Then the child sorts them into a coin tray and a note tray.
- **check**: Which is worth more: a ₹10 note or a ₹5 coin? — Answer: the ₹10 note

### step_2: Rupees and Paise
- **tts**:
  - 100 paise make 1 rupee. So two 50-paise coins make ₹1.
  - We can write ₹3 and 50 paise for three rupees and fifty paise.
- **on_screen**: Two 50-paise coins slide together and turn into a ₹1 coin. Then six 50-paise coins group into pairs and become ₹3.
- **check**: How many 50-paise coins make ₹2? — Answer: 4

### step_3: Making an Amount in Different Ways
- **tts**:
  - The same amount can be made in many ways. ₹10 can be one ₹10 note, two ₹5 coins, or five ₹2 coins.
  - To use the fewest notes and coins, start with the biggest one that fits and keep going.
- **on_screen**: A target of ₹37 appears. A ₹20 note, a ₹10 note, a ₹5 coin and a ₹2 coin drop in one by one, and the counter shows 4 pieces.
- **check**: What is the fewest notes and coins to make ₹15 using ₹10 notes and ₹5 coins? — Answer: 2

### step_4: Adding, Subtracting and Change
- **tts**:
  - Add prices to find a bill. Subtract the bill from the money you give to find the change.
  - Regroup carefully when the money you give has zeros, like ₹100 or ₹500.
- **on_screen**: A child buys a ₹36 bookmark set with a ₹50 note. The note breaks into ₹40 + ₹10, and ₹14 change counts back from ₹36 up to ₹50.
- **check**: A cap costs ₹43. You pay with a ₹50 note. What is your change? — Answer: ₹7

### step_5: Bills and Price Lists
- **tts**:
  - A bill lists each item, how many you bought (quantity), the price of one (rate) and the amount.
  - Amount = quantity × rate. The total is all the amounts added together.
- **on_screen**: A shop bill fills in line by line: 3 apples at ₹10 gives ₹30, 2 buns at ₹8 gives ₹16, and the total ₹46 glows at the bottom.
- **check**: On a bill, 5 erasers at ₹3 each. What is the amount? — Answer: ₹15

### step_6: Multiplying and Sharing Money
- **tts**:
  - If one pen costs ₹7, then 4 pens cost 4 × ₹7 = ₹28.
  - To share money equally, divide. ₹60 shared among 5 children is ₹12 each.
- **on_screen**: Four pens line up with ₹7 tags and add to ₹28. Then ₹60 in ₹10 notes is dealt into 5 equal piles of ₹12.
- **check**: ₹40 is shared equally among 4 friends. How much does each get? — Answer: ₹10

### step_7: Offers and Best Buys
- **tts**:
  - Shops give offers like 'Buy 2, get ₹5 off'. Work out what you really pay before you decide.
  - Compare the whole cost of what you need at each shop. The cheaper price for one item is not always the cheaper total.
- **on_screen**: Two shop counters show the same snack: one at ₹10 each, the other at ₹12 each with 'Buy 3, get 1 free'. The child buys 4 at each counter and compares ₹40 with ₹36.
- **check**: Biscuits cost ₹10 a packet. With 'Buy 2, get ₹5 off', what do 2 packets cost? — Answer: ₹15

### Q03

- **stem**: Darshan's grandmother gives him a 50-paise coin for every page of neat handwriting he finishes. On Sunday he finishes 6 pages. How much money does he get?
- **options**:
  - A) ₹6
  - B) ₹30
  - C) ₹3

## Pictorial notes
- Pictorial items: Set A Q01, Q04, Q06, Q08, Q11, Q15, Q18, Q21, Q24; Set B Q01, Q06, Q08, Q10, Q11, Q14, Q17, Q20, Q24.
- Figure kinds: purses and wallets holding notes and coins; four labelled groups of money; a row of every kind of note and coin that may be used; price tags (single row, two shop boards, an hourly price pattern with a '?' tag, a toy tag beside two coin kinds); shop bills with Item / Qty / Rate / Amount columns (one with the quantities missing); piggy-bank tally charts with gates of five.
- Currency artwork: coins and notes are simple ORIGINAL generic designs (a rim and face for coins; a coloured rectangle with a frame, a wavy line and the value for notes). There are no real currency images, emblems, portraits or security features. Each denomination always has the same coin size or note colour across the chapter, so children can tell them apart, and the printed value is always shown.
- Exactness, money: every coin and note carries `data-value` in paise. The verify script reads the denomination from the PRINTED label (not the attribute), checks that it matches `data-value` and that the coin size or note colour is the one fixed for that denomination, checks that no two coins or notes overlap, and adds up each purse, wallet or group from what it read.
- Exactness, tags and bills: tags carry `data-item` and `data-price`, and the printed item name and price must match. For bills the verify script re-reads every Qty, Rate and Amount cell, checks Amount = Qty × Rate, and checks that the printed Total is the sum of the rows. For the bill with missing quantities (Set A Q21) it brute-forces every pair of quantities that fits the item count in the footer and the total, and requires exactly one.
- Exactness, tallies: each tally row's count is the number of drawn marks, and every fifth mark must be a diagonal gate. The coin beside the row gives its value.
- Each pictorial figure carries a `data-key` expression; the verify script evaluates it on the values it re-read from the drawing (not on the generator's data) and checks the result against the keyed option. Set A Q15 and Set B Q14 also brute-force every set of notes in the wallet that makes the target amount.
- Money is computed in paise throughout. No item offers two options of equal value written differently (for example ₹1 and 50 paise next to 150 paise); the generator and the verify script both convert every money option to paise and assert that the values differ.
- Coin puzzles (Set A Q20, Q22; Set B Q20, Q22) were solved by brute force in the generator and again, independently from the numbers in the stem or the drawing, in the verify script.
- Every SVG is self-contained (no external fonts, CSS, scripts or images), carries `role="img"` + `aria-label`, and each item also has an `alt` line. Label text is 8–16 px in the viewBox (8 px only for the small corner number on a note); scale with `max-width: 100%; height: auto;`.
- olympiad_section values use the house tokens: `logical` = Logical Reasoning, `mathematical` = Mathematical Reasoning, `everyday` = Everyday Maths, `achievers` = Achievers. Difficulty `hard` = Achievers-level.
- All keys were computed and asserted by `/workspace/scratch/gen_g3ch06.py` (figure helpers in `/workspace/scratch/g3ch06_money.py`); `/workspace/scratch/verify_g3ch06.py` re-checks the saved file and re-reads every figure from the SVG markup.
