## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: The Clock Face
- **tts**:
  - A clock has a short hand and a long hand. The short hand is the hour hand. The long hand is the minute hand.
  - The long hand goes all the way round in one hour. Each number it passes is 5 more minutes, so at 3 it shows 15 minutes and at 9 it shows 45 minutes.
- **on_screen**: A large clock face appears. The long hand sweeps from 12 round to 12 while the minute count rises 5, 10, 15 … 60, and the short hand creeps from 2 to 3.
- **check**: The long hand points to 8. How many minutes past the hour is it? — Answer: 40

### step_2: Reading to the Minute and on a Digital Clock
- **tts**:
  - Between two numbers there are 4 small marks, so we can read the exact minute. Count in fives to the number, then count the small marks.
  - A digital clock shows hours, then a colon, then minutes, like 9:23. The short hand is just past 9 at that time, never at 10.
- **on_screen**: The long hand stops 3 small marks past 4. The count goes 5, 10, 15, 20, then 21, 22, 23, and a digital display beside it lights up 9:23.
- **check**: The long hand is 2 small marks past 6, and the short hand is just past 2. What time is it? — Answer: 2:32

### step_3: Past, To, Quarter and Half
- **tts**:
  - Half past 4 is 4:30. Quarter past 4 is 4:15. Quarter to 5 is 4:45, because 15 minutes are left before 5.
  - After the half hour we often say 'minutes to' the next hour: 7:50 is 10 minutes to 8.
- **on_screen**: A clock face splits into four coloured quarters. The long hand stops on each quarter while the words 'quarter past', 'half past', 'quarter to' and 'o'clock' appear.
- **check**: How do we write 25 minutes to 9 on a digital clock? — Answer: 8:35

### step_4: a.m. and p.m.
- **tts**:
  - a.m. times run from 12 midnight to 12 noon. p.m. times run from 12 noon to 12 midnight.
  - When you count past 12 noon, the clock starts again from 1, and the times become p.m.
- **on_screen**: A sun rises and sets across a strip marked midnight, noon and midnight. Morning activities slide into the a.m. half and evening ones into the p.m. half.
- **check**: Is 7:00 in the evening written as 7:00 a.m. or 7:00 p.m.? — Answer: 7:00 p.m.

### step_5: How Long? Start and End Times
- **tts**:
  - To find how long something takes, count on from the start: first to the next full hour, then whole hours, then the minutes left.
  - To find a start time, count back from the end time by the hours, then by the minutes.
- **on_screen**: A timeline from 2:00 to 5:00 lights up in jumps: 2:40 to 3:00 is 20 minutes, 3:00 to 4:00 is 1 hour, 4:00 to 4:15 is 15 minutes, giving 1 hour 35 minutes.
- **check**: A game starts at 3:40 and ends at 4:10. How long does it last? — Answer: 30 minutes

### step_6: Days, Weeks, Months and the Calendar
- **tts**:
  - A week has 7 days. A year has 12 months. April, June, September and November have 30 days; February has 28, or 29 in a leap year; the others have 31.
  - On a calendar page, each column is one day of the week, so moving straight down one row adds 7 days.
- **on_screen**: A calendar page appears. A date glows, then a ring hops straight down one row at a time, showing +7, +14 and the same weekday each time.
- **check**: If the 5th of a month is a Tuesday, what day is the 12th? — Answer: Tuesday

### step_7: Units of Time and Timetables
- **tts**:
  - 60 seconds make 1 minute, 60 minutes make 1 hour, 24 hours make 1 day, and 7 days make 1 week.
  - A timetable lists when things start and end. Read across a row to find one event and down a column to compare times.
- **on_screen**: Blocks of 60 seconds stack into minutes, minutes into hours and hours into a day. Then a school timetable fills in row by row with periods and their times.
- **check**: How many minutes are there in 2 hours? — Answer: 120

- **alt**: An analogue clock. The short hour hand is a little past 4, a little less than halfway to 5. The long minute hand points to 7.
- **options**:
  - A) 5:35
  - B) 4:35
  - C) 7:20
  - D) 4:07
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: The long minute hand points to 7, and counting in fives from 12 gives 35 minutes. The short hand has passed 4 but not reached 5, so the hour is 4. The time is 4:35. 5:35 is tempting because the short hand is close to 5, but it has not reached 5 yet, so the hour is still 4.
- **hint**: On the hall clock, the short hand tells the hour it has just passed; count along the long hand's numbers in fives.

### Q02
- **id**: g3-maths-ch07-a-02
- **stem**: Abhay's tabla class starts at quarter to 6 in the evening. Which reading on a digital clock shows this time?
- **options**:
  - A) 6:45
  - B) 5:45
  - C) 6:15
  - D) 5:15
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Quarter to 6 means 15 minutes are still left before 6 o'clock. 15 minutes before 6:00 is 5:45. 6:45 is tempting because it has the 6 and the 45 in it, but 6:45 comes after 6 o'clock, not before it.
- **hint**: Abhay's class time names the coming hour; count back the minutes that are still left before that hour.
- **skill**: quarter_to_digital
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: application
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: everyday

### Q03
- **id**: g3-maths-ch07-a-03
- **stem**: Archana finishes her morning swim at 10:30 a.m. Her visit to the dentist is exactly 3 hours later. At what time is the dentist visit?
- **options**:
  - A) 1:30 a.m.
  - B) 1:30 p.m.
  - C) 10:33 a.m.
  - D) 7:30 a.m.
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Counting on 3 hours from 10:30 a.m. gives 11:30 a.m., 12:30 p.m. and then 1:30 p.m. After 12 noon the times are p.m. 1:30 a.m. is tempting because the clock reading is right, but 1:30 a.m. is in the middle of the night, not after a morning swim.
- **hint**: Count forward hour by hour from Archana's swim time, and notice what changes after the clock passes noon.
- **skill**: am_pm_across_noon
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: application
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: everyday

- **alt**: An analogue clock with small minute marks and no minute hand. The hour hand points exactly 2 small marks past 4.
- **options**:
  - A) 4:24
  - B) 4:10
  - C) 4:02
  - D) 4:20
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: From 4 to 5 the hour hand passes 5 small marks in 60 minutes, so each small mark is 60 ÷ 5 = 12 minutes. 2 marks past 4 is 2 × 12 = 24 minutes past 4: 4:24. 4:10 is tempting if you count each small mark as 5 minutes, but that is true only for the minute hand.
- **hint**: Between two numbers, Harshit's hour hand passes several small marks in a full hour; share the hour's minutes among them.
- **skill**: hour_hand_only_reading
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: pictorial
- **pictorial**: yes
