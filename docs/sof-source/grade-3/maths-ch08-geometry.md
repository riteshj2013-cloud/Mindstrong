## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Flat Shapes: Sides and Corners
- **tts**:
  - A flat shape made of straight sides is named by how many sides it has. A triangle has 3 sides and 3 corners, a square and a rectangle have 4, a pentagon has 5 and a hexagon has 6.
  - A circle has one curved edge and no corners. Turning a shape does not change its name: a square standing on a corner is still a square.
- **on_screen**: A triangle, a square, a pentagon and a hexagon appear one by one. Each corner lights up with a dot while a counter ticks up, and then the square spins onto one corner and keeps its name.
- **check**: How many corners does a hexagon have? — Answer: 6

### step_2: Straight Lines, Curved Lines and Solid Shapes
- **tts**:
  - Some lines are straight and some are curved. Solid shapes can have flat faces, curved surfaces, edges where faces meet, and corners where edges meet.
  - A cube has 6 square faces, 12 edges and 8 corners. A cylinder has 2 flat circles and a curved surface. A cone has 1 flat circle, a curved surface and a pointed tip. A sphere is curved all over.
- **on_screen**: A dice, a can, an ice-cream cone and a ball sit on a shelf. On the dice, faces glow, then edges, then corners; the can and the ball roll away while the dice stays put.
- **check**: How many edges does a cube have? — Answer: 12

### step_3: Perimeter: The Distance Round a Shape
- **tts**:
  - The perimeter is the distance all the way round the edge of a shape. Add the lengths of all the sides, or count the unit lengths along the edge on squared paper.
  - When two shapes are joined, the sides that touch are inside, so they are not part of the new perimeter.
- **on_screen**: An ant walks round a rectangle on squared paper. Each unit side it passes flashes and a counter adds 1; then two tiles slide together and the shared side fades out.
- **check**: A rectangle has sides of 5 cm and 2 cm. What is its perimeter? — Answer: 14 cm

### step_4: Area: Counting Squares
- **tts**:
  - The area of a flat shape is the number of unit squares that cover it. Count the squares, or count the rows and how many squares are in each row.
  - Two shapes can cover the same number of squares and still have different perimeters.
- **on_screen**: A shape on squared paper fills with squares one row at a time while the count grows, and a long thin strip and a small block are placed side by side for comparison.
- **check**: A rectangle on squared paper is 3 rows of 4 squares. How many squares does it cover? — Answer: 12

### step_5: Symmetry, Folding and Cutting
- **tts**:
  - A line of symmetry is a fold line where the two halves match exactly. Some shapes have one such line, some have more, and some have none.
  - If you fold paper and cut along the fold, the opened hole is the cut shape together with its mirror image.
- **on_screen**: A paper butterfly folds along a dotted line and its wings match. Then a folded paper is snipped and opens out into a full shape, and the mirror half glows.
- **check**: How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle that is not a square have? — Answer: 2

### step_6: Turns, Patterns and Tiles
- **tts**:
  - A quarter turn moves a shape like a clock hand going from 12 to 3. Two quarter turns make a half turn and four quarter turns make a full turn.
  - A pattern repeats a group of shapes. Shapes that fit together with no gaps and no overlaps make a tiling, like squares on a floor.
- **on_screen**: An arrow card turns a quarter at a time with the clock hand beside it. Then square tiles click together across a floor with no gaps.
- **check**: How many quarter turns make one full turn? — Answer: 4

### step_7: Counting Shapes, Tangrams and Nets
- **tts**:
  - To count shapes in a figure, count the smallest ones first, then the ones made by joining two, three or more, so you do not miss any.
  - A tangram square splits into 7 pieces that can be moved around to make many pictures. A net is a flat shape that folds up into a solid; a cube net has 6 squares.
- **on_screen**: A grid of four squares glows square by square, then the big outer square glows too. Then a cross of six paper squares folds up into a cube.
- **check**: How many squares of any size are there in a 2-by-2 grid of squares? — Answer: 5

### Q02
- **id**: g3-maths-ch08-a-02
- **stem**: Nimisha's tiffin set has a cube-shaped box and a cylinder-shaped bottle. How many flat faces do the box and the bottle have altogether?
- **options**:
  - A) 6
  - B) 7
  - C) 8
  - D) 9
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: A cube has 6 flat square faces. A cylinder has 2 flat circle faces, one at the top and one at the bottom, and a curved surface. 6 + 2 = 8. 7 is tempting if you count only the bottom circle of the bottle, but its top is a flat circle too.
- **hint**: Count the flat faces of Nimisha's box and of her bottle separately; the curved part of the bottle is not flat.
- **skill**: flat_faces_of_solids
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: application

### Q04
- **id**: g3-maths-ch08-a-04
- **stem**: Kunika makes a mat from 6 tiles with 1 cm sides, laid 3 in a row and 2 rows deep. A beetle crawls once round the outer edge of the mat. How many centimetres does it crawl?
- **options**:
  - A) 6 cm
  - B) 10 cm
  - C) 12 cm
  - D) 17 cm
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Along the long sides there are 3 + 3 = 6 tile edges, and along the short sides there are 2 + 2 = 4 tile edges. That is 10 edges of 1 cm, so the beetle walks 10 cm. 6 cm is tempting because there are 6 tiles, but the beetle walks along the outside edges, not across the tiles.
- **hint**: Count only the tile edges on the outer edge of Kunika's mat, going all the way round it.
- **skill**: perimeter_counting_units
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: application
- **pictorial**: no

### Q23
- **id**: g3-maths-ch08-b-23
- **stem**: Ritvik cuts a 12 cm by 12 cm square sheet into 4 equal strips, each as long as the sheet. How much longer is the total distance round all 4 strips than the distance round the sheet?
- **options**:
  - A) 72 cm
  - B) 120 cm
  - C) 48 cm
  - D) 24 cm
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: Each strip is 12 cm long and 12 ÷ 4 = 3 cm wide, so its perimeter is 12 + 3 + 12 + 3 = 30 cm. The 4 strips together have 4 × 30 = 120 cm. The sheet's perimeter is 4 × 12 = 48 cm. 120 − 48 = 72 cm. 120 cm is tempting because it is the total for the strips, but the question asks how much more it is than the sheet.
- **hint**: Find the width and the perimeter of each of Ritvik's strips, then compare all the strips with the whole sheet.
- **skill**: perimeter_increase_cutting

- Exactness, nets and solids: cube nets are checked by rolling a die across the drawn squares (six different faces are needed), and the faces meeting at the starred corner are found the same way; the verify script checks this with a separate 3D fold. The cylinder net's rectangle is as long as the distance round the circles (2 × π × radius, to 0.01). Solid drawings are recognised from the drawn parts (box faces, ellipses, side lines, outline circle).
