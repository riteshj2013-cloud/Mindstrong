## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Measuring Length with a Ruler
- **tts**:
  - Length tells us how long something is. Small things are measured in centimetres, written cm.
  - Put one end of the object at 0 and read the mark at the other end. If it does not start at 0, take the start mark away from the end mark.
- **on_screen**: A pencil slides onto a ruler and stops from 0 to 9. Then it moves to start at 2 and end at 11, and the sum 11 − 2 = 9 cm flashes: the same pencil, the same length.
- **check**: A leaf lies on a ruler from the 3 mark to the 8 mark. How long is it? — Answer: 5 cm

### step_2: Metres and Kilometres
- **tts**:
  - 100 centimetres make 1 metre, and 1000 metres make 1 kilometre.
  - We use cm for small things, m for rooms and cloth, and km for distances between places.
- **on_screen**: A row of 100 one-centimetre beads stretches into a metre stick. Then 1000 metre sticks line up along a road from a school to a temple, labelled 1 km.
- **check**: How many centimetres are there in 2 m? — Answer: 200 cm

### step_3: Weight: Grams and Kilograms
- **tts**:
  - Weight tells us how heavy something is. Light things are weighed in grams, g; heavy things in kilograms, kg.
  - 1 kilogram is the same as 1000 grams. On a pan balance, the heavier side goes down.
- **on_screen**: A pan balance with a 1 kg packet of salt on one side. Four 250 g weights are added to the other pan one by one until the beam becomes level.
- **check**: Which is heavier, 1 kg of rice or 900 g of dal? — Answer: 1 kg of rice

### step_4: Capacity: Litres and Millilitres
- **tts**:
  - Capacity tells us how much liquid a container can hold. Big amounts are in litres, L; small amounts are in millilitres, mL.
  - 1 litre is the same as 1000 millilitres. Read a jug at the top of the liquid, using the marks.
- **on_screen**: Four 250 mL glasses of water pour into a 1 L bottle and fill it exactly. Then a jug with marks every 100 mL fills to 700 mL, and the mark glows.
- **check**: How many 250 mL glasses fill a 1 L bottle? — Answer: 4

### step_5: Choosing a Sensible Unit
- **tts**:
  - Before you measure, estimate. A pencil is about 15 cm, a door is about 2 m, a bag of atta can be 5 kg, and a spoon holds about 5 mL.
  - If an answer sounds silly, like a spoon holding 5 L, check the unit again.
- **on_screen**: Cards pop up: an ant, a classroom, a road trip, a watermelon, a bucket, a spoon of medicine. The child drags each card onto cm, m, km, g, kg, L or mL.
- **check**: Which unit would you use for the water in a bucket: mL or L? — Answer: L

### step_6: Adding and Subtracting Measures
- **tts**:
  - Change everything to the same unit before you add or take away.
  - Regroup when you need to: 100 cm make 1 m, 1000 g make 1 kg, and 1000 mL make 1 L.
- **on_screen**: A 1 m ribbon has 35 cm cut off. The ribbon turns into 100 cm, the 35 cm piece snips away, and 65 cm stays.
- **check**: What is 1 m − 35 cm? — Answer: 65 cm

### step_7: Balance and Jug Puzzles
- **tts**:
  - A level balance means both pans weigh the same, so you may take the same thing off both pans and it stays level.
  - With jugs that have no marks, you can measure new amounts by filling one jug and pouring into another until it is full.
- **on_screen**: A level balance with 3 apples on one side and 1 apple plus 200 g on the other. One apple vanishes from each side, leaving 2 apples balancing 200 g.
- **check**: If 2 apples balance 200 g, how much does one apple weigh? — Answer: 100 g

### Q02
- **id**: g3-maths-ch05-a-02
- **stem**: Bhavesh wants to show his little sister how long 1 km is. Which of these is closest to 1 km?
- **options**:
  - A) the length of a school bus
  - B) the height of a coconut tree
  - C) 10 football grounds placed end to end
  - D) the length of a cricket bat
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: A football ground is about 100 m long, and 10 of them in a line make about 1000 m, which is 1 km. The coconut tree is tempting because it is very tall, but it is only about 20 to 30 m high, far less than a kilometre.

### Q04
- **id**: g3-maths-ch05-a-04
- **stem**: Dev pushes a 1 m long bamboo stick straight down into a flower pot to hold up a money plant. 85 cm of the stick is above the soil. How much of the stick is inside the soil?
- **options**:
  - A) 25 cm
  - B) 15 cm
  - C) 115 cm
  - D) 185 cm
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: 1 m = 100 cm, and 100 cm − 85 cm = 15 cm is inside the soil. 25 cm is tempting if you forget to regroup (taking 8 tens from 10 tens leaves 2 tens, but 5 ones cannot come from 0 ones without borrowing a ten).

- Exactness, rulers: each ruler group carries `data-x0` (the x position of the 0 cm mark, which lies off the drawn ruler for the broken one) and `data-unit` (pixels per cm). The verify script checks that every number label sits on its tick, that ticks are evenly spaced, that every object end lands exactly on a half-centimetre mark, and then reads each object's start, end and length from its rectangle.
- Exactness, balances: the tilt is read from the beam's two end points (left end lower = left pan heavier). Pans must hang from the beam ends. Weights carry `data-mass` and must match their printed label; unknown loads carry `data-var` and print that letter. The verify script brute-forces whole-gram values for the unknowns over every balance in the figure, requires exactly one solution consistent with every level or tilted beam, and keys the answer from that solution.
- Exactness, jugs and bottles: each jug carries `data-y0` (the bottom) and `data-ppm` (pixels per mL). The verify script reads the liquid amount from the water rectangle's height, checks that the liquid rests on the bottom and that its top lies on a drawn tick, and checks every tick label against its height. Bottles are drawn with heights proportional to capacity and the label must match. In Set B Q20 the verify script re-runs a breadth-first search over every possible pour to confirm the minimum of 7 pours from the drawn capacities and starting amounts.
- Exactness, bar models: each segment's length is read back from its width (`data-ppu` pixels per unit), every printed length must match its drawn width, the total arrow or sum note must equal the drawn total, and blank unit boxes in Set B Q24 must all be the same width.
- Each pictorial figure carries a `data-key` expression; the verify script evaluates it on the values it re-measured from the drawing (not on the generator's data) and checks the result against the keyed option.

- All keys were computed and asserted by `/workspace/scratch/gen_g3ch05.py` (figure helpers in `/workspace/scratch/g3ch05_meas.py`); `/workspace/scratch/verify_g3ch05.py` re-checks the saved file and re-measures every figure from the SVG markup.
- Original content only: written from public NCERT Class 3 themes (measuring length, weight and capacity) and generic olympiad topic headings; no past or sample paper items were used.
