## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Greetings for Every Time of Day
- **tts**:
  - We say Good morning before lunch time, and Good afternoon after lunch.
  - We say Good evening when the sun goes down, and Good night when someone goes to bed.
- **on_screen**: A sun slides across a sky from sunrise to night. As it moves, the right greeting pops up: Good morning, Good afternoon, Good evening, Good night.
- **check**: You meet your music teacher at 4 in the afternoon. What do you say? — Answer: Good afternoon

### step_2: Saying Who You Are
- **tts**:
  - To introduce yourself, say hello and tell your name.
  - To introduce a friend, say: This is my friend, and add one thing about them.
- **on_screen**: Two cartoon children meet at a park. Speech bubbles appear: 'Hi, I'm Joel.' then 'Hi, Joel! I'm Priya. Nice to meet you.'
- **check**: How can you end a hello when you meet someone new? — Answer: Nice to meet you.

### step_3: Asking Nicely
- **tts**:
  - Polite requests often start with May I, Could I or Could you.
  - Add please, and your words sound kind, not bossy.
- **on_screen**: The bossy line 'Pass the salt!' gets a red cross. It changes into 'Could you pass the salt, please?' with a green tick.
- **check**: Make this polite: 'Open the door.' — Answer: Could you open the door, please?

### step_4: Sorry and Thank You
- **tts**:
  - When we make a mistake, we say sorry and try to fix it.
  - When someone helps us, we say thank you. When someone thanks us, we can say You're welcome or Happy to help.
- **on_screen**: A child knocks over a plant pot, says 'Sorry! Let me pick it up,' and sweeps the soil back. Then a friend hands back a ball and gets a 'Thank you!'
- **check**: Your friend helps you pick up your crayons. What do you say? — Answer: Thank you!

### step_5: Saying No Kindly
- **tts**:
  - Sometimes we have to say no.
  - Say sorry or thank you, give a short reason, and if you can, offer another time.
- **on_screen**: A friend asks, 'Come to the park now?' The reply bubble builds up piece by piece: 'Sorry,' plus 'I'm helping Amma,' plus 'Can we go tomorrow?'
- **check**: What three parts make a kind no? — Answer: sorry or thanks, a reason, another time

### step_6: Notes, Notices and Invitations
- **tts**:
  - Short writing must answer who, what, when and where.
  - A notice is for many people. A note or invitation is for one person or a few friends.
- **on_screen**: An empty invitation card fills in line by line: who is inviting, what the party is, the day and time, and the place.
- **check**: An invitation says the day and the place. What else must it say? — Answer: the time (and what it is for)

### step_7: What Comes Next?
- **tts**:
  - In a conversation, read the last line carefully.
  - Your reply must answer exactly what the other person asked.
- **on_screen**: A comic: 'Would you like tea or milk?' Three reply bubbles float up; 'Milk, please' glows green, 'Yes, I am fine' fades out.
- **check**: Your friend asks, 'What is your favourite fruit?' Is 'I am eight' a good reply? — Answer: No; name a fruit, like 'Mango!'

## Dialogue, notice and message bank

### D1 — A Phone Call for Rhea (dialogue)

**Ishaan:** Hello, this is Ishaan.
**Mr. Bose:** Hello, Ishaan. I am Mr. Bose, Rhea's cricket coach. Is Rhea at home?
**Ishaan:** Sorry, sir, she has gone to the library. May I take a message?
**Mr. Bose:** Yes, please. Tell her that practice on Saturday will start at 8 a.m., not 7 a.m. She must bring her own water bottle.
**Ishaan:** Practice at 8 a.m. on Saturday, and a water bottle. I will tell her, sir.
**Mr. Bose:** Thank you, Ishaan. Bye!

### D2 — At the Clinic (dialogue)

**Receptionist:** Good morning! How can I help you?
**Nisha:** Good morning. My grandmother has an appointment with Dr. Fernandes at 11 o'clock.
**Receptionist:** May I know her name, please?
**Nisha:** Her name is Kamala Pillai.
**Receptionist:** Thank you. Please take a seat. The doctor will call her in ten minutes.
**Nisha:** Thank you very much.
