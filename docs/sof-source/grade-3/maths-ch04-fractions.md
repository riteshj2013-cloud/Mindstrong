## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Equal Parts: Halves, Thirds and Quarters
- **tts**:
  - A fraction is a part of a whole, and the parts must be equal.
  - Two equal parts are halves, three equal parts are thirds, and four equal parts are quarters.
- **on_screen**: A paper roti folds once into 2 equal halves, then a new roti is cut into 3 equal slices and another into 4. A wobbly cut into 3 unequal pieces flashes red: these are not thirds!
- **check**: A chapati is cut into 3 equal pieces. What is each piece called? — Answer: one third (1/3)

### step_2: Numerator and Denominator
- **tts**:
  - The bottom number, the denominator, tells how many equal parts the whole has.
  - The top number, the numerator, tells how many of those parts we take. When the top is 1, it is a unit fraction.
- **on_screen**: The fraction 4/7 sits next to a bar of 7 equal parts. Tapping the 7 lights up every part; tapping the 4 lights up just the 4 shaded parts.
- **check**: In 4/7, how many equal parts is the whole cut into? — Answer: 7

### step_3: Fraction of a Collection
- **tts**:
  - To find one third of a group, share the group into three equal groups and take one.
  - Two thirds is two of those groups.
- **on_screen**: Nine bangles slide into 3 equal boxes; one box glows to show 1/3 of 9 = 3, then a second box glows to show 2/3 of 9 = 6.
- **check**: What is 1/3 of 9 bangles? — Answer: 3 bangles

### step_4: Fractions on a Number Line
- **tts**:
  - Cut the space from 0 to 1 into equal gaps. Each gap is one part.
  - Count the gaps from 0, not the tick marks, to name a point.
- **on_screen**: A number line from 0 to 1 splits into 4 equal gaps. A bunny hops gap by gap, and the labels 1/4, 2/4 and 3/4 appear under the marks.
- **check**: A line from 0 to 1 is cut into 4 equal gaps. What fraction is the second mark after 0? — Answer: 2/4

### step_5: Equivalent Fractions
- **tts**:
  - Different fractions can show the same amount. Two quarters cover exactly one half.
  - If you multiply the top and the bottom by the same number, the amount stays the same.
- **on_screen**: Two same-size bars: one in halves with 1 shaded, one in quarters. The child shades quarters until the shaded parts line up, then sees 1/2 = 2/4.
- **check**: How many quarters are the same as 1/2? — Answer: 2

### step_6: Comparing Fractions
- **tts**:
  - Same bottom number? Then the parts are the same size, so more parts means more.
  - Same top number? Then the smaller bottom number wins, because its pieces are bigger.
- **on_screen**: Two same-size bars show 2/5 and 4/5 for the child to compare. Then two bars show 2/5 and 2/9, and the bigger fifths glow.
- **check**: Which is bigger, 2/5 or 2/9? — Answer: 2/5

### step_7: Adding and Subtracting Like Fractions
- **tts**:
  - When the bottom numbers are the same, add or take away only the top numbers.
  - The bottom number stays the same, because the size of each part does not change.
- **on_screen**: A bar of 9 equal parts: 4 parts glow, then 1 part fades away, leaving 3 glowing parts and the sum 4/9 − 1/9 = 3/9.
- **check**: What is 4/9 − 1/9? — Answer: 3/9

### Q02
- **id**: g3-maths-ch04-a-02
- **stem**: Ravi ate some slices of a pizza and wrote the fraction 5/8 for what he ate. What does the 8 in his fraction tell us?
- **options**:
  - A) Ravi ate 8 slices.
  - B) 8 slices were left over.
  - C) Each slice was 8 cm wide.
  - D) The pizza was cut into 8 equal slices.
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: The bottom number, the denominator, tells how many equal slices the whole pizza was cut into: 8. The top number, 5, tells how many slices Ravi ate. 'Ravi ate 8 slices' is tempting because 8 is the bigger number, but what he ate is shown by the top number.
- **hint**: The bottom number of a fraction describes the whole pizza, not the slices Ravi ate.
- **skill**: numerator_denominator
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: recall
- **pictorial**: no
