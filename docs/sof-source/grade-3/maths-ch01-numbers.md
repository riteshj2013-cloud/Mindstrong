## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Bundles of Sticks
- **tts**:
  - Let's count sticks! Ten loose sticks make one bundle. We call it a ten.
  - Ten bundles of ten make a big bundle. That is one hundred!
- **on_screen**: Loose sticks on a mat; child taps to tie groups of 10 into bundles, then 10 bundles into one big bundle of 100.
- **check**: How many sticks are in one big bundle? — Answer: 100

### step_2: Hundreds, Tens and Ones
- **tts**:
  - Every digit lives in a house. The houses are hundreds, tens and ones.
  - In three hundred forty-seven, three is in the hundreds house, four in tens, seven in ones.
- **on_screen**: Three coloured houses labelled H, T, O. Child drags the digits of 347 into the right houses.
- **check**: In 347, which digit is in the tens house? — Answer: 4

### step_3: What Is a Digit Worth?
- **tts**:
  - A digit's worth depends on its house. Eight in the hundreds house means eight hundred!
  - So four hundred seventy-two is four hundred, plus seventy, plus two.
- **on_screen**: Tap any digit of 472 to see what it is worth (400, 70, 2); blocks of hundreds, tens and ones pop up to match.
- **check**: What is 8 worth in 832? — Answer: 800

### step_4: Reading and Writing Number Names
- **tts**:
  - Let's say numbers in words. Four zero nine is four hundred nine.
  - The zero means there are no tens. Shh, we don't say it!
- **on_screen**: Number cards (409, 760, 518) and word cards; child matches each number to its name, and the zero glows softly.
- **check**: How do we write 409 in words? — Answer: Four hundred nine

### step_5: Which Is Bigger?
- **tts**:
  - To compare, look at the hundreds first. The bigger hundreds digit wins.
  - If the hundreds are the same, check the tens, and then the ones.
- **on_screen**: Two toffee jars labelled 618 and 681; child taps the jar with more toffees, then lines up three jars from smallest to biggest.
- **check**: Which is bigger, 618 or 681? — Answer: 681

### step_6: Make the Biggest and Smallest Numbers
- **tts**:
  - Want the biggest number? Put the biggest digit first.
  - For the smallest, put the smallest digit first. But zero can never go first!
- **on_screen**: Digit tiles 5, 2, 9 and then 7, 0, 4; child arranges them to make the biggest and the smallest numbers.
- **check**: Make the smallest number with 7, 0 and 4. — Answer: 407

### step_7: Even, Odd and Number Patterns
- **tts**:
  - Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8. They can make pairs with none left over.
  - Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9. One is always left alone!
- **on_screen**: Children in a park line up in pairs; the child sorts number balloons into Even and Odd baskets. Then a hopping frog skip-counts by 5s and 10s.
- **check**: Is 46 even or odd? — Answer: Even

### step_8: Before, After and Between
- **tts**:
  - The number just after is one more. The number just before is one less.
  - What comes after two hundred ninety-nine? Three hundred!
- **on_screen**: A number train with carriages 298, 299, ?, 301; child fills in the missing carriages.
- **check**: What number comes just after 299? — Answer: 300
