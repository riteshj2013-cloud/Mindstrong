# Chapter 3: Sun, Moon, and the Solar System

## Meta
- Grade: 5
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: sun, moon, earth, solar system, planets, day night, seasons, phases of moon
- Source basis: NCERT Class 5 EVS / typical olympiad themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Explain that the Sun is a star, a huge ball of hot gases that gives Earth light and heat, and that it looks small only because it is very far away.
- Explain that day and night happen because Earth rotates (spins) on its axis, and use this to work out where the Sun rises and where shadows fall.
- Explain that one revolution of Earth around the Sun makes one year, and that seasons happen because Earth's axis is tilted as it goes around the Sun.
- Explain that the Moon has no light of its own and shines by reflecting sunlight, and put the phases of the Moon in order from New Moon to Full Moon and back.
- Name the eight planets in order from the Sun (Mercury to Neptune) and recall simple facts about them, such as the largest, the hottest, and the Red Planet.
- Use simple models (a torch and a ball) to reason about day and night, seasons, and Moon phases.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: The Sun, our nearest star
- **Visual:** A dark sky full of tiny twinkling stars. One star zooms closer and closer until it becomes our bright, glowing Sun. A small blue Earth appears beside it for size. Then over a hundred Earths line up across the Sun's face. A "Do not look directly at the Sun" safety sign pops up with sunglasses crossed out.
- **TTS:**
  - "Look at all those stars! Guess what? Our Sun is one of them."
  - "The Sun is a star. It is a giant ball of very hot gases."
  - "It is our nearest star, so it looks much bigger and brighter than the others."
  - "The Sun is so big that more than a hundred Earths could line up across it."
  - "It looks small in the sky only because it is very, very far away."
  - "The Sun gives us light and heat. Without it, plants and animals could not live."
  - "Remember, never look straight at the Sun. It can hurt your eyes."
- **Interaction:** True or false cards. "The Sun is a planet." "The Sun gives light and heat." "The Sun is small." The child taps True or False, and each answer gets a short spoken explanation.

### Scene 2: Spinning Earth, day and night
- **Visual:** A globe with India marked by a small flag. A lamp on the left acts as the Sun. The globe spins slowly from west to east. As India moves into the lit half, a cartoon child wakes up. As it moves into the dark half, the child goes to sleep under the stars. A stick standing on the ground shows its shadow getting shorter towards noon and longer towards evening.
- **TTS:**
  - "Earth spins like a top on an imaginary line called its axis. This spinning is called rotation."
  - "One full spin takes about twenty-four hours. That is one day and one night."
  - "The half of Earth facing the Sun has day. The half facing away has night."
  - "Earth spins from west to east. That is why the Sun seems to rise in the east and set in the west."
  - "The Sun is not really moving around us. We are the ones spinning!"
  - "Shadows fall on the side away from the Sun. At noon the Sun is high, so shadows are shortest."
- **Interaction:** Spin the globe. The child drags the globe to turn it and must stop when India has daytime, then night-time. Then a quick tap question: "It is morning. Which way does your shadow point?" (Answer: west.)

### Scene 3: Going around the Sun, years and seasons
- **Visual:** A top view of Earth moving along an oval path (orbit) around the Sun, with a calendar flipping months as it goes. Earth's axis is drawn as a slanted stick that always leans the same way. When the northern half (with India) leans towards the Sun, it shows a summer scene with mangoes and fans. Six months later, it leans away and shows a winter scene with sweaters. A small Australia label shows the opposite season at the same time.
- **TTS:**
  - "Earth also travels around the Sun on a path called its orbit. This is called revolution."
  - "One trip around the Sun takes about three hundred sixty-five and a quarter days. That is one year."
  - "Those extra quarter days add up. Every four years we add one extra day, February twenty-ninth."
  - "Earth's axis is tilted, like a leaning pole. It always leans the same way."
  - "When our half leans towards the Sun, sunlight falls more directly. Days are longer and hotter. That is summer."
  - "When our half leans away, sunlight is slanting and weaker. That is winter."
  - "So when India has summer, Australia has winter at the very same time!"
- **Interaction:** Drag Earth to four spots on its orbit. At each spot, the child picks the season for India from four picture cards. A hint arrow shows which way the axis is leaning.

### Scene 4: The Moon and its changing shapes
- **Visual:** The Sun shines from the left. The Moon goes around Earth in a circle. The half of the Moon facing the Sun is always bright. A window on the side shows what a child on Earth sees each night: New Moon (dark), thin crescent, half Moon (first quarter), gibbous, Full Moon, then the shapes shrinking back. Astronaut footprints sit still on the dusty grey surface.
- **TTS:**
  - "The Moon is Earth's natural satellite. It goes around Earth."
  - "The Moon has no light of its own. It shines because it reflects sunlight, like a mirror."
  - "Half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun. But we see different amounts of that lit half."
  - "These changing shapes are called phases of the Moon."
  - "On New Moon night we cannot see the Moon. On Full Moon night we see a bright round disc."
  - "When the lit part grows each night, the Moon is waxing. When it shrinks, it is waning."
  - "The whole cycle takes about twenty-nine and a half days, nearly a month."
  - "The Moon has no air and no water. So there is no wind, no rain, and no sound."
- **Interaction:** Phase puzzle. Five Moon-shape cards are shuffled. The child drags them into order from New Moon to Full Moon. Then a torch-and-ball mini model: the child moves the ball around a cartoon head to make each phase appear.

### Scene 5: Meet the planets
- **Visual:** A sliding solar system map with the Sun on the left. The eight planets pop out one by one with a fun fact card: Mercury (closest, smallest), Venus (hottest, brightest in our sky), Earth (water and life), Mars (Red Planet), Jupiter (largest), Saturn (beautiful rings), Uranus (icy and blue-green), Neptune (farthest, windy and blue). A rocky band appears between Mars and Jupiter labelled "Asteroid belt". Pluto waves from far away with a "Dwarf planet" badge.
- **TTS:**
  - "The Sun and everything that goes around it make up our solar system."
  - "There are eight planets. In order from the Sun they are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune."
  - "Here is a trick to remember them: My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles."
  - "Mercury is closest to the Sun. But Venus is the hottest, because its thick clouds trap heat."
  - "Mars looks red because of rusty dust. Jupiter is the biggest planet of all."
  - "Saturn is famous for its bright rings. Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun."
  - "Planets do not make their own light. Like the Moon, they shine by reflecting sunlight."
  - "Earth is the only planet we know that has life."
- **Interaction:** Planet parade. Planet cards fall in a jumble, and the child drags them into the correct order from the Sun. Then a riddle round: "I am the biggest. Who am I?" The child taps the matching planet.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** What is the Sun?
**Options:**
A) A planet
B) A star
C) A satellite
D) A comet
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Sun is a star. It is a huge ball of very hot gases that makes its own light and heat. It is the nearest star to Earth.

### Q2
**Stem:** Which planet is closest to the Sun?
**Options:**
A) Venus
B) Earth
C) Mercury
D) Mars
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Mercury is the first planet from the Sun. The order is Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

### Q3
**Stem:** What causes day and night on Earth?
**Options:**
A) Earth going around the Sun
B) The Moon spinning on its axis
C) The Sun moving around Earth
D) Earth spinning on its axis
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Earth rotates (spins) on its axis once in about 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has day, and the side facing away has night.

### Q4
**Stem:** About how long does Earth take to go once around the Sun?
**Options:**
A) About 365¼ days
B) About 24 hours
C) About 29½ days
D) About 12 hours
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** One revolution of Earth around the Sun takes about 365¼ days. This is one year. 24 hours is one rotation, and about 29½ days is one cycle of Moon phases.

### Q5
**Stem:** Why does the Moon shine at night?
**Options:**
A) It has fires burning on its surface.
B) It reflects light from the Sun.
C) It reflects light from the lamps in our cities.
D) Its rocks glow because they are very hot.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Moon has no light of its own. Sunlight falls on it and bounces off towards Earth, so we see it shining.

### Q6
**Stem:** Which is the largest planet in our solar system?
**Options:**
A) Saturn
B) Neptune
C) Earth
D) Jupiter
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Jupiter is the biggest planet. More than a thousand Earths could fit inside it. Saturn is the second largest.

### Q7
**Stem:** Which planet is known as the "Red Planet"?
**Options:**
A) Mars
B) Venus
C) Jupiter
D) Mercury
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Mars is covered with reddish, rusty dust and rocks, so it looks red in the sky.

### Q8
**Stem:** On which night can we NOT see the Moon at all?
**Options:**
A) Full Moon night
B) First Quarter (half Moon) night
C) New Moon night
D) Gibbous Moon night
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** At New Moon, the lit half of the Moon faces away from Earth. The side facing us is dark, so we cannot see the Moon.

### Q9
**Stem:** What is Earth's position in the order of planets from the Sun?
**Options:**
A) Second
B) Third
C) Fourth
D) Fifth
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Mercury is first, Venus is second, and Earth is third. Mars comes fourth.

### Q10
**Stem:** The Moon is Earth's:
**Options:**
A) Nearest planet
B) Nearest star
C) Natural satellite
D) Largest asteroid
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A satellite is a body that goes around a planet. The Moon goes around Earth naturally, so it is Earth's natural satellite.

### Q11
**Stem:** Earth spins from west to east. Because of this, in which direction does the Sun appear to rise?
**Options:**
A) East
B) West
C) North
D) South
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** As Earth turns from west to east, places on Earth turn towards the Sun from the eastern side. So the Sun seems to rise in the east and set in the west.

### Q12
**Stem:** At 7 a.m., Arjun stands in an open field. The Sun has just risen in the east. In which direction will his shadow point?
**Options:**
A) East
B) North
C) West
D) South
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A shadow always falls on the side opposite to the light. The Sun is in the east, so Arjun's shadow points to the west.

### Q13
**Stem:** At what time of a sunny day is your shadow the shortest?
**Options:**
A) Early morning
B) Around noon
C) Late afternoon
D) Just before sunset
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Around noon the Sun is highest in the sky, so light falls almost straight down and the shadow is shortest. In the morning and evening the Sun is low, so shadows are long.

### Q14
**Stem:** It is daytime in India. Which part of Earth is having night at this moment?
**Options:**
A) The half of Earth facing away from the Sun
B) The half of Earth facing the Sun
C) Only the North Pole
D) No part; the whole Earth has day
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** At any moment, the Sun lights only the half of Earth that faces it. The other half, facing away, has night.

### Q15
**Stem:** Two astronauts stand on the Moon without radios. One shouts, but the other cannot hear anything. Why?
**Options:**
A) The Moon is too cold for sound.
B) The Moon is too dark for sound.
C) The Moon's dust soaks up all sound.
D) There is no air on the Moon to carry sound.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Sound needs air (or another material) to travel. The Moon has no air, so sound cannot travel from one astronaut to the other. Astronauts talk using radios.

### Q16
**Stem:** Two nights after New Moon, Meena sees a thin, curved, bright shape of the Moon in the sky. What is this shape called?
**Options:**
A) Full Moon
B) Gibbous Moon
C) Half Moon
D) Crescent Moon
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Just after New Moon, we see only a thin sliver of the lit half. This curved shape is called a crescent. It grows bigger night by night.

### Q17
**Stem:** Kabir notices that the bright part of the Moon is getting bigger each night. Which phase will he see next when it stops growing?
**Options:**
A) Full Moon
B) New Moon
C) Thin crescent
D) No Moon at all
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** When the lit part grows each night, the Moon is waxing. Waxing ends with the Full Moon, when we see the whole lit face as a round disc.

### Q18
**Stem:** In a classroom model, a torch is the Sun and a ball is Earth. What should a student do with the ball to show day and night?
**Options:**
A) Keep the ball still and switch the torch on and off.
B) Move the torch around the still ball.
C) Spin the ball on its own axis while the torch stays still.
D) Hide the ball behind a book.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Day and night happen because Earth spins on its axis. Spinning the ball in front of a steady torch shows each place moving into light (day) and then into darkness (night).

### Q19
**Stem:** Every fourth year, February has 29 days. Why do we add this extra day?
**Options:**
A) Because the Moon goes around Earth faster that year.
B) Because Earth spins slower that year.
C) Because the Sun moves away from Earth that year.
D) Because Earth takes about 365¼ days to go around the Sun, and four quarter days add up to one day.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Our normal calendar year has 365 days, but Earth's revolution takes about 365¼ days. After four years, the extra quarters add up to about one full day, so we add February 29. That year is called a leap year.

### Q20
**Stem:** The footprints left by astronauts on the Moon are still there after many years. What is the best reason?
**Options:**
A) The Moon's soil is like wet cement.
B) The Moon has no air or water, so no wind or rain wipes them away.
C) The Moon is always dark.
D) The Moon's gravity is stronger than Earth's.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** On Earth, wind and rain slowly wipe away footprints. The Moon has no air and no liquid water, so there is no wind or rain to disturb the dust.

### Q21
**Stem:** In June, India (in the northern half of Earth) has summer. What season is Australia (in the southern half of Earth) most likely having in June?
**Options:**
A) Summer
B) Spring
C) Winter
D) The same season as everywhere else on Earth
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** In June, the northern half of Earth leans towards the Sun and has summer. At the same time, the southern half leans away from the Sun, so Australia has winter.

### Q22
**Stem:** Solve the riddle: "I am not the closest planet to the Sun, but I am the hottest. My thick, cloudy air traps the Sun's heat like a blanket." Who am I?
**Options:**
A) Mercury
B) Venus
C) Mars
D) Jupiter
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Venus is the second planet from the Sun, yet it is hotter than Mercury. Its very thick layer of gases and clouds traps heat, so it stays extremely hot day and night.

### Q23
**Stem:** Imagine Earth stopped spinning on its axis but still kept going around the Sun. What would most likely happen?
**Options:**
A) Nothing would change.
B) Every place would have night forever.
C) Earth would stop having years.
D) Each place would have very long days and very long nights, lasting months.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Day and night come from spinning. Without spinning, one side would face the Sun for a very long time as Earth slowly moved along its orbit. Each place would get months of daylight followed by months of darkness.

### Q24
**Stem:** Which statement best explains why Earth has seasons?
**Options:**
A) Earth's axis is tilted, so as Earth goes around the Sun, each half gets more direct sunlight at some times of the year and less at other times.
B) Earth moves very close to the Sun in summer and very far away in winter.
C) The Sun becomes hotter in summer and cooler in winter.
D) The Moon blocks sunlight during winter.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis as it revolves. When your half of Earth leans towards the Sun, sunlight is more direct and days are longer, so it is summer. Distance is not the main reason, because the two halves of Earth have opposite seasons at the same time.

## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** What does the Sun give to Earth?
**Options:**
A) Only light
B) Only heat
C) Both light and heat
D) Neither light nor heat
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Sun gives Earth both light and heat. Plants use sunlight to make food, and the Sun's heat keeps Earth warm enough for living things.

### Q2
**Stem:** How many planets are there in our solar system?
**Options:**
A) 7
B) 8
C) 9
D) 10
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** There are eight planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Pluto is now called a dwarf planet.

### Q3
**Stem:** Which planet is farthest from the Sun?
**Options:**
A) Uranus
B) Saturn
C) Pluto
D) Neptune
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Neptune is the eighth and last planet from the Sun. Pluto is farther in parts of its path, but it is a dwarf planet, not one of the eight planets.

### Q4
**Stem:** Which planet is most famous for its bright, beautiful rings?
**Options:**
A) Saturn
B) Mars
C) Mercury
D) Earth
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Saturn has wide, bright rings made of countless pieces of ice and rock. They are easy to see through a small telescope.

### Q5
**Stem:** What is the imaginary line around which Earth spins called?
**Options:**
A) Orbit
B) Equator
C) Axis
D) Horizon
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Earth spins around an imaginary line that runs through the North and South Poles. This line is called the axis.

### Q6
**Stem:** What is the path along which Earth moves around the Sun called?
**Options:**
A) Axis
B) Orbit
C) Shadow
D) Pole
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The path Earth follows as it goes around the Sun is its orbit. One trip along the orbit takes about one year.

### Q7
**Stem:** The phases of the Moon repeat, from one Full Moon to the next, about every:
**Options:**
A) 7 days
B) 15 days
C) 29½ days
D) 365 days
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Moon goes from Full Moon to New Moon and back to Full Moon in about 29½ days, which is close to one month.

### Q8
**Stem:** Which is the only planet known to have life?
**Options:**
A) Mars
B) Venus
C) Jupiter
D) Earth
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Earth is the only planet where we know life exists. It has air, liquid water, and a temperature that is just right for living things.

### Q9
**Stem:** What is Pluto called today?
**Options:**
A) A dwarf planet
B) The ninth planet
C) A star
D) A moon of Earth
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Pluto was once counted as the ninth planet. Scientists now call it a dwarf planet because it is very small and shares its path with many other icy objects.

### Q10
**Stem:** The asteroid belt, a band of many rocky pieces, lies between which two planets?
**Options:**
A) Earth and Mars
B) Mars and Jupiter
C) Jupiter and Saturn
D) Mercury and Venus
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The asteroid belt lies between Mars (the fourth planet) and Jupiter (the fifth planet). It separates the small rocky planets from the giant planets.

### Q11
**Stem:** The Sun is much bigger than Earth, yet it looks like a small disc in the sky. Why?
**Options:**
A) The Sun is actually smaller than Earth.
B) The Sun is very, very far away from Earth.
C) Clouds always cover most of the Sun.
D) Earth's air makes the Sun shrink.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Things look smaller when they are far away, like an aeroplane high in the sky. The Sun is huge, but it is so far away that it looks small.

### Q12
**Stem:** Gravity on the Moon is much weaker than on Earth (about one-sixth). If Priya jumps with the same effort on the Moon as she does on Earth, she will jump:
**Options:**
A) Lower
B) Exactly the same height
C) Much higher
D) Not at all
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Moon pulls things down much less strongly than Earth does. So with the same jump, Priya would rise much higher and come down more slowly.

### Q13
**Stem:** Rohan observes the Moon for many months. He always sees the same dark patches on it. Why?
**Options:**
A) The Moon does not move at all.
B) The same side of the Moon always faces Earth.
C) Earth always blocks the other side.
D) The other side of the Moon has no patches.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Moon spins once in the same time it takes to go once around Earth. Because of this, the same face of the Moon always points towards us, and we see the same patches.

### Q14
**Stem:** Which group contains only the four rocky planets closest to the Sun?
**Options:**
A) Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars
B) Mercury, Venus, Jupiter, Saturn
C) Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Neptune
D) Venus, Earth, Saturn, Uranus
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The four inner planets, Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars, have hard, rocky surfaces. The four outer planets are giant planets made mostly of gases and ices.

### Q15

**Options:**
A) Saturn
B) Neptune
C) Jupiter
D) Uranus
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Each first letter matches a planet in order: My (Mercury), Very (Venus), Educated (Earth), Mother (Mars), Just (Jupiter), Served (Saturn), Us (Uranus), Noodles (Neptune).

### Q16
**Stem:** After Full Moon, the bright part of the Moon gets smaller night by night. What is this called?
**Options:**
A) Waxing
B) Eclipse
C) Rotation
D) Waning
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** When the lit part of the Moon we see shrinks, the Moon is waning. When it grows, the Moon is waxing.

### Q17
**Stem:** Tara puts a stick upright in the ground. At 4 p.m., the Sun is in the western sky. In which direction will the stick's shadow point?
**Options:**
A) East
B) West
C) North
D) Straight down, with no shadow
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A shadow points away from the light. In the afternoon the Sun is in the west, so the shadow points towards the east.

### Q18
**Stem:** Sunita in Delhi is having breakfast in the morning. Her cousin lives in the USA, almost on the opposite side of Earth. What is her cousin most likely doing?
**Options:**
A) Also having breakfast in the morning
B) Eating lunch at noon
C) Getting ready for bed at night
D) Watching the same sunrise
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Places on opposite sides of Earth have opposite times. When Delhi is turning towards the Sun (morning), the far side is turning away from it, so it is evening or night there.

### Q19
**Stem:** Venus is sometimes called the "morning star" or "evening star" because it shines so brightly. Which statement about Venus is correct?
**Options:**
A) It is a planet that shines by reflecting sunlight.
B) It is a real star that makes its own light.
C) It is a second Moon of Earth.
D) It shines because of lights built by people.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Venus is a planet, not a star. Its thick white clouds reflect a lot of sunlight, so it looks very bright, often just after sunset or just before sunrise.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which planet takes the longest time to go once around the Sun?
**Options:**
A) Mercury
B) Earth
C) Jupiter
D) Neptune
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Planets farther from the Sun have much longer paths and move more slowly. Neptune is the farthest planet, so its year is the longest, about 165 Earth years.

### Q21
**Stem:** In a dark room, Anu holds a white ball (the Moon) at arm's length. A lamp (the Sun) is on the far side of the room. When she holds the ball directly between her eyes and the lamp, what will she see on the ball?
**Options:**
A) Almost no lit part, like a New Moon
B) A fully lit round face, like a Full Moon
C) A half-lit ball, like a half Moon
D) A ball glowing with its own light
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** When the ball is between her and the lamp, the lit side faces the lamp, away from Anu. She sees the dark side. This is just like a New Moon, when the Moon is between Earth and the Sun.

### Q22
**Stem:** Neel says, "It is hot in summer because Earth is closest to the Sun then." Which fact best shows that Neel is wrong?
**Options:**
A) The Sun is a star.
B) When India has summer, Australia has winter at the same time, even though both are at the same distance from the Sun.
C) Earth takes 24 hours to spin once.
D) The Moon reflects sunlight.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** India and Australia are on the same Earth, so they are at the same distance from the Sun. If distance caused seasons, they would have the same season. They have opposite seasons because of Earth's tilt.

### Q23
**Stem:** A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes directly between the Sun and Earth and blocks the Sun. In which phase must the Moon be at that time?
**Options:**
A) Full Moon
B) First Quarter
C) New Moon
D) Gibbous Moon
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** When the Moon is between the Sun and Earth, its lit side faces away from us. That position is the New Moon. So a solar eclipse can only happen at New Moon (though not every New Moon has one).

### Q24
**Stem:** Find the odd one out: Sun, Pole Star, Sirius, Moon.
**Options:**
A) Sun
B) Pole Star
C) Sirius
D) Moon
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The Sun, the Pole Star, and Sirius are all stars that make their own light. The Moon is a satellite that only reflects sunlight, so it is the odd one out.
