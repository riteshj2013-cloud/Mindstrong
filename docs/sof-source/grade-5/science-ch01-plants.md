# Chapter 1: Plants — Seeds, Germination, and Dispersal

## Meta
- Grade: 5
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: plants, seeds, germination, seed dispersal, life processes
- Source basis: NCERT Class 5 EVS / typical olympiad themes (original items only)

## Learning objectives
- Name the main parts of a seed (seed coat, cotyledons, embryo with radicle and plumule) and say what each part does.
- State the three conditions most seeds need to germinate: water, air, and warmth (a suitable temperature).
- Put the stages of germination in the correct order, from a seed soaking up water to a seedling with its first leaves.
- Identify the four main ways seeds are dispersed (wind, water, animals, explosion) and match seeds to them using their features.
- Explain why seed dispersal helps plants survive and spread.
- Plan and read simple, fair germination experiments.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: What is inside a seed?
- **Visual:** A large soaked bean seed in the centre. On tap, the seed coat peels away and the seed splits into two halves, showing the cotyledons and the tiny baby plant (embryo) with its radicle and plumule. Each part glows and gets a label.
- **TTS:**
  - "Every seed is like a tiny lunch box with a baby plant inside."
  - "The outer skin is the seed coat. It protects the seed from injury and drying out."
  - "Inside are two fat halves called cotyledons. They store food for the baby plant."
  - "The baby plant is called the embryo."
  - "The radicle is the part that grows into the root. The plumule grows into the shoot."
- **Interaction:** Drag-and-drop labels (seed coat, cotyledon, radicle, plumule) onto the opened bean. A wrong drop wiggles back. A correct drop plays a soft chime.

### Scene 2: What does a seed need to wake up?
- **Visual:** Three glass jars of moong seeds: Jar 1 on dry cotton, Jar 2 on wet cotton, Jar 3 fully under boiled and cooled water with a lid. A fridge icon sits beside a fourth jar. A day counter fast-forwards three days, and only Jar 2 sprouts.
- **TTS:**
  - "Germination means a seed starting to grow into a young plant."
  - "Most seeds need three things to germinate: water, air, and warmth."
  - "The dry seeds got no water, so they stayed asleep."
  - "The seeds under boiled water got no air. Boiling removes most of the air from water."
  - "The seeds in the fridge were too cold."
  - "Surprise! Seeds do not need soil or sunlight to start sprouting. They use their own stored food."
- **Interaction:** Choice: "Which jar will sprout?" The child taps a jar before fast-forwarding. Feedback explains which condition each jar was missing.

### Scene 3: Stages of germination
- **Visual:** A time-lapse of a gram seed on wet cotton: the seed swells, the seed coat splits, the radicle pokes out and grows down, the plumule grows up, the first green leaves open, and the cotyledons shrink.
- **TTS:**
  - "First, the seed soaks up water and swells."
  - "The seed coat softens and bursts open."
  - "The radicle comes out first and grows down to become the root."
  - "Next, the plumule grows up toward the light and becomes the shoot."
  - "Soon the first leaves open. Now the plant can make its own food."
  - "As the seedling grows, it uses up the food in the cotyledons, so they shrink."
- **Interaction:** Sequence sort. Drag four picture cards (seed swells, radicle appears, shoot grows up, leaves open) into the correct order.

### Scene 4: How do seeds travel?
- **Visual:** Four zones on a map: Sky (madar, cotton, drumstick and maple seeds floating or spinning), Sea and river (a coconut and a lotus fruit bobbing), Animals (Xanthium burrs on a dog's fur, a bird eating a guava, a squirrel burying a nut), and Pop! (balsam and pea pods bursting and flinging seeds).
- **TTS:**
  - "Plants cannot walk, so their seeds need help to travel. This is called seed dispersal."
  - "Wind carries light seeds with hairs or wings, like madar, cotton, and drumstick."
  - "Water carries seeds that can float, like coconut and lotus."
  - "Animals help too. Hooked seeds stick to fur, and birds drop the seeds of fruits they eat."
  - "Some pods burst open with a pop and fling their seeds. Balsam and pea do this."
- **Interaction:** Sorting game. Seed cards drop from the top, and the child swipes each one into the Wind, Water, Animal, or Explosion zone. A hint button points to the feature that helps the seed travel (hairs, wings, floating husk, hooks, juicy pulp, pod).

### Scene 5: Why travel at all?
- **Visual:** Split screen. Left: dozens of seedlings crowded under a big parent tree, looking pale and droopy in the shade. Right: seedlings spread across a field, each with space and sunlight, looking healthy.
- **TTS:**
  - "What if every seed just fell under its parent plant?"
  - "The seedlings would be crowded, and they would fight for sunlight, water, and space."
  - "Dispersal spreads seeds to new places where they have room to grow."
  - "It also helps plants reach new areas, even faraway islands."
- **Interaction:** Quick choice: "Which side's seedlings will grow better?" After the tap, a short recap card lists the three takeaways: seed parts, germination needs, and dispersal methods.

## Quiz Set A

### Q1
**Stem:** Which part of a seed is its outer protective covering?
**Options:**
A) Cotyledon
B) Seed coat
C) Radicle
D) Plumule
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The seed coat is the tough outer skin. It protects the baby plant inside from injury, insects, and drying out.

### Q2
**Stem:** Which part of the embryo grows into the root of a new plant?
**Options:**
A) Plumule
B) Seed coat
C) Radicle
D) Cotyledon
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The radicle is the baby root. It is the first part to come out of the seed, and it grows downward.

### Q3
**Stem:** The plumule of a seed develops into the plant's —
**Options:**
A) shoot
B) root
C) seed coat
D) fruit
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The plumule is the baby shoot. It grows upward and later gives rise to the stem and leaves.

### Q4
**Stem:** In a bean seed, most of the stored food is found in the —
**Options:**
A) seed coat
B) radicle
C) plumule
D) cotyledons
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The two thick cotyledons store food. The young plant uses this food until it can make its own food in its leaves.

### Q5
**Stem:** Which set of conditions do most seeds need to germinate?
**Options:**
A) Water, light, and soil
B) Soil, fertiliser, and sunlight
C) Water, air, and warmth
D) Air, light, and manure
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Water, air, and a suitable temperature wake a seed up. Soil, light, and fertiliser become important later, when the plant grows bigger.

### Q6
**Stem:** Seeds stored in a dry, closed jar stay the same for months and do not sprout. Which condition are they mainly missing?
**Options:**
A) Sunlight
B) Water
C) Soil
D) Fertiliser
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Without water, a seed cannot swell or start using its stored food, so it stays dormant (resting).

### Q7
**Stem:** When a seed germinates, which part usually comes out first?
**Options:**
A) Green leaves
B) Plumule
C) Flower
D) Radicle
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The radicle comes out first and grows down. This anchors the seedling and lets it take in water early on.

### Q8
**Stem:** A coconut fruit is mainly dispersed by —
**Options:**
A) water
B) explosion
C) wind
D) sticking to animals
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A coconut has a light, fibrous husk that traps air, so it floats. Sea currents can carry it to faraway shores.

### Q9
**Stem:** Xanthium fruits are covered with tiny hooked spines. How are they mostly dispersed?
**Options:**
A) By wind
B) By animals
C) By water
D) By explosion
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The hooks catch on animal fur and on people's clothes. The fruits drop off later, far from the parent plant.

### Q10
**Stem:** Madar (aak) seeds have a tuft of soft, silky hairs. These hairs help the seeds travel by —
**Options:**
A) water
B) animals
C) wind
D) explosion
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The silky hairs work like a parachute. They let the light seed float on the wind over long distances.

### Q11
**Stem:** When ripe balsam (touch-me-not) pods are touched, they burst open and throw out their seeds. This method of dispersal is called —
**Options:**
A) explosion
B) water dispersal
C) wind dispersal
D) dispersal by birds
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The ripe pod bursts suddenly and flings its seeds away from the plant. This is dispersal by explosion.

### Q12
**Stem:** The carrying of seeds away from the parent plant to new places is called —
**Options:**
A) germination
B) pollination
C) photosynthesis
D) seed dispersal
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Seed dispersal means spreading seeds to new places. Germination is sprouting, and photosynthesis is how leaves make food.

### Q13
**Stem:** Why is seed dispersal useful for plants?
**Options:**
A) It makes seeds heavier.
B) It makes the parent plant grow taller.
C) It keeps all the seeds inside the fruit.
D) It reduces crowding and competition for light, water, and space.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Seeds that land in new places do not have to compete with the parent or with each other. This gives them a better chance to grow.

### Q14
**Stem:** Riya soaked some gram (chana) seeds overnight. The next morning, they were bigger and their skin was wrinkled and loose. Why do we soak seeds before sprouting them?
**Options:**
A) To wash away their stored food
B) The seeds absorb water, which softens the seed coat and starts germination
C) To kill the germs and the embryo
D) To make the seeds heavy so they sink in soil
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Soaking lets the seed take in water and swell. The softer seed coat then splits more easily so the embryo can grow.

### Q15
**Stem:** Which feature helps a fruit or seed float and travel on water?
**Options:**
A) Hooks and spines
B) Thin papery wings
C) A light, fibrous, air-filled outer layer
D) Sweet, sticky pulp
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Air trapped in a fibrous layer, like a coconut's husk, keeps the fruit afloat. Wings suit wind dispersal, and hooks suit animal dispersal.

### Q16
**Stem:** How many cotyledons does a maize grain have?
**Options:**
A) One
B) Two
C) Three
D) None
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Maize, wheat, and rice seeds have one cotyledon. Bean, pea, and gram seeds have two.

### Q17
**Stem:** Birds eat ripe guavas. Later, guava plants start growing in places far from the tree, where the birds left their droppings. Which kind of dispersal is this?
**Options:**
A) Wind
B) Water
C) Animals
D) Explosion
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The hard seeds pass unharmed through the bird's body and are dropped far away. This is dispersal by animals.

### Q18
**Stem:** Arjun set up three cups of moong seeds in a warm room. Cup 1 had dry cotton. Cup 2 had wet cotton. In Cup 3, the seeds were fully under boiled and cooled water. After three days, in which cup(s) did the seeds sprout?
**Options:**
A) Cup 1 only
B) Cup 3 only
C) All three cups
D) Cup 2 only
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Only Cup 2 had water, air, and warmth together. Cup 1 had no water. In Cup 3, boiling had removed most of the air, and the seeds were cut off from it.

### Q19
**Stem:** Seeds placed on wet cotton inside a refrigerator did not sprout, but the same seeds on wet cotton on a kitchen shelf did. Which condition was missing in the refrigerator?
**Options:**
A) Water
B) Warmth (a suitable temperature)
C) Air
D) Soil
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Both sets of seeds had water and air. The refrigerator was too cold, and seeds need a suitable warm temperature to germinate.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which of these is NOT needed for most seeds to start germinating?
**Options:**
A) Sunlight
B) Water
C) Air
D) A suitable temperature
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Most seeds can sprout even in the dark because they use their stored food. Sunlight becomes necessary once the leaves need to make food.

### Q21
**Stem:** Which order of germination is correct?
**Options:**
A) Seed absorbs water → seed coat bursts → radicle grows down → plumule grows up
B) Plumule grows up → seed absorbs water → radicle grows down → seed coat bursts
C) Seed coat bursts → leaves open → seed absorbs water → radicle grows down
D) Radicle grows down → seed absorbs water → leaves open → seed coat bursts
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Water comes first. The seed then swells, the coat splits, the root comes out, and the shoot grows up last.

### Q22
**Stem:** Pea pods dry up and split open suddenly, flinging their seeds. Which other plant scatters its seeds in the same way?
**Options:**
A) Coconut
B) Cotton
C) Castor
D) Mango
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Castor fruits also burst open when they are dry and throw their seeds out. Coconut uses water, cotton uses wind, and mango uses animals.

### Q23
**Stem:** Which statement about germination is correct?
**Options:**
A) Seeds cannot germinate without soil.
B) Seeds need fertiliser to germinate.
C) Seeds always germinate faster in the dark.
D) A seed can germinate on wet cotton without soil because it uses its own stored food.
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The cotyledons feed the young seedling at first. Wet cotton supplies water, the air supplies oxygen, and the room supplies warmth.

### Q24
**Stem:** Compare a dandelion seed with a coconut. Which statement is TRUE?
**Options:**
A) Both are dispersed by wind.
B) The dandelion seed is tiny with parachute-like hairs for wind; the coconut is large and floats on water.
C) Both are dispersed by sticking to animals.
D) Both are dispersed by bursting pods.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A seed's features match how it travels. A light seed with hairs rides the wind, and a large fruit with an air-filled husk floats.

## Quiz Set B

### Q1
**Stem:** The tiny baby plant found inside a seed is called the —
**Options:**
A) fruit
B) flower
C) embryo
D) pod
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The embryo is the baby plant. It has a radicle (baby root) and a plumule (baby shoot), ready to grow when conditions are right.

### Q2
**Stem:** What is the main job of the seed coat?
**Options:**
A) To protect the inner parts of the seed from injury and drying out
B) To make food using sunlight
C) To absorb minerals from the soil
D) To attract insects to the flower
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The seed coat is a protective cover. Making food is the job of leaves, and absorbing minerals is the job of roots.

### Q3
**Stem:** A soaked bean seed splits easily into two halves. These two halves are the —
**Options:**
A) seed coats
B) radicles
C) plumules
D) cotyledons
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A bean has two cotyledons, which are seed leaves full of stored food. The tiny embryo lies tucked between them.

### Q4
**Stem:** The process in which a seed sprouts and begins to grow into a young plant is called —
**Options:**
A) dispersal
B) germination
C) pollination
D) respiration
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Germination is the start of growth from a seed. Dispersal is about seeds moving to new places.

### Q5
**Stem:** Why does a germinating seed need air?
**Options:**
A) It uses oxygen from the air to breathe and get energy from its stored food.
B) It needs carbon dioxide to make soil.
C) Air dries the seed so it can grow.
D) Air pushes the seed coat off.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Like all living things, a seed breathes. It uses oxygen to release energy from its stored food for growth.

### Q6
**Stem:** Which part of a seedling grows upward, toward light?
**Options:**
A) Radicle
B) Root hairs
C) Plumule (shoot)
D) Seed coat
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The plumule grows up into the shoot so the leaves can reach sunlight. The radicle grows down into the soil.

### Q7
**Stem:** Lotus grows in ponds. Its fruit is light and spongy and can float. How are lotus seeds mainly dispersed?
**Options:**
A) By explosion
B) By water
C) By hooks catching on animals
D) By wind
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The spongy, floating fruit drifts on the water and carries its seeds to new spots in the pond or stream.

### Q8
**Stem:** Seeds of the silk cotton (semal) tree are wrapped in white, fluffy fibres. These seeds are dispersed by —
**Options:**
A) explosion
B) water
C) animals
D) wind
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The fluffy fibres make the seeds very light, so even a gentle breeze can carry them far away.

### Q9
**Stem:** A squirrel buries nuts to eat later but forgets some of them. Months later, new trees grow there. This is seed dispersal by —
**Options:**
A) wind
B) water
C) animals
D) explosion
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The squirrel carried the nuts and buried them in new places. That makes it an animal helper in dispersal.

### Q10
**Stem:** When Ruellia (pataka) pods get wet, they snap open with a crackling sound and throw their seeds far away. This is dispersal by —
**Options:**
A) explosion
B) wind
C) water
D) animals
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The pod bursts and flings out its seeds. Water only triggers the burst; the seeds are not carried by water.

### Q11
**Stem:** Which feature helps a seed or fruit "hitch a ride" on an animal?
**Options:**
A) Thin wings
B) Parachute-like hairs
C) A floating, air-filled husk
D) Hooks or spines
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Hooks and spines cling to fur, feathers, and clothes. Wings and hairs suit wind, and a floating husk suits water.

### Q12
**Stem:** Mango, ber, and many berries have sweet, juicy, colourful fruits. How does this help their seeds get dispersed?
**Options:**
A) The wind is attracted to bright colours.
B) Animals and birds eat the fruits and drop or spit out the seeds in other places.
C) Juicy fruits float better on water.
D) Sweet fruits burst open by themselves.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Tasty fruits work like a reward. Animals carry the seeds away while eating, then drop them somewhere new.

### Q13
**Stem:** Aman placed moong seeds on wet cotton in two cups in the same warm room. He kept one cup in a dark cupboard and one near a window. Seeds in both cups sprouted. What can he conclude?
**Options:**
A) Light is needed for seeds to germinate.
B) Light is not needed for these seeds to germinate.
C) The cupboard seeds must be dead.
D) Water is not needed for germination.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The only difference between the cups was light, and both sets sprouted. So light was not needed to start germination.

### Q14
**Stem:** Why do farmers store seeds in dry, closed containers until sowing time?
**Options:**
A) So the seeds get more light
B) To make the seeds germinate faster
C) So insects can eat the seeds
D) To keep the seeds dry so they do not sprout or rot before sowing
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Without moisture, seeds stay dormant and safe. A closed container also keeps out insects and damp air.

### Q15
**Stem:** As a bean seedling grows, its cotyledons slowly shrink and shrivel. Why?
**Options:**
A) The young plant uses up the food stored in them.
B) They absorb too much water.
C) Sunlight burns them.
D) Birds peck at them.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The cotyledons are a food store. Once the seedling has used the food and its green leaves can make food, they shrink and fall off.

### Q16
**Stem:** Which of these seeds has two cotyledons?
**Options:**
A) Maize
B) Wheat
C) Gram (chana)
D) Rice
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Gram, like bean and pea, splits into two halves (two cotyledons). Maize, wheat, and rice have only one.

### Q17
**Stem:** Arrange these germination stages in the correct order. P: radicle comes out. Q: seed swells with water. R: first leaves open. S: shoot pushes up.
**Options:**
A) P, Q, R, S
B) S, R, Q, P
C) Q, S, P, R
D) Q, P, S, R
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The seed swells first (Q). Then the root comes out (P), the shoot grows up (S), and finally the leaves open (R).

### Q18
**Stem:** Coconut palms often grow on small islands far from other land. How did they most likely get there?
**Options:**
A) Coconuts floated across the sea and washed ashore.
B) Strong winds blew the heavy coconuts there.
C) Coconut fruits exploded and flew across the sea.
D) Squirrels carried them across the sea.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Coconuts float on sea water for a long time. Their hard shell protects the seed until it lands on a beach and sprouts.

### Q19
**Stem:** A plant makes many very light seeds with feathery hairs. Where are its new plants most likely to grow?
**Options:**
A) Only directly under the parent plant
B) Only underwater
C) Spread over a wide area, in the direction the wind blows
D) Only where birds sit
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Light, hairy seeds are carried by the wind. They can land far away, so the young plants appear spread out.

### Q20
**Stem:** Which pair of plant and dispersal method is matched correctly?
**Options:**
A) Coconut – explosion
B) Drumstick (winged seeds) – wind
C) Balsam – water
D) Cotton – animals
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Drumstick seeds have papery wings that catch the wind. Coconut uses water, balsam uses explosion, and cotton uses wind.

### Q21
**Stem:** Imagine a plant whose seeds all fall right under it, with no dispersal at all. What is most likely to happen?
**Options:**
A) The seedlings will be crowded, compete for light, water, and space, and many will die.
B) The seedlings will grow faster than usual.
C) The parent plant will produce more fruits.
D) The seeds will never germinate at all.
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The seeds can still sprout, but crowded seedlings share too little light, water, and space. This is why dispersal matters.

### Q22
**Stem:** Meera wants to test whether seeds need water to germinate. Which setup is a fair test?
**Options:**
A) Cup 1 with wet cotton in sunlight; Cup 2 with dry cotton in a fridge
B) Different kinds of seeds in each cup
C) Only one cup with wet cotton
D) Two cups with the same kind of seeds, kept in the same place; only one gets water
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A fair test changes only one thing, here water. Option A also changes temperature, so you cannot tell which change mattered.

### Q23
**Stem:** Which of these is NOT a way seeds are dispersed?
**Options:**
A) Wind
B) Water
C) Photosynthesis
D) Animals
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Photosynthesis is how green leaves make food using sunlight. The four ways seeds travel are wind, water, animals, and explosion.

### Q24
**Stem:** Xanthium plants are often found growing along paths where cattle and goats walk every day. What is the best explanation?
**Options:**
A) Cattle plant the seeds on purpose.
B) The hooked burrs stick to the animals' fur and drop off along the paths they use.
C) The wind blows only along paths.
D) Paths always have more water than fields.
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Xanthium burrs hitch a ride on passing animals. They fall off along the animals' routes, so new plants grow beside the paths.
