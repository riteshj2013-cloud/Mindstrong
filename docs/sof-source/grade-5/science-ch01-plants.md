## Learning objectives
- Name the main parts of a seed (seed coat, cotyledons, embryo with radicle and plumule) and say what each part does.
- State the three conditions most seeds need to germinate: water, air, and warmth (a suitable temperature).
- Put the stages of germination in the correct order, from a seed soaking up water to a seedling with its first leaves.
- Identify the four main ways seeds are dispersed (wind, water, animals, explosion) and match seeds to them using their features.
- Explain why seed dispersal helps plants survive and spread.
- Plan and read simple, fair germination experiments.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: What is inside a seed?
- **Visual:** A large soaked bean seed in the centre. On tap, the seed coat peels away and the seed splits into two halves, showing the cotyledons and the tiny baby plant (embryo) with its radicle and plumule. Each part glows and gets a label.
- **TTS:**
  - "Every seed is like a tiny lunch box with a baby plant inside."
  - "The outer skin is the seed coat. It protects the seed from injury and drying out."
  - "Inside are two fat halves called cotyledons. They store food for the baby plant."
  - "The baby plant is called the embryo."
  - "The radicle is the part that grows into the root. The plumule grows into the shoot."
- **Interaction:** Drag-and-drop labels (seed coat, cotyledon, radicle, plumule) onto the opened bean. A wrong drop wiggles back. A correct drop plays a soft chime.

### Scene 2: What does a seed need to wake up?
- **Visual:** Three glass jars of moong seeds: Jar 1 on dry cotton, Jar 2 on wet cotton, Jar 3 fully under boiled and cooled water with a lid. A fridge icon sits beside a fourth jar. A day counter fast-forwards three days, and only Jar 2 sprouts.
- **TTS:**
  - "Germination means a seed starting to grow into a young plant."
  - "Most seeds need three things to germinate: water, air, and warmth."
  - "The dry seeds got no water, so they stayed asleep."
  - "The seeds under boiled water got no air. Boiling removes most of the air from water."
  - "The seeds in the fridge were too cold."
  - "Surprise! Seeds do not need soil or sunlight to start sprouting. They use their own stored food."
- **Interaction:** Choice: "Which jar will sprout?" The child taps a jar before fast-forwarding. Feedback explains which condition each jar was missing.

### Scene 3: Stages of germination
- **Visual:** A time-lapse of a gram seed on wet cotton: the seed swells, the seed coat splits, the radicle pokes out and grows down, the plumule grows up, the first green leaves open, and the cotyledons shrink.
- **TTS:**
  - "First, the seed soaks up water and swells."
  - "The seed coat softens and bursts open."
  - "The radicle comes out first and grows down to become the root."
  - "Next, the plumule grows up toward the light and becomes the shoot."
  - "Soon the first leaves open. Now the plant can make its own food."
  - "As the seedling grows, it uses up the food in the cotyledons, so they shrink."
- **Interaction:** Sequence sort. Drag four picture cards (seed swells, radicle appears, shoot grows up, leaves open) into the correct order.

### Scene 4: How do seeds travel?
- **Visual:** Four zones on a map: Sky (madar, cotton, drumstick and maple seeds floating or spinning), Sea and river (a coconut and a lotus fruit bobbing), Animals (Xanthium burrs on a dog's fur, a bird eating a guava, a squirrel burying a nut), and Pop! (balsam and pea pods bursting and flinging seeds).
- **TTS:**
  - "Plants cannot walk, so their seeds need help to travel. This is called seed dispersal."
  - "Wind carries light seeds with hairs or wings, like madar, cotton, and drumstick."
  - "Water carries seeds that can float, like coconut and lotus."
  - "Animals help too. Hooked seeds stick to fur, and birds drop the seeds of fruits they eat."
  - "Some pods burst open with a pop and fling their seeds. Balsam and pea do this."
- **Interaction:** Sorting game. Seed cards drop from the top, and the child swipes each one into the Wind, Water, Animal, or Explosion zone. A hint button points to the feature that helps the seed travel (hairs, wings, floating husk, hooks, juicy pulp, pod).

### Scene 5: Why travel at all?
- **Visual:** Split screen. Left: dozens of seedlings crowded under a big parent tree, looking pale and droopy in the shade. Right: seedlings spread across a field, each with space and sunlight, looking healthy.
- **TTS:**
  - "What if every seed just fell under its parent plant?"
  - "The seedlings would be crowded, and they would fight for sunlight, water, and space."
  - "Dispersal spreads seeds to new places where they have room to grow."
  - "It also helps plants reach new areas, even faraway islands."
- **Interaction:** Quick choice: "Which side's seedlings will grow better?" After the tap, a short recap card lists the three takeaways: seed parts, germination needs, and dispersal methods.

**Stem:** The picture shows a soaked bean seed that has been opened. Which letter points to the seed coat?
**Options:**
A) P
B) Q
C) R
D) S
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Q points to the tough outer skin, which is the seed coat. It protects the baby plant from injury, insects, and drying out. P is a cotyledon, R is the radicle, and S is the plumule.
### Q2
**Stem:** Which part of the embryo grows into the root of a new plant?
**Options:**
A) Plumule
B) Seed coat
C) Radicle
D) Cotyledon
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The radicle is the baby root. It is the first part to come out of the seed, and it grows downward.

### Q3
**Stem:** The plumule of a seed develops into the plant's —
**Options:**
A) shoot
B) root
C) seed coat
D) fruit
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The plumule is the baby shoot. It grows upward and later gives rise to the stem and leaves.

### Q4
**Stem:** In a bean seed, most of the stored food is found in the —
**Options:**
A) seed coat
B) radicle
C) plumule
D) cotyledons
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The two thick cotyledons store food. The young plant uses this food until it can make its own food in its leaves.

### Q5
**Stem:** Which set of conditions do most seeds need to germinate?
**Options:**
A) Water, light, and soil
B) Soil, fertiliser, and sunlight
C) Water, air, and warmth
D) Air, light, and manure
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Water, air, and a suitable temperature wake a seed up. Soil, light, and fertiliser become important later, when the plant grows bigger.

### Q6
**Stem:** Seeds stored in a dry, closed jar stay the same for months and do not sprout. Which condition are they mainly missing?
**Options:**
A) Sunlight
B) Water
C) Soil
D) Fertiliser
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Without water, a seed cannot swell or start using its stored food, so it stays dormant (resting).

### Q8
**Stem:** A coconut fruit is mainly dispersed by —
**Options:**
A) water
B) explosion
C) wind
D) sticking to animals
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A coconut has a light, fibrous husk that traps air, so it floats. Sea currents can carry it to faraway shores.
