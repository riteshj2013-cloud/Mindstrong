### Q01
- **id**: g5-maths-ch04-ach-x-01
- **stem**: In a festival sale, a saree priced at ₹1,200 first gets 25% off. On the last day of the sale, another 10% is taken off that reduced price. What does Mrunal pay for the saree on the last day?
- **options**:
  - A) ₹810
  - B) ₹780
  - C) ₹900
  - D) ₹840
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: 25% of ₹1,200 is ₹300, so the price drops to ₹900. 10% of ₹900 is ₹90, so Mrunal pays ₹900 − ₹90 = ₹810. ₹780 is tempting because it adds the two discounts to make 35% of ₹1,200, but the 10% is taken from the reduced price of ₹900, not from ₹1,200.
- **hint**: Find Mrunal's price after the first discount, and then take the second percentage of that new price.
- **skill**: two_discounts_one_after_another
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q02
- **id**: g5-maths-ch04-ach-x-02
- **stem**: Naitik ties three pieces of rope, 1.25 m, 0.8 m and 2.05 m long, end to end in one line. Each knot uses up 0.1 m of rope. How long is the joined rope?
- **options**:
  - A) 3.8 m
  - B) 4.1 m
  - C) 3.9 m
  - D) 3.18 m
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: The pieces add up to 1.25 + 0.80 + 2.05 = 4.10 m. Three pieces in a line need only 2 knots, which use 2 × 0.1 = 0.2 m. The joined rope is 4.1 − 0.2 = 3.9 m. 3.8 m is tempting because it takes away a knot for every piece, but joining three pieces in a line needs one knot fewer than the number of pieces.
- **hint**: Count the knots needed to join Naitik's pieces in a line before taking away the rope they use.
- **skill**: decimal_lengths_with_knots
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q03
- **id**: g5-maths-ch04-ach-x-03
- **stem**: Nehal's school has 600 children, and 45% of them are girls. 20% of the girls and 30% of the boys come to school by bus. How many children come by bus?
- **options**:
  - A) 150
  - B) 153
  - C) 147
  - D) 162
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Girls: 45% of 600 = 270, so there are 600 − 270 = 330 boys. By bus: 20% of 270 = 54 girls and 30% of 330 = 99 boys, which makes 153 children. 150 is tempting because it takes 25%, halfway between 20% and 30%, of all 600 children, but the two groups are not the same size, so each percentage must be found from its own group.
- **hint**: Split Nehal's school into girls and boys first; each group has its own bus percentage.
- **skill**: percentages_of_two_groups
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
