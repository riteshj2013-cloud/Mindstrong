## Interactive lesson outline
- L1: Hello, word wizard! Words are like tools in a toolbox, and today you'll learn to pick the right one every time.
- L2: Some words are twins in meaning, like big and huge. We call them synonyms. Opposites, like big and small, are antonyms.
- L3: Small add-ons change a word's meaning. Add "un" to happy and you get unhappy. Add "ful" to help and you get helpful.
- L4: Watch out for homophones! "Sea" and "see" sound the same but mean different things.
- L5: Writers also paint pictures with words. "As light as a feather" is a simile, because it compares using "as" or "like."
- L6: (optional) An idiom is a phrase with a secret meaning. "Butterflies in my stomach" means I feel nervous, not that I swallowed a butterfly!
- L7: Here's the trick for any tricky word: read the whole sentence, then ask, "Which meaning fits here?"
- L8: Let's try one. Listen: "Tara was all ears when Nani began her story. She didn't miss a single word."
- L9: "All ears" can't mean Tara grew extra ears. That would be silly!
- L10: The next sentence is my clue: she didn't miss a word. So "all ears" means listening very carefully.
- L11: Punctuation helps too. Inverted commas hold the exact words someone says, and an apostrophe can show that something belongs to someone.
- L12: (optional) And when you speak, choose kind words. "Could I," "please" and "thank you" open many doors.
- L13: Now it's your turn. Read each question, find your clue, and choose the word that fits best.
- L14: Brilliant work, word wizard! The right word in the right place makes everything you say shine.

## Passage bank (for quizzes)

### P1 — The Monsoon Drummer (poem)
The sky is a drummer who practises at noon,
He thunders and rumbles, then hums a soft tune.
The peacock unfolds like a fan made of light,
The frogs in the ditches sing all through the night.

The puddles are mirrors, the lanes are a stream,
Paper boats sail like the ships in a dream.
Amma calls, "Children! Come in, you'll be wet!"
But who wants to leave when the show isn't done yet?

By morning the drummer has packed up his sound,
And quietly, gently, green covers the ground.

### P2 — At the Stationery Shop (dialogue)
**Shopkeeper:** Good morning! How may I help you?
**Ishaan:** Good morning, uncle. I need a geometry box and two notebooks, please.
**Shopkeeper:** Ruled or unruled?
**Ishaan:** Ruled, please. They're for my science project.
**Shopkeeper:** Here you are. That will be one hundred and twenty rupees.
**Ishaan:** Oh no, I have only one hundred rupees. Could I take just one notebook today?
**Shopkeeper:** Of course. You can buy the other one tomorrow.
**Ishaan:** Thank you so much, uncle. You're very kind.
**Shopkeeper:** Not at all. Best of luck with your project!

### P3 — Ammu's Big Moment
Ammu had butterflies in her stomach. Her teacher, Mr. D'Souza, had asked for a volunteer to welcome the guests at the school's Annual Day, and Ammu's hand had shot up before she could stop it. For a whole week, she practised in front of the mirror. Her little brother, Kiran, was her audience. He clapped loudly even when she stumbled over a word. On the big day, the hall was as busy as a beehive. Ammu walked to the microphone, took a deep breath and spoke clearly. "Good evening, everyone, and welcome!" When she finished, the applause was louder than the monsoon rain. Her knees were still shaking, but her heart felt as light as a feather.
