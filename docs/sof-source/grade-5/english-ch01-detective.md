## Interactive lesson outline
- L1: Hello, detective! Today, you will read stories the way a detective looks at a puzzle.
- L2: Writers don't always tell us everything. Sometimes they leave little clues and let us work out the rest.
- L3: (optional) Have you ever guessed what was for dinner just from the smell in the kitchen? That's a clue at work!
- L4: Here is our detective trick. Look, Link, Decide.
- L5: Look for clues: what characters do, say, see, and feel.
- L6: Link the clues to what you already know from real life.
- L7: Decide what makes the most sense, and check it against the story.
- L8: You can use the same trick for a new word. Read the sentences around it and ask, "What would fit here?"
- L9: Let's try one together. Listen carefully.
- L10: "Asha opened her tiffin and groaned. Her shoes were muddy, and her hair was dripping. She shook out her soggy notebook."
- L11: Hmm. Muddy shoes, dripping hair, a soggy notebook. Something wet happened on her way to school.
- L12: From real life, I know rain makes things muddy and wet. So I decide: Asha got caught in the rain!
- L13: And "soggy"? A notebook that has been in the rain is wet and soft. So "soggy" means very wet.
- L14: (optional) Notice that the story never said the word "rain." We found it ourselves, like real detectives.
- L15: Your turn now. Read each passage slowly, and hunt for at least two clues before you choose an answer.
- L16: (optional) If you feel stuck, go back to the passage. The answer is always hiding in the clues.
- L17: Wonderful work, detective! The more closely you read, the more every story will tell you.

## Passage bank (for quizzes)

### P1 — The Pongal Mystery
On the morning of Pongal, Kavya woke early and found the rangoli outside her door smudged at one corner. Tiny prints, shaped like little stars, ran across the white rice-flour pattern and disappeared under the neem tree. Kavya frowned, then smiled. Last night she had left a bowl of milk near the gate for the thin grey kitten that visited every evening. Now the bowl was empty and licked clean. She looked up. On the lowest branch of the neem tree sat the kitten, its paws dusted white. "So you are my artist," Kavya laughed. Instead of feeling cross, she fetched more rice flour and drew a small kitten in the corner of her design.

### P2 — Dadi's Weather Report
Rohan was tying his shoelaces for cricket practice when Dadi called from the balcony. "Take your umbrella, beta." Rohan glanced at the sky. The sun was still peeping out, though a heavy blanket of grey was creeping in from the east. "It won't rain, Dadi," he said. Dadi simply pointed at the steps. A long line of ants was marching up the wall, carrying tiny white eggs. The sparrows had gone quiet, and the air smelled of wet earth even though the ground was dry. Rohan sighed and grabbed his umbrella. Twenty minutes later, as the first fat drops drummed on the ground, he was the only player on the field who stayed dry. He grinned and decided to listen to Dadi more often.

### P3 — The Cosy Nook
Class 5B had a problem: the books in their classroom cupboard were gathering dust, and nobody could find anything. So Ms. Fernandes suggested a project. First, the students sorted the books into three piles: stories, science and poems. Next, Imran and Priya made colourful labels for each shelf. Then the whole class voted on a name for their new corner. "The Cosy Nook" won by six votes. Finally, Tenzin designed a borrowing card so that every book could be tracked. Within a month, the once-forgotten books were hardly ever on the shelves. They were always out with readers. Ms. Fernandes was delighted. "An empty shelf," she said, "is the best compliment a library can get."
