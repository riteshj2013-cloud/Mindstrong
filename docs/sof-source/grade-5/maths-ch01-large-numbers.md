## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Big Numbers All Around Us
- **tts**:
  - Have you ever wondered how many people fit in a big cricket stadium? It can be more than fifty thousand!
  - Today we will learn to read, write and play with really big numbers.
- **on_screen**: Animated stadium filling with dots; a counter ticks up to 52,400. Child taps "Start".
- **check**: Which is bigger, 9,999 or 10,000? — Answer: 10,000

### step_2: The Indian Place Value Chart
- **tts**:
  - In India we group digits as ones, thousands, lakhs and crores.
  - Commas come after the first three digits from the right, and then after every two digits.
- **on_screen**: Place value chart with columns Crores | Ten Lakhs | Lakhs | Ten Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones. Child drags digits of 3,46,25,108 into the correct columns.
- **check**: Put commas in 52608913 the Indian way. — Answer: 5,26,08,913

### step_3: Place Value, Face Value and Expanded Form
- **tts**:
  - The face value of a digit is the digit itself. Its place value depends on where it sits.
  - In 4,70,312 the seven is worth seventy thousand!
- **on_screen**: Tap any digit of 4,70,312 to reveal its face value and place value; then build the expanded form with number blocks.
- **check**: What is the place value of 6 in 2,61,045? — Answer: 60,000

### step_4: Comparing and Ordering
- **tts**:
  - First count the digits. The number with more digits is bigger.
  - If the digits are equal in count, compare from the left, one place at a time.
- **on_screen**: Two number cards slide in; child taps the bigger one, then drags four town populations into ascending order.
- **check**: Which is greater: 5,08,999 or 5,09,001? — Answer: 5,09,001

### step_5: Make the Greatest and Smallest Numbers
- **tts**:
  - To make the greatest number, put the biggest digits on the left.
  - For the smallest number, start small, but never begin with zero!
- **on_screen**: Digit tiles 0, 4, 7, 2, 9. Child arranges them into the greatest and then the smallest 5-digit number.
- **check**: Smallest 5-digit number using 0, 4, 7, 2, 9 once each? — Answer: 20,479

### step_6: Rounding and Estimation
- **tts**:
  - Rounding helps us make quick, smart guesses.
  - Look at the digit just to the right. If it is five or more, round up. If it is less than five, round down.
- **on_screen**: A number line from 46,000 to 47,000 with a ball at 46,382; child slides it to the nearer thousand. Then estimates a sum by rounding both numbers.
- **check**: Round 7,861 to the nearest hundred. — Answer: 7,900

### step_7: Patterns and Word Problems
- **tts**:
  - Big numbers also make patterns. Find what is added or taken away each time.
  - In word problems, read slowly, decide whether to add or subtract, and then check your answer.
- **on_screen**: Pattern strip 2,15,000 → 2,25,000 → 2,35,000 → ? followed by a short story about a school fair collection; child picks the operation and solves.
- **check**: What comes next: 2,15,000; 2,25,000; 2,35,000; ? — Answer: 2,45,000

### Q01
- **stem**: In the number 4,73,58,216, which digit is in the ten-lakhs place?
- **options**:
  - A) 3
  - B) 4
  - C) 7
  - D) 5
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: Reading from the left, 4 is in crores and 7 is in ten lakhs, so 3 (lakhs) is a common off-by-one-place trap.
- **skill**: place_value
- **difficulty**: easy
