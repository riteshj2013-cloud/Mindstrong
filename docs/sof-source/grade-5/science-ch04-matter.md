## Learning objectives
- Explain that matter takes up space and has mass, and sort everyday materials by useful properties.
- Compare transparent, translucent and opaque materials; conductors and insulators of heat and electricity.
- Describe dissolving, floating/sinking, and simple mixtures/solutions.
- Choose separation methods: handpicking, sieving, sedimentation, filtration, evaporation, magnets.
- Tell reversible from irreversible changes and name melting, freezing, evaporation and condensation.
- Link a material's property to why we use it for a job (wires, windows, raincoats, pan handles).

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Matter and materials around us
- **Visual:** Kitchen and classroom objects glow as matter with different properties.
- **TTS:** Everything that takes up space and has mass is matter. Materials are the substances we use to make things.
- **Interaction:** Sort cards into hard/soft, flexible/rigid, transparent/opaque.

### Scene 2: Conductors and insulators
- **Visual:** Circuit gap X and a hot pan with wood versus metal handles.
- **TTS:** Metals usually let heat and electricity pass. Wood, plastic and rubber often stop them — they are insulators.
- **Interaction:** Predict which rod lights the bulb; which handle stays cooler.

### Scene 3: Mixtures and dissolving
- **Visual:** Salt stirred into water; sand settling in a jar.
- **TTS:** Some solids dissolve to make a solution. Others settle or can be filtered out.
- **Interaction:** Choose dissolve versus settle for salt, sand and chalk.

### Scene 4: Separation methods
- **Visual:** Sieve, filter funnel, evaporating dish, magnet over sand.
- **TTS:** We pick the method that matches the mixture: size, dissolve, magnetic, or liquid versus solid.
- **Interaction:** Match each mixture card to the best method.

### Scene 5: Changes we can reverse — and ones we cannot
- **Visual:** Ice melting and refreezing beside paper burning to ash.
- **TTS:** Melting and freezing can reverse. Burning usually cannot give the paper back.
- **Interaction:** Tap reversible or irreversible for five everyday changes.

## Quiz Set A
### Q1
**Stem:** Anything that takes up space and has mass is called —
**Options:**
A) matter
B) energy
C) force
D) light
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Matter is anything that takes up space and has mass. Solids, liquids and gases are all matter.

### Q2
**Stem:** Which of these is a property of most metals?
**Options:**
A) They are usually good conductors of heat
B) They are always transparent
C) They dissolve in water easily
D) They never bend
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Most metals conduct heat (and electricity) well. Transparency and easy dissolving are not metal properties.

### Q4
**Stem:** A material that lets almost all light pass through clearly is called —
**Options:**
A) transparent
B) opaque
C) translucent
D) magnetic
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Transparent materials (clear glass, clean water) let light pass so you can see through them clearly.

### Q5
**Stem:** Salt disappears when stirred into water, but you can taste it. The salt has —
**Options:**
A) dissolved
B) vanished forever
C) frozen
D) evaporated
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Salt dissolves in water. The particles spread out so you cannot see them, but the salt is still there.

### Q11
**Stem:** To separate iron nails from a heap of sand quickly, the best method is —
**Options:**
A) filtration
B) a magnet
C) evaporation
D) using only a large-hole sieve
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Iron is magnetic, so a magnet pulls the nails out of the sand. Filtration needs a liquid mixture.

### Q12
**Stem:** Sand does not dissolve in water. After stirring and waiting, sand settles at the bottom. This settling is called —
**Options:**
A) evaporation
B) sedimentation
C) condensation
D) melting
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Sedimentation is when heavier undissolved particles settle down. You can then pour off the clear water (decantation).
