- **alt**: Six yellow clue cards, one under another. Clue 1: It is a 6-digit number. Clue 2: Its lakhs digit is 3. Clue 3: Rounded to the nearest ten thousand, it is 3,50,000. Clue 4: Its last three digits are all 0. Clue 5: Its digits add up to 12. Clue 6: Its thousands digit is more than its ten-thousands digit.
- **options**:
  - A) 3,54,000
  - B) 3,45,000
  - C) 3,36,000
  - D) 3,44,000
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: Clues 2 and 4 give 3, ?, ?, 0, 0, 0, so the two middle digits add up to 12 − 3 = 9. Clue 3 means the number is from 3,45,000 to 3,54,999, so the ten-thousands and thousands digits are 4 and 5 or 5 and 4. Clue 6 needs the thousands digit to be the bigger one, so the number is 3,45,000. 3,54,000 is tempting because it passes Clues 1 to 5, but its thousands digit 4 is less than its ten-thousands digit 5, so it breaks Clue 6.
- **hint**: Aashna's rounding card means the number lies close to 3,50,000; list the numbers that also pass the digit-sum card.
- **skill**: six_digit_number_from_rounding_clues
- **difficulty**: hard

### Q02
- **id**: g5-maths-ch01-ach-x-02
- **stem**: A milk dairy collected 4,84,750 litres in March and 3,16,480 litres in April. Abeer rounds both amounts to the nearest ten thousand and then subtracts. His teacher subtracts the exact amounts first and then rounds that difference to the nearest ten thousand. How many litres apart are their two answers?
- **options**:
  - A) 8,270 L
  - B) 1,730 L
  - C) 20,000 L
  - D) 10,000 L
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: Abeer: 4,80,000 − 3,20,000 = 1,60,000. Teacher: 4,84,750 − 3,16,480 = 1,68,270, which rounds to 1,70,000. The answers are 1,70,000 − 1,60,000 = 10,000 L apart. 8,270 L is tempting because it compares Abeer's answer with the exact difference 1,68,270, but the teacher rounds that difference before comparing.
- **hint**: Work out Abeer's rounded subtraction and the teacher's exact subtraction separately, and round the teacher's result before comparing them.
- **skill**: round_then_subtract_versus_subtract_then_round
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q03
- **id**: g5-maths-ch01-ach-x-03
- **stem**: Agastya writes 6,4□,872 and 6,48,□15. The same digit goes in both boxes. For how many of the digits 0 to 9 is the first number greater than the second?
- **options**:
  - A) 2
  - B) 1
  - C) 3
  - D) 0
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: Both numbers start 6,4. If the box digit is less than 8, the first number has a smaller ten-thousands digit and loses. With 9 the first number is 6,49,872, which beats 6,48,915. With 8 the numbers are 6,48,872 and 6,48,815, and 872 is more than 815, so 8 works too. That makes 2 digits. 1 is tempting because only 9 makes the ten-thousands digit bigger, but when the box is 8 the comparison moves to the hundreds, tens and ones, and the first number still wins.
- **hint**: Compare Agastya's numbers place by place from the left; when the box digit matches the other number's digit, keep comparing further right.
- **skill**: missing_digit_comparison_count
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q04
- **id**: g5-maths-ch01-ach-x-04
- **stem**: Ahana's list holds every whole number whose nearest ten thousand is 7,40,000. How many numbers on her list have 3 as their ten-thousands digit?
- **options**:
  - A) 4,999
  - B) 10,000
  - C) 5,000
  - D) 5,001
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: The list runs from 7,35,000 (the smallest number that rounds up) to 7,44,999 (the largest that rounds down). The numbers with ten-thousands digit 3 are 7,35,000 to 7,39,999. Counting both ends, that is 7,39,999 − 7,35,000 + 1 = 5,000 numbers. 4,999 is tempting because it is 7,39,999 − 7,35,000, but subtracting the ends leaves out one of them, so 1 must be added back.
- **hint**: Find the smallest and largest numbers on Ahana's list first, then keep the stretch whose ten-thousands digit is 3 and count both ends.
- **skill**: count_numbers_in_a_rounding_range
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
