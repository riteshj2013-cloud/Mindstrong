# Chapter 5: Force and Simple Machines

## Meta
- Grade: 5
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: force, friction, gravity, lever, pulley, inclined plane, wheel and axle, screw, wedge, pictorial, svg-diagrams
- Source basis: NCERT Class 5 EVS / typical olympiad themes (original items only)

## Pictorial notes
Visual items include force scenes P–S, see-saw lever, fixed pulley, ramp versus lift, wheel cart, and axe wedge. Grade 5 depth only — no pascal calculations.

## Learning objectives
- Define force as a push or pull and list what forces can do to motion and shape.
- Give everyday examples of friction, gravity and magnetic force (G5 level, no pressure formulas).
- Identify lever, pulley, inclined plane, wheel and axle, screw and wedge.
- Name fulcrum, load and effort on a simple lever.
- Explain how a ramp or wheels can make a task easier.
- Say when friction helps (grip) and when we reduce it (oil, smooth wheels).

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Pushes and pulls
- **Visual:** Kick a ball, squash clay, tug a rope.
- **TTS:** A force is a push or a pull. It can start, stop, turn or reshape things.
- **Interaction:** Match each clip to start motion, slow down, or change shape.

### Scene 2: Friction, gravity, magnets
- **Visual:** Shoes on tiles, mango falling, magnet and pin.
- **TTS:** Friction opposes sliding. Gravity pulls us down. Magnets can pull some metals without touching.
- **Interaction:** Sort force cards into contact and non-contact.

### Scene 3: Levers
- **Visual:** See-saw with fulcrum F, load and effort E.
- **TTS:** A lever is a bar that turns on a fulcrum. Crowbars and scissors use this idea.
- **Interaction:** Drag labels fulcrum, load and effort onto the see-saw.

### Scene 4: Pulleys and ramps
- **Visual:** Flagpole pulley and a box on a ramp.
- **TTS:** A fixed pulley changes pull direction. A ramp lets you use a smaller force over a longer path.
- **Interaction:** Predict which path needs the smaller push.

### Scene 5: Wheels, screws and wedges
- **Visual:** Cart wheels, jar lid threads, axe wedge.
- **TTS:** Wheels reduce scraping. A screw is a ramp around a rod. A wedge splits materials apart.
- **Interaction:** Match each tool photo to its simple machine name.

## Quiz Set A
### Q1
**Stem:** In science, a force is best described as —
**Options:**
A) a push or a pull
B) only a colour change
C) only a loud sound
D) the mass of an object
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A force is any push or pull. Mass measures how much matter an object has; it is not a force.

### Q3
**Stem:** Earth pulls a falling mango downward. This force is called —
**Options:**
A) gravity
B) friction only
C) magnetism only
D) sound
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Gravity is the pull of Earth on objects. It acts even when nothing is touching the mango.

### Q4
**Stem:** A simple machine that is a stiff bar turning about a fixed point is a —
**Options:**
A) lever
B) screw only
C) wheel without an axle
D) electric motor
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A lever is a rigid bar that turns about a fulcrum. See-saws and crowbars are levers.

**Options:**
A) Scissors
B) A slide ramp alone
C) A screw lid alone
D) A magnet
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Scissors have two lever arms sharing a fulcrum at the pivot screw.

### Q7
**Stem:** A ball at rest starts moving when kicked. The kick —
**Options:**
A) removes all gravity
B) applies a force that starts motion
C) changes the ball's mass
D) is not a force
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Forces can start motion. The kick pushes the ball; mass stays the same and gravity still acts.

### Q8
**Stem:** We rub our hands together to feel warmth. The force opposing the rubbing is —
**Options:**
A) gravity alone
B) friction
C) magnetism alone

**Explanation:** Friction between the palms opposes the sliding and can produce heat.

### Q11
**Stem:** Which force can act without touching the object?
**Options:**
A) Friction between shoe and floor
B) The pull of a magnet on a nearby iron pin
C) Pushing a door with your hand
D) Dragging a box on sand
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Magnetism (and gravity) can act at a distance. Friction and muscular pushes need contact.

### Q12

**Options:**

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Rougher soles raise friction so your feet do not slide as easily.

### Q17
**Stem:** Oiling a bicycle chain usually —
**Options:**
A) increases friction on purpose
B) increases the bike's mass a lot
C) reduces friction so parts move more easily
D) turns the chain into a lever
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Lubricants reduce friction between moving parts so they do not scrape and wear as much.

### Q18
**Stem:** The load on a lever is —
**Options:**
A) always the fulcrum pin
B) the effort you apply only
C) the object you want to move or lift
D) never needed
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Load means the weight or resistance you are trying to move. Effort is the force you apply.

### Q19
**Stem:** Which example is an inclined plane?
**Options:**
A) A see-saw
B) A flagpole pulley
C) Scissors
D) A sloping ramp into a truck
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A ramp is a classic inclined plane. See-saws and scissors are levers; flagpoles often use pulleys.

### Q20
**Stem:** A door knob is a simple machine closest to a —
**Options:**
A) wedge splitting wood
B) fixed pulley on a well
C) lever see-saw only
D) wheel and axle
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Turning the knob (wheel) turns a shaft (axle) that pulls the latch — wheel and axle.

### Q21
**Stem:** Which statement is true?
**Options:**
A) Forces cannot change direction of motion
B) Friction always helps sliding with zero grip needed
C) Magnets attract all plastics
D) A force can change an object's speed or direction
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Forces can speed up, slow down, or turn objects. Magnets do not attract ordinary plastics.

### Q22
**Stem:** Why do we use a crowbar to lift a heavy lid?
**Options:**
A) It removes the lid's mass
B) It creates energy
C) It stops gravity
D) It is a lever that helps a smaller effort move a larger load
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** With the fulcrum placed well, a crowbar multiplies your effort so a heavy lid becomes easier to lift.

### Q23
**Stem:** Smooth ice is slippery mainly because there is —
**Options:**
A) extra magnetism
B) extra gravity only
C) more sand friction
D) very little friction
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Low friction means surfaces slide easily. That is why ice feels slippery.

### Q24
**Stem:** Which is NOT a simple machine in this chapter's usual list?
**Options:**
A) Lever
B) Pulley
C) Inclined plane
D) Smartphone battery
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Levers, pulleys, ramps, wheels, screws and wedges are simple machines. A battery is an energy source, not a simple machine.
