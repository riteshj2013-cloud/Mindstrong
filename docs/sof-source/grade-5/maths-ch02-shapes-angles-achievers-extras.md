### Q02
- **id**: g5-maths-ch02-ach-x-02
- **stem**: At 4:40, what is the smaller angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of Anshika's wall clock? Remember that the hour hand keeps moving as the minutes pass.
- **options**:
  - A) 100°
  - B) 120°
  - C) 110°
  - D) 80°
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: The minute hand points at 8, which is 8 × 30° = 240° from 12. The hour hand moves 30° in an hour, so in 40 minutes it moves 20° past the 4: it is at 120° + 20° = 140°. The angle between them is 240° − 140° = 100°. 120° is tempting because it treats the hour hand as still pointing exactly at 4, but by 4:40 it has moved two-thirds of the way towards 5.
- **hint**: In forty minutes the hour hand on Anshika's clock creeps part of the way from the 4 towards the 5.
- **skill**: clock_angle_with_moving_hour_hand
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q03
- **id**: g5-maths-ch02-ach-x-03
- **stem**: Aradhya draws four rays OP, OQ, OR and OS from point O, in that order. Each angle between neighbouring rays is 40°. Counting every angle made by any two of the rays, how many of these angles are acute?
- **options**:
  - A) 3
  - B) 6
  - C) 4
  - D) 5
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: Four rays make 6 angles in all: three between neighbours (POQ, QOR, ROS) of 40° each, two that skip one ray (POR, QOS) of 40° + 40° = 80° each, and one from the first ray to the last (POS) of 120°. The 40° and 80° angles are acute and 120° is obtuse, so 5 angles are acute. 3 is tempting because it counts only the angles between neighbouring rays, but angles such as POR, made by rays that are not next to each other, are angles too.
- **hint**: List every pair of Aradhya's rays, not just neighbours, and find each angle by adding the 40° steps between them.
- **skill**: count_all_angles_between_rays
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q05
- **id**: g5-maths-ch02-ach-x-05
- **stem**: A giant wheel at a mela has 12 cabins spaced equally, numbered 1 to 12 going clockwise. Cabin 1 is at the top. Chetna's wheel turns clockwise until cabin 5 reaches the top for the first time. Through how many degrees has it turned?
- **options**:
  - A) 120°
  - B) 150°
  - C) 270°
  - D) 240°
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: Neighbouring cabins are 360° ÷ 12 = 30° apart, so cabin 5 starts 4 × 30° = 120° clockwise from the top. Turning clockwise carries cabin 5 further away from the top first, so it must go the long way round: 360° − 120° = 240°. 120° is tempting because cabin 5 is 120° from the top, but that turn would be anticlockwise.
- **hint**: As Chetna's wheel turns clockwise, think about which way cabin 5 travels and how far it must go to reach the top.
- **skill**: giant_wheel_turn_direction
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
