## Interactive lesson outline
- L1: Hello, word builder! Today, we are going to build sentences the way builders put up a house: one strong block at a time.
- L2: Every word in a sentence has a job. Once you know the job, grammar becomes easy.
- L3: Nouns name things: people, places, animals and ideas, like Kabir, Pune, goose and kindness.
- L4: Pronouns stand in for nouns, so we don't repeat ourselves. He, she, it, they, mine, himself.
- L5: Adjectives describe nouns. They can compare too: tall, taller, tallest.
- L6: Verbs show action, and their tense tells us when: yesterday, right now, or tomorrow.
- L7: Adverbs tell us how, when or where something happens, like slowly, today or outside.
- L8: (optional) Here's a secret: a word can change its job. "Fast" can describe a car or tell how someone runs.
- L9: Let's build one together. Listen: "Mira quickly packed her heavier bag. She is leaving for camp tomorrow."
- L10: Mira is a noun, a name. "She" is a pronoun standing in for Mira.
- L11: "Heavier" is an adjective comparing two bags, so it ends in -er. "Quickly" is an adverb telling how she packed.
- L12: "Is leaving" plus "tomorrow" tells me this is a plan for the future. Every block has a job!
- L13: One last check: the subject and verb must agree. One girl "packs." Two girls "pack."
- L14: (optional) If a sentence sounds odd when you read it aloud, look for a block that doesn't fit.
- L15: Your turn now. For each question, ask, "What job is this word doing?"
- L16: Fantastic building, everyone! Every sentence you fix makes your English stronger.

## Passage bank (for quizzes)

### P1 — The Lemonade Stall
Every year, our colony holds a small mela in the park behind the post office. This year, my brother Kabir and I are running a lemonade stall. Last Sunday, we painted a bright yellow sign and borrowed six glass jars from Mrs. Iyer. Right now, Kabir is squeezing lemons, and I am counting paper cups. Our cousin Zoya will bring mint leaves tomorrow morning. She grows them in pots on her balcony. Kabir thinks our lemonade is the tastiest in the whole colony. I am not so sure, because Uncle Joseph's kulfi stall is always the busiest one! Still, we are going to try our best. The mela begins at four o'clock sharp.

### P2 — At the Bird Sanctuary (dialogue)
**Guide:** Look carefully, children. That heron is standing very still.
**Neha:** Why is it so quiet?
**Guide:** It is waiting for a fish. Herons hunt patiently.
**Sam:** Those ducks are much noisier than the heron!
**Guide:** Yes, and the geese are the noisiest birds here. Each goose honks loudly when someone comes near.
**Neha:** Who feeds them?
**Guide:** Nobody feeds them. They find their own food in the lake.
**Sam:** I will draw the heron in my notebook tonight.
**Neha:** And I am going to write a poem about the geese!

### P3 — Grandpa's Old Bicycle
Grandpa's bicycle is older than my father. It has a rusty bell, a wide leather seat and a basket that once carried vegetables from the market every day. Yesterday, Grandpa and I cleaned it together. He oiled the chain carefully while I polished the handlebars. "This bicycle took me to my first job," he said proudly. "It carried your grandmother to her school, where she taught for thirty years." I tried to ride it, but my feet barely touched the pedals. Grandpa laughed gently. "You will grow into it," he promised. Today, the bicycle stands in the courtyard, shining like new. It is the most beautiful thing in our house.
