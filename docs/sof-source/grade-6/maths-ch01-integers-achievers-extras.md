- **alt**: A number line from −10 to 10 with every whole number printed below its tick. Point P is drawn at −7 and point Q at 5.
- **options**:
  - A) 2
  - B) 3
  - C) −1
  - D) −5
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: P is at −7 and Q is at 5, so they are 5 − (−7) = 12 units apart. Halfway is 6 units from P: −7 + 6 = −1. Moving 4 units to the left gives −1 − 4 = −5. 2 is tempting because it takes half of the 12-unit gap and counts those 6 units from zero, then moves left, but the halfway point has to be measured from P (or back from Q), not from zero.
- **hint**: Count the units from P to Q on Hridaan's line, then start at P and go halfway towards Q before moving left.

### Q02
- **id**: g6-maths-ch01-ach-x-02
- **stem**: At 6 a.m. in Leh, Ishir's thermometer read −9°C. By noon the temperature had risen by 14°C. By midnight it had fallen by 3°C more than the rise. What did the thermometer read at midnight?
- **options**:
  - A) 2°C
  - B) −12°C
  - C) −26°C
  - D) −3°C
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: At noon the reading was −9 + 14 = 5°C. The fall was 3°C more than the 14°C rise, so the temperature fell 14 + 3 = 17°C. At midnight the thermometer read 5 − 17 = −12°C. 2°C is tempting because it takes the fall as only 3°C, but “3°C more than the rise” means a fall of 17°C.
- **hint**: First find the noon reading on Ishir's thermometer, then work out the size of the fall before subtracting it.
- **skill**: temperature_rise_then_bigger_fall
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q03
- **id**: g6-maths-ch01-ach-x-03
- **stem**: Kavish works out X = (−6) × (−4) − 30 and Y = (−45) ÷ (−9) − (−3) × (−4). Which of these statements is true?
- **options**:
  - A) Y is greater, by 1
  - B) X is greater, by 13
  - C) X is greater, by 1
  - D) Y is greater, by 13
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: X: (−6) × (−4) = 24, and 24 − 30 = −6. Y: (−45) ÷ (−9) = 5 and (−3) × (−4) = 12, so Y = 5 − 12 = −7. On the number line −6 is to the right of −7, so X is greater, by 1, because −6 − (−7) = 1. Y is greater, by 1 is tempting because 7 is bigger than 6, but among negative numbers the one farther from zero is the smaller.
- **hint**: Work out each part of Kavish's X and Y separately, keeping careful track of signs, then place both results on a number line.
- **skill**: compare_two_integer_expressions
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers

### Q05
- **id**: g6-maths-ch01-ach-x-05
- **stem**: In a quiz of 25 questions, each correct answer earns +4 marks, each wrong answer earns −1 mark, and a question left out earns 0. Dhairya answered 20 questions and scored 50 marks. How many of his answers were wrong?
- **options**:
  - A) 14
  - B) 10
  - C) 6
  - D) 4
- **answer**: C
- **explanation**: If all 20 answers had been correct he would have scored 20 × 4 = 80. Each answer that is wrong instead of correct loses 4 + 1 = 5 marks. He is 80 − 50 = 30 marks short, so 30 ÷ 5 = 6 answers were wrong. Check: 14 correct and 6 wrong give 14 × 4 − 6 = 50. 14 is tempting because it is the number of correct answers, but the question asks how many were wrong.
- **hint**: Imagine all of Dhairya's answered questions were correct, then see how many marks each wrong answer costs compared with a correct one.
- **skill**: negative_marking_quiz_score
- **difficulty**: hard
- **type**: mcq
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: achievers
