## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Read Every Clue First
- **tts**:
  - An achiever reads the whole question, every clue and every number, before choosing an answer.
  - An option that fits the first clue may still break a later one, so a quick choice is often wrong.
- **on_screen**: Four clue cards flip over one at a time; an option that looked right fades out when the last card is read.
- **check**: A number is between 10 and 50, is a multiple of 5, and its digits add up to 8. Which number is it? — Answer: 35

### step_2: Make the Units Match
- **tts**:
  - Before you compare or add amounts, write them all in the same unit: metres with metres, millilitres with millilitres.
  - Remember 1 km = 1,000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 kg = 1,000 g and 1 L = 1,000 mL.
- **on_screen**: Bottles labelled in litres and millilitres slide onto one scale and their labels change to millilitres.
- **check**: Which is longer, 1 km 80 m or 1,200 m? — Answer: 1,200 m

### step_3: Cross Out What Fails
- **tts**:
  - When many numbers are possible, list them and test one clue at a time.
  - Cross out a number as soon as it fails a single clue; whatever survives every clue is the answer.
- **on_screen**: A grid of numbers loses a few numbers with each clue until one number glows.
- **check**: Which of 12, 18, 20 and 24 is a multiple of 4 but not of 6? — Answer: 20

### step_4: Test Each Statement
- **tts**:
  - For a true-or-false question, work out each statement with real numbers instead of judging by how it looks.
  - Bigger numbers in a fraction do not always mean a bigger amount: one quarter of 40 is less than one third of 36.
- **on_screen**: Four statement cards each get a tick or a cross after the sums beside them are worked out.
- **check**: True or false: one half of 18 is more than one third of 24. — Answer: True (9 is more than 8)

### step_5: Join Two Ideas
- **tts**:
  - Hard problems often join two ideas: first find a perimeter, then a cost; or first read a measure, then take a fraction of it.
  - Finish the first step fully and write its answer down before starting the second step.
- **on_screen**: A garden outline gets its sides added up, then the total slides into a price box and is multiplied.
- **check**: A square park has sides of 10 m. Fencing costs ₹20 for each metre. What does fencing the whole park cost? — Answer: ₹800

### step_6: Watch the Leftover
- **tts**:
  - Some numbers in a story are there to catch you: a short bit left over after cutting, a part kept aside, or a weight on the same pan.
  - Ask what each number is for, and check whether the leftover part belongs in the answer.
- **on_screen**: A ribbon is cut into equal pieces and the short end piece blinks to show it is not one of them.
- **check**: From a 1 m ribbon, how many whole 30 cm pieces can be cut? — Answer: 3

### step_7: Check Your Answer
- **tts**:
  - When you have an answer, put it back into the question and test every condition once more.
  - If an option passes all but one condition, it is a wrong option the question set, not the answer.
- **on_screen**: The chosen answer is dropped back into the story and each condition lights up green, one by one.
- **check**: Does 46 fit both clues 'leaves 1 over in fives' and 'is a multiple of 3'? — Answer: No, 46 is not a multiple of 3

### Q02
- **id**: g4-maths-ch13-a-02
- **stem**: Bhuvi reads 5,38,204 on her railway ticket. Which sentence correctly gives the value of one of its digits?
- **options**:
  - A) The digit 3 stands for 3,000.
  - B) The digit 2 stands for 20.
  - C) The digit 5 stands for 50,000.
  - D) The digit 8 stands for 8,000.
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: In 5,38,204 the places from the left are lakhs, ten thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens and ones. The 8 is in the thousands place, so it stands for 8,000. - **skill**: true_place_value_statement
