## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Tally Marks
- **tts**:
  - Tally marks help us count while things happen. Draw one stick for each thing.
  - The fifth mark goes across the first four, making a bundle of five. Count bundles in fives, then add the single sticks.
- **on_screen**: Cars zoom past a school gate; each tap adds a stick. On the fifth tap a red line swoops across the four sticks and the bundle glows '5'.
- **check**: A tally has 2 bundles and 3 single sticks. What number does it show? — Answer: 13

### step_2: Pictographs and the Key
- **tts**:
  - A pictograph uses small pictures to show numbers. The key tells you what one picture stands for.
  - Half a picture stands for half of the key. If one picture is 10, half a picture is 5.
- **on_screen**: An ice-cream pictograph fills up row by row. The child taps the key, and every cone flips to show '5'. A half cone flips to show its value too.
- **check**: The key says 1 picture = 4 books. How many books do 3 pictures show? — Answer: 12

### step_3: Bar Graphs and Their Scale
- **tts**:
  - In a bar graph, the length of each bar shows a number. Read where the bar ends against the scale.
  - Check how much each line on the scale is worth. The lines may go up in twos, fives or tens.
- **on_screen**: Bars grow up from the bottom of a rainfall graph. A dotted line slides from the top of a bar to the scale and the number lights up.
- **check**: A scale is labelled 0, 10, 20 with a thin line halfway between labels. A bar stops on the thin line between 10 and 20. What does it show? — Answer: 15

### step_4: Reading Tables
- **tts**:
  - A table lists data in rows and columns. Find the right row, then read across.
  - Tables, tally charts, pictographs and bar graphs can all show the same data in different ways.
- **on_screen**: A table of favourite fruits turns into a tally chart, then a pictograph, then a bar graph, while the numbers stay the same.
- **check**: A table shows Red 7, Blue 4. How many in all? — Answer: 11

### step_5: More, Fewer and Total
- **tts**:
  - 'How many more' or 'how many fewer' means find the difference: subtract.
  - 'In all' or 'altogether' means add. For two-step questions, do one step at a time.
- **on_screen**: Two bars stand side by side; the extra part of the taller bar is highlighted and labelled with the difference.
- **check**: Mango has 14 votes and Guava has 6. How many more votes does Mango have? — Answer: 8

### step_6: Fair Comparison and Checking Graphs
- **tts**:
  - Before comparing two pictographs, check their keys. More pictures does not always mean more!
  - To check a graph, read every bar or row and compare it with the table, one item at a time.
- **on_screen**: Two pictographs with different keys appear. The child converts both rows into numbers before choosing which is bigger. Then a wrong bar flashes until the child fixes it.
- **check**: School X has 3 pictures with 1 picture = 10. School Y has 5 pictures with 1 picture = 5. Which school has more? — Answer: School X (30 is more than 25)

### Q02
- **id**: g4-maths-ch07-a-02
- **stem**: Gopal is keeping a tally of 17 cars passing his school gate. Each bundle is four upright sticks with a line across. What will his finished tally show?
- **options**:
  - A) 4 bundles and 1 single stick
  - B) 3 bundles and 2 single sticks
  - C) 1 bundle and 7 single sticks
  - D) 3 bundles and 3 single sticks
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: The line across is the fifth mark, so each bundle stands for 5. 17 = 5 + 5 + 5 + 2, so 3 bundles and 2 single sticks. '4 bundles and 1 single stick' is tempting if you count a bundle as only its 4 upright sticks (4 × 4 + 1 = 17), but that tally really shows 21.
- **hint**: Remember the line across is a mark as well. See how many whole bundles fit into the cars Gopal counts.
- **skill**: tally_make
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: recall
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: mathematical

### Q04
- **id**: g4-maths-ch07-a-04
- **stem**: Runs scored in a street-cricket match: Aarav 26, Bela 39, Chirag 14, Diya 31. How many more runs did the top scorer make than the lowest scorer?
- **options**:
  - A) 53
  - B) 25
  - C) 8
  - D) 17
- **answer**: B
- **explanation**: The top scorer is Bela with 39 and the lowest is Chirag with 14. 39 − 14 = 25. 53 is tempting because 39 + 14 = 53, but 'how many more' asks for the difference, not the total.
- **hint**: Find the most runs and the fewest runs in the list, then find the gap between those two numbers.
- **skill**: table_compare
- **difficulty**: easy

- **stem**: Meghna's tally of shells shows 3 bundles (four sticks with a line across) and 4 single sticks. She finds 2 more shells. How should her tally look now?
- **options**:
  - A) 4 bundles and 1 single stick
  - B) 3 bundles and 6 single sticks
  - C) 5 bundles and 1 single stick
  - D) 4 bundles and 2 single sticks
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: The tally showed 15 + 4 = 19 shells; now there are 21. The 5th single mark becomes a line across, so 21 = 4 bundles and 1 single stick. - **hint**: Add the new shells' marks to the tally. Whenever loose sticks fill up a bundle, the next mark goes across.
- **skill**: tally_update
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: application
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: mathematical
