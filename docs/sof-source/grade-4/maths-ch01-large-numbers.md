## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Big Numbers All Around Us
- **tts**:
  - A big cricket stadium can hold forty thousand people! That is a large number.
  - Today, let's read, write and play with big numbers.
- **on_screen**: A cartoon stadium fills up with tiny fans while a counter ticks up to 40,000; a school bus odometer and a library shelf with 2,500 books flash by. Child taps "Let's go!".
- **check**: How many digits does 40,000 have? — Answer: 5

### step_2: The Place Value Chart
- **tts**:
  - Each place is worth ten times the place on its right.
  - Nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine plus one is ten thousand!
- **on_screen**: Place value chart with columns Ten Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones. Child drags the digits of 46,215 into the right columns; then a bead counter rolls 9,999 to 10,000 as a new column lights up.
- **check**: In 46,215, which digit is in the ten thousands place? — Answer: 4

### step_3: Face Value and Place Value
- **tts**:
  - Face value is just the digit. Place value depends on where it sits.
  - In thirty-seven thousand five hundred, the seven is worth seven thousand.
- **on_screen**: Tap any digit of 37,582 to flip a card showing its face value on one side and its place value on the other.
- **check**: What is the place value of 5 in 25,309? — Answer: 5,000

### step_4: Expanded Form and Number Names
- **tts**:
  - Expanded form breaks a number into the worth of each digit.
  - Zero holds an empty place, but we don't say it aloud.
- **on_screen**: 46,083 splits into blocks 40,000 + 6,000 + 80 + 3; the name card "forty-six thousand eighty-three" builds word by word as each block is tapped, and the 0 in the hundreds place glows softly.
- **check**: Which number is 30,000 + 500 + 7? — Answer: 30,507

### step_5: Comparing and Ordering
- **tts**:
  - More digits means a bigger number.
  - Same number of digits? Compare from the left, one place at a time.
- **on_screen**: Two school-trip buses show distances 48,309 m and 48,390 m; child taps the longer trip, then drags four book-fair totals into order from smallest to greatest.
- **check**: Which is bigger, 48,309 or 48,390? — Answer: 48,390

### step_6: Make the Greatest and Smallest Numbers
- **tts**:
  - For the greatest number, put the biggest digit first.
  - For the smallest, start with the smallest digit, but never with zero!
- **on_screen**: Digit tiles 3, 0, 8, 5; child arranges them into the greatest and then the smallest 4-digit number. A buzzer sounds if 0 is placed first.
- **check**: What is the smallest 4-digit number using 3, 0, 8 and 5 once each? — Answer: 3,058

### step_7: Rounding Off
- **tts**:
  - Rounding gives a quick, friendly number. Look at the digit just to the right.
  - Five or more? Round up. Less than five? Round down.
- **on_screen**: A number line from 6,400 to 6,500 with a ball at 6,472; child slides the ball to the nearer hundred. Then the same for nearest 10 and nearest 1,000.
- **check**: Round 6,472 to the nearest hundred. — Answer: 6,500

### step_8: Before, After and Meeting One Lakh
- **tts**:
  - The successor is one more. The predecessor is one less.
  - Ninety-nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine plus one is one lakh!
- **on_screen**: A number train with carriages 19,998, 19,999, ?, 20,001 and a pattern strip 25,000 → 26,000 → 27,000 → ?; then an odometer rolls from 99,999 to 1,00,000 and a new Lakhs column appears on the chart.
- **check**: What is the predecessor of 20,000? — Answer: 19,999
