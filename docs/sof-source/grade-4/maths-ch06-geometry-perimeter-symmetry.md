## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Sides, Corners and Right Angles
- **tts**:
  - A closed shape made of straight lines has sides and corners. A triangle has three of each, and a hexagon has six.
  - A right angle is a square corner, like the corner of your notebook. A rectangle has four of them.
- **on_screen**: Shapes slide in one by one; the child taps each corner to count it. A paper-corner tool appears and the child drags it onto corners: it glows green on right angles and orange on slanted ones.
- **check**: How many right-angle corners does a rectangle have? — Answer: 4

### step_2: Perimeter: The Distance Around
- **tts**:
  - Perimeter is the total length all the way around a shape. Just add up the sides!
  - A square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is four times one side.
- **on_screen**: An ant walks around a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle while a counter adds 6, 4, 6, 4. Then the child drags a fence around a square park and sees 4 × side.
- **check**: What is the perimeter of a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle? — Answer: 20 cm

### step_3: Perimeter on a Grid
- **tts**:
  - For a shape on a grid, trace the outline and count every square edge you pass.
  - Cutting a square out of a corner does not always make the perimeter smaller. Count to check!
- **on_screen**: An L-shape on a grid; each edge lights up as the child traces it, and the count grows. A corner square pops out and the count stays the same.
- **check**: A rectangle 3 squares long and 2 squares wide is drawn on a grid. How many square edges go around it? — Answer: 10

### step_4: Area: Counting Squares
- **tts**:
  - Area is the space inside a shape. Count how many unit squares cover it.
  - Two half squares make one whole square.
- **on_screen**: The child paints squares inside a shape and a counter shows the area. Triangle halves snap together into whole squares.
- **check**: A shape covers 6 whole squares and 2 half squares. What is its area? — Answer: 7 square units

### step_5: Lines of Symmetry
- **tts**:
  - A line of symmetry folds a shape into two halves that match exactly.
  - To complete a symmetric picture, copy each square the same distance away on the other side of the mirror line.
- **on_screen**: A paper butterfly folds along a dotted line and the halves match. Then half a rangoli sits beside a mirror line and the child taps squares to finish the other half.
- **check**: How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle that is not a square have? — Answer: 2

### step_6: Solids, Faces, Edges and Nets
- **tts**:
  - A cube has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 corners. A cuboid has the same numbers, but its faces are rectangles.
  - A net is a flat pattern that folds up into a solid. Not every set of six squares folds into a cube!
- **on_screen**: A 3D cube spins; faces, edges and corners light up in turn. A cross-shaped net folds up into a cube, then a straight row of six squares tries and fails.
- **check**: How many corners does a cube have? — Answer: 8

### step_7: Tangrams and Counting Shapes
- **tts**:
  - A tangram is a square cut into seven pieces: five triangles, one square and one parallelogram.
  - When counting shapes in a figure, count the small ones first, then look for bigger ones made by joining them.
- **on_screen**: The seven tangram pieces fly apart and back together. Then a triangle cut by one line from its top corner appears, and triangles light up one by one as the child finds them.
- **check**: A triangle is cut into two parts by one line from its top corner to its base. How many triangles are there in the figure? — Answer: 3

### Q01
- **id**: g4-maths-ch06-a-01
- **stem**: Ishaan draws a triangle, a square and a pentagon on his slate. He counts all their corners together. How many corners does he count?
- **options**:
  - A) 12 corners
  - B) 7 corners
  - C) 10 corners
  - D) 15 corners
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: A triangle has 3 corners, a square has 4 and a pentagon has 5, so 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 corners. 7 corners is tempting if you stop after the triangle and the square (3 + 4), but the pentagon's corners must be added too.
- **hint**: A straight-sided shape has as many corners as sides. Count the corners of each shape on the slate, then add.
- **skill**: polygon_corners
- **difficulty**: easy
- **type**: recall
- **pictorial**: no
- **olympiad_section**: mathematical

- **id**: g4-maths-ch06-a-03
- **stem**: Kriti glues a gold border all around a square photo frame with sides of 15 cm. How long is the border?
- **options**:
  - A) 60 cm
  - B) 30 cm
  - C) 225 cm
  - D) 45 cm
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: A square has 4 equal sides, so the border is 15 + 15 + 15 + 15 = 4 × 15 = 60 cm. 225 is tempting because 15 × 15 = 225, but that measures the space inside (area), not the length around.
- **hint**: The border goes around all the sides, and a square's sides are all the same length.
