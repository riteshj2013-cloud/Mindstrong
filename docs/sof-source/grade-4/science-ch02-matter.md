# Chapter 2: Solids, Liquids and Gases

## Meta
- Grade: 4
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: matter, solid, liquid, gas, states of matter, shape, volume, change of state
- Source basis: NCERT Class 4 / typical NSO themes (original items only)
- Language: Grade 4 (clear, high visual/TTS)

## Learning objectives
- I can explain that everything around me that takes up space and has weight is called matter.
- I can sort things into solids, liquids and gases using everyday examples.
- I can tell how solids, liquids and gases differ in shape and volume (the space they take up).
- I can name the changes melting, freezing, evaporation, boiling and condensation.
- I can use simple home activities, like pouring water or blowing a balloon, to show how matter behaves.
- I can explain everyday events, like wet clothes drying or a cold glass getting wet outside.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Everything Is Matter
- **Visual:** A bright kitchen. A stone, a glass of milk and a floating balloon glow one by one with labels.
- **TTS:** "Look around! A stone, milk and air all take up space. We call all of them matter."
- **Interaction:** Tap-to-reveal: child taps 6 kitchen objects; each shows a "Matter!" badge. Then a quick yes/no: "Does air take up space?" (Yes — a balloon gets bigger when air goes in.)

### Scene 2: Solids Keep Their Shape
- **Visual:** A book, a stone and an ice cube are placed into a round bowl, then a square box. Their shapes stay the same.
- **TTS:** "Solids keep their own shape and size. A book stays a book in any box."
- **Interaction:** Drag-and-drop: move a book, stone, ice cube and coin into different containers and watch the shape stay fixed. Then sort 6 picture cards into "Solid" or "Not solid".

### Scene 3: Liquids Take the Shape of Their Container
- **Visual:** One jug of blue water is poured into a tall glass, a flat plate and a star-shaped mould. A measuring line shows the same amount each time.
- **TTS:** "Liquids flow. They take the shape of their container, but the amount stays the same."
- **Interaction:** Pour simulator: child tilts the jug into three containers. A meter shows "Same amount of water". Bonus tap: water, milk, oil, honey — "Which flows slowest?" (Honey.)

### Scene 4: Gases Fill All the Space
- **Visual:** A child blows up a balloon; then a room where the smell of fresh rotis slowly spreads from the kitchen to every corner.
- **TTS:** "Gases have no fixed shape or size. They spread out to fill all the space."
- **Interaction:** Blow-the-balloon: child holds a button to blow air in and sees the balloon fill evenly. Then a syringe demo: push a plunger on air (it moves in) versus water (it hardly moves).

### Scene 5: Changing States — Ice, Water, Steam
- **Visual:** A circle diagram: ice cube → water → steam from a kettle → drops on a cold lid → back to water → freezer → ice. Side panel: wet clothes drying on a sunny line.
- **TTS:** "Heat melts ice into water. More heat makes vapour. Cooling turns it back again."
- **Interaction:** Arrow-matching game: drag the words Melting, Freezing, Boiling, Evaporation and Condensation onto the correct arrows. Then a "Where did the water go?" question about drying clothes (into the air as water vapour).

## Quiz Set A — 24 MCQs

### A1 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a solid?
- A) Milk
- B) Stone
- C) Air
- D) Juice

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A stone has its own fixed shape and size, so it is a solid. Milk and juice are liquids, and air is a gas.

### A2 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a liquid?
- A) Pencil
- B) Steam
- C) Oil
- D) Brick

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Oil flows and takes the shape of the bottle it is kept in, so it is a liquid. A pencil and a brick are solids, and steam is a gas.

### A3 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a gas?
- A) Air inside a balloon
- B) An ice cube
- C) Honey
- D) A wooden spoon

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Air is a gas. It spreads out to fill the whole balloon. Honey is a liquid, and an ice cube and a spoon are solids.

### A4 (Easy)
**Stem:** Anything that takes up space and has weight is called ______.
- A) Energy
- B) Light
- C) Sound
- D) Matter

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Matter is anything that takes up space and has weight. Solids, liquids and gases are all matter. Light and sound are not matter.

### A5 (Easy)
**Stem:** An ice cube is left on a plate on a warm day. What does it turn into?
- A) Steam
- B) Salt
- C) Water
- D) Air

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Warmth melts the ice, and it becomes liquid water. This change from solid to liquid is called melting.

### A6 (Easy)
**Stem:** A book lying on a table keeps its shape. This is because a book is a ______.
- A) Solid
- B) Liquid
- C) Gas
- D) Shadow

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Solids have a fixed shape. A book does not spread out or flow, so it is a solid.

### A7 (Easy)
**Stem:** Meera pours milk from a jug into a round bowl. What shape does the milk take?
- A) It keeps the shape of the jug
- B) It becomes a square
- C) It rolls into a ball
- D) It takes the shape of the bowl

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Milk is a liquid. Liquids do not have their own shape, so they take the shape of the container they are in.

### A8 (Easy)
**Stem:** What goes into a balloon when you blow into it?
- A) Water
- B) Air
- C) Sand
- D) Milk

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** When we blow, air from our body goes into the balloon. The air spreads out and makes the balloon bigger.

### A9 (Easy)
**Stem:** A tray of water is kept in the freezer for a few hours. What does the water become?
- A) Ice
- B) Steam
- C) Oil
- D) Air

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Strong cold turns liquid water into solid ice. This change is called freezing.

### A10 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which group has ONLY solids?
- A) Chair, milk, stone
- B) Air, coin, water
- C) Coin, chair, stone
- D) Water, juice, oil

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A coin, a chair and a stone all have a fixed shape and size, so they are all solids. The other groups mix in liquids or gases.

### A11 (Medium)
**Stem:** The change of a liquid into a solid by cooling is called ______.
- A) Melting
- B) Boiling
- C) Evaporation
- D) Freezing

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Freezing means a liquid becomes a solid when it is cooled, like water turning into ice. Melting is the opposite change.

### A12 (Medium)
**Stem:** Wet clothes on a line become dry in the sun. Where does the water go?
- A) It goes only into the ground
- B) It goes into the air as water vapour
- C) It turns into cloth
- D) It turns into ice

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The sun's heat slowly changes the water in the clothes into water vapour, a gas, which mixes with the air. This is called evaporation.

### A13 (Medium)
**Stem:** Which of these has a fixed volume (amount) but NO fixed shape?
- A) Brick
- B) Water
- C) Air
- D) Eraser

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Water is a liquid. Its amount stays the same, but its shape changes with the container. A brick and an eraser have a fixed shape, and air has neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.

### A14 (Medium)
**Stem:** Tiny water drops appear on the outside of a glass of ice-cold water. What is this change called?
- A) Melting
- B) Freezing
- C) Boiling
- D) Condensation

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Water vapour in the air touches the cold glass, cools down and turns into tiny water drops. A gas turning into a liquid is called condensation.

### A15 (Medium)
**Stem:** An incense stick is lit in one corner. Soon, the whole room smells nice. Why?
- A) The smoke and smell are gases that spread to fill the room
- B) The incense stick is a liquid that flows
- C) Solids move very fast across a room
- D) The walls make the smell

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Gases have no fixed shape or size. They spread out in every direction until they fill all the space they can reach.

### A16 (Medium)
**Stem:** Which of these is NOT a liquid?
- A) Honey
- B) Coconut oil
- C) Salt
- D) Vinegar

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Salt is made of tiny solid crystals that keep their own shape. Honey, coconut oil and vinegar are liquids that flow.

### A17 (Medium)
**Stem:** Water in a kettle is heated until it bubbles quickly and turns into steam. What is this called?
- A) Freezing
- B) Melting
- C) Condensation
- D) Boiling

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** When water is heated strongly, it bubbles and quickly turns into steam. This is called boiling.

### A18 (Medium)
**Stem:** One cup of water is poured first into a tall thin bottle, then into a wide flat bowl. What happens to the amount of water?
- A) It stays the same
- B) It becomes more in the bowl
- C) It becomes less in the bottle
- D) It disappears

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A liquid has a fixed volume. Only its shape changes with the container. The amount of water stays one cup.

### A19 (Medium)
**Stem:** An ice cream is left outside on a hot afternoon. What happens to it?
- A) It becomes harder
- B) It turns into a gas at once
- C) It melts and becomes runny
- D) It turns into stone

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Heat melts the solid ice cream into a liquid. That is why it drips and becomes runny.

### A20 (Medium)
**Stem:** We cannot see air. How can we tell that air is around us?
- A) Air has a bright colour
- B) We feel the wind and see leaves move
- C) Air always smells sweet
- D) Air is hard to touch

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Moving air is called wind. We feel it on our face and see it moving leaves, flags and kites, so we know air is there.

### A21 (Hard)
**Stem:** Sugar can be poured from a jar like water. Why is sugar still called a solid?
- A) Each tiny grain of sugar has its own fixed shape
- B) Sugar tastes sweet
- C) Sugar is white in colour
- D) Sugar is kept in a jar

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Sugar pours because it is made of many tiny grains. Each grain keeps its own shape and size, so sugar is a solid. Taste and colour do not decide the state.

### A22 (Hard)
**Stem:** Riya closes the tip of a syringe full of air and pushes the plunger. It moves in a little. With water inside, it hardly moves at all. What does this show?
- A) Water is a gas
- B) Air cannot be pushed at all
- C) Water takes up no space
- D) Air can be squeezed into less space, but water cannot easily

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Gases like air can be pressed into a smaller space. Liquids like water have a fixed volume, so they are very hard to squeeze.

### A23 (Hard)
**Stem:** Ice changes into water, and then water changes into water vapour. Which two changes happened, in order?
- A) Freezing, then condensation
- B) Melting, then evaporation
- C) Boiling, then melting
- D) Condensation, then freezing

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Solid ice to liquid water is melting. Liquid water to water vapour is evaporation (or boiling when heated strongly).

### A24 (Hard)
**Stem:** On which day will a puddle of water dry up the fastest?
- A) A cold, cloudy, still day
- B) A rainy day
- C) A hot, sunny, windy day
- D) A cold night

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Heat from the sun and moving air both help water evaporate faster. So a hot, sunny, windy day dries the puddle quickest.

## Quiz Set B — 24 MCQs

### B1 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a liquid?
- A) Rock
- B) Smoke
- C) Milk
- D) Spoon

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Milk flows and takes the shape of its glass, so it is a liquid. A rock and a spoon are solids, and smoke is mostly gas.

### B2 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these keeps its own shape in any container?
- A) A marble
- B) Water
- C) Juice
- D) Air

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A marble is a solid, so it keeps its round shape wherever you put it. Water and juice take the shape of the container, and air fills it.

### B3 (Easy)
**Stem:** A football is pumped up until it is firm. What is inside it?
- A) Water
- B) Sand
- C) Oil
- D) Air

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A football is filled with air. The air spreads out and fills the whole inside of the ball.

### B4 (Easy)
**Stem:** What are the three states of matter?
- A) Hot, cold and warm
- B) Solid, liquid and gas
- C) Red, blue and green
- D) Big, small and tiny

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Matter is found in three states: solid, liquid and gas. For example, ice, water and water vapour.

### B5 (Easy)
**Stem:** Water vapour rising from a pot of hot water is a ______.
- A) Gas
- B) Solid
- C) Liquid
- D) Metal

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** When water is heated, it changes into water vapour, which is a gas. It rises and spreads into the air.

### B6 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a solid?
- A) Coconut water
- B) Air
- C) Rain water
- D) An ice cube

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** An ice cube has a fixed shape and size, so it is a solid. It is frozen water.

### B7 (Easy)
**Stem:** A glass of water spills on the floor. What does the water do?
- A) It stands up tall like a tower
- B) It stays in the shape of a cube
- C) It spreads out and flows
- D) It floats up in the air

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Water is a liquid, and liquids flow. Without a container, it spreads out over the floor.

### B8 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these can flow?
- A) Brick
- B) Honey
- C) Pencil
- D) Plate

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Honey is a liquid, so it can flow, even if it flows slowly. A brick, a pencil and a plate are solids.

### B9 (Easy)
**Stem:** What does melting mean?
- A) A liquid changes into a solid
- B) A gas changes into a liquid
- C) A liquid changes into a gas
- D) A solid changes into a liquid

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Melting happens when a solid is heated and becomes a liquid, like butter melting in a hot pan.

### B10 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these must be kept in a tightly closed container so it does not escape into the air?
- A) Cooking gas in a cylinder
- B) A stone
- C) A book
- D) A toy car

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Gases spread out in all directions, so they must be kept in closed containers. Solids like a stone, book or toy car stay where they are.

### B11 (Medium)
**Stem:** Which pair has a FIXED volume (amount of space taken up)?
- A) Air and steam
- B) Stone and water
- C) Smoke and air
- D) Steam and smoke

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Solids like a stone and liquids like water both have a fixed volume. Gases like air and steam spread to fill any space, so their volume is not fixed.

### B12 (Medium)
**Stem:** After a hot bath, the bathroom mirror becomes foggy. Why?
- A) The mirror melts
- B) Ice forms on the mirror
- C) Water vapour cools on the mirror and turns into tiny drops
- D) Soap jumps onto the mirror

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Warm water vapour from the bath touches the cooler mirror and changes into tiny water drops. This is condensation.

### B13 (Medium)
**Stem:** Melted wax drips down a candle and becomes hard again as it cools. What is this hardening called?
- A) Freezing (becoming solid)
- B) Boiling
- C) Evaporation
- D) Melting

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** When a liquid cools and becomes a solid, it is called freezing or solidifying. Liquid wax turns back into solid wax.

### B14 (Medium)
**Stem:** Why does the air inside a balloon take the shape of the balloon?
- A) Air is a solid
- B) Air has its own fixed shape
- C) Air spreads out to fill all the space inside the balloon
- D) Air is a heavy liquid

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Air is a gas. Gases have no fixed shape, so they spread out to fill whatever container they are in.

### B15 (Medium)
**Stem:** Which of these will melt quickly if you hold it in your warm hand?
- A) Iron nail
- B) Stone
- C) Glass marble
- D) A piece of chocolate

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Chocolate melts at a low temperature, so even the warmth of your hand can melt it. A nail, stone and marble need much more heat.

### B16 (Medium)
**Stem:** Ravi pours a full cup of juice from a cup onto a plate. What changes?
- A) The amount of juice
- B) Only the shape of the juice
- C) The colour of the juice
- D) The taste of the juice

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A liquid takes the shape of its container, but its amount, colour and taste stay the same. Only the shape changes.

### B17 (Medium)
**Stem:** A dish of water is kept near a sunny window. After a few days, the water is gone. What is this called?
- A) Freezing
- B) Melting
- C) Condensation
- D) Evaporation

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Slowly, the water changes into water vapour and goes into the air. This change from liquid to gas is called evaporation.

### B18 (Medium)
**Stem:** Find the odd one out.
- A) Wooden block
- B) Milk
- C) Oil
- D) Vinegar

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A wooden block is a solid. Milk, oil and vinegar are all liquids.

### B19 (Medium)
**Stem:** Water vapour rises high into the sky and cools down. What does it form?
- A) Sand
- B) Stones
- C) Clouds made of tiny water drops
- D) Ice cream

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** High up, the air is cold. Water vapour cools and condenses into tiny water drops that together make clouds.

### B20 (Medium)
**Stem:** Why does a bicycle tyre become firm when we pump more air into it?
- A) The air turns into a solid
- B) More air is squeezed into the same space
- C) The air turns into water
- D) The tyre melts and grows

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Air is a gas and can be pressed into a smaller space. Pumping pushes more and more air into the tyre, which makes it firm.

### B21 (Hard)
**Stem:** Which statement is TRUE?
- A) Liquids have a fixed shape
- B) Gases have a fixed volume
- C) Solids take the shape of their container
- D) Liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Liquids keep the same amount but change shape to fit their container. Solids keep their own shape, and gases have neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.

### B22 (Hard)
**Stem:** Some ice cubes are put in a glass. After a while, the ice becomes water, and drops appear on the outside of the glass. Which two changes happened?
- A) Melting of the ice and condensation on the glass
- B) Boiling and freezing
- C) Evaporation and freezing
- D) Melting and boiling

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The ice melts into water inside the glass. Water vapour in the air cools on the cold glass and condenses into drops outside.

### B23 (Hard)
**Stem:** Meena covers a pot of boiling water with a lid. When she lifts the lid, she sees water drops under it. What is the correct order of changes?
- A) Water → ice → water
- B) Water → steam → ice
- C) Water → water vapour → water drops
- D) Steam → ice → water

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Boiling turns water into water vapour. The vapour touches the cooler lid and condenses back into water drops.

### B24 (Hard)
**Stem:** A closed bottle is half full of juice. What is in the top half of the bottle?
- A) Nothing at all, it is truly empty
- B) Air, which is a gas
- C) More juice that we cannot see
- D) A hidden solid

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The "empty" part of the bottle is filled with air. Air is a gas, and it fills any space that is not taken up by the juice.
