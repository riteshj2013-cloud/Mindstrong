# Chapter 2: Solids, Liquids and Gases

## Meta
- Grade: 4
- Subject: Science
- Theme tags: matter, solid, liquid, gas, states of matter, shape, volume, change of state, pictorial, svg-diagrams
- Source basis: NCERT Class 4 / typical NSO themes (original items only)
- Language: Grade 4 (clear, high visual/TTS)

## Learning objectives
- I can explain that everything around me that takes up space and has weight is called matter.
- I can sort things into solids, liquids and gases using everyday examples.
- I can tell how solids, liquids and gases differ in shape and volume (the space they take up).
- I can name the changes melting, freezing, evaporation, boiling and condensation.
- I can use simple home activities, like pouring water or blowing a balloon, to show how matter behaves.
- I can explain everyday events, like wet clothes drying or a cold glass getting wet outside.

## Interactive lesson outline

### Scene 1: Everything Is Matter
- **Visual:** A bright kitchen. A stone, a glass of milk and a floating balloon glow one by one with labels.
- **TTS:** "Look around! A stone, milk and air all take up space. We call all of them matter."
- **Interaction:** Tap-to-reveal: child taps 6 kitchen objects; each shows a "Matter!" badge. Then a quick yes/no: "Does air take up space?" (Yes — a balloon gets bigger when air goes in.)

### Scene 2: Solids Keep Their Shape
- **Visual:** A book, a stone and an ice cube are placed into a round bowl, then a square box. Their shapes stay the same.
- **TTS:** "Solids keep their own shape and size. A book stays a book in any box."
- **Interaction:** Drag-and-drop: move a book, stone, ice cube and coin into different containers and watch the shape stay fixed. Then sort 6 picture cards into "Solid" or "Not solid".

### Scene 3: Liquids Take the Shape of Their Container
- **Visual:** One jug of blue water is poured into a tall glass, a flat plate and a star-shaped mould. A measuring line shows the same amount each time.
- **TTS:** "Liquids flow. They take the shape of their container, but the amount stays the same."
- **Interaction:** Pour simulator: child tilts the jug into three containers. A meter shows "Same amount of water". Bonus tap: water, milk, oil, honey — "Which flows slowest?" (Honey.)

### Scene 4: Gases Fill All the Space
- **Visual:** A child blows up a balloon; then a room where the smell of fresh rotis slowly spreads from the kitchen to every corner.
- **TTS:** "Gases have no fixed shape or size. They spread out to fill all the space."
- **Interaction:** Blow-the-balloon: child holds a button to blow air in and sees the balloon fill evenly. Then a syringe demo: push a plunger on air (it moves in) versus water (it hardly moves).

### Scene 5: Changing States — Ice, Water, Steam
- **Visual:** A circle diagram: ice cube → water → steam from a kettle → drops on a cold lid → back to water → freezer → ice. Side panel: wet clothes drying on a sunny line.
- **TTS:** "Heat melts ice into water. More heat makes vapour. Cooling turns it back again."
- **Interaction:** Arrow-matching game: drag the words Melting, Freezing, Boiling, Evaporation and Condensation onto the correct arrows. Then a "Where did the water go?" question about drying clothes (into the air as water vapour).

**Stem:** Look at the three jars. Which jar shows a SOLID?
- A) Jar 2
- B) Jar 1
- C) Jar 3
- D) None of the jars

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Jar 1 has an ice cube. It keeps its own fixed shape and does not spread out, so it is a solid. Jar 2 has water (a liquid with a flat, level top), and Jar 3 has a gas spread all through the jar.

### A2 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a liquid?
- A) Pencil
- B) Steam
- C) Oil
- D) Brick

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Oil flows and takes the shape of the bottle it is kept in, so it is a liquid. A pencil and a brick are solids, and steam is a gas.

### A3 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which of these is a gas?
- A) Air inside a balloon
- B) An ice cube
- C) Honey
- D) A wooden spoon

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Air is a gas. It spreads out to fill the whole balloon. Honey is a liquid, and an ice cube and a spoon are solids.

### A4 (Easy)
**Stem:** Anything that takes up space and has weight is called ______.
- A) Energy
- B) Light
- C) Sound
- D) Matter

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Matter is anything that takes up space and has weight. Solids, liquids and gases are all matter. Light and sound are not matter.

### A5 (Easy)
**Stem:** An ice cube is left on a plate on a warm day. What does it turn into?
- A) Steam
- B) Salt
- C) Water
- D) Air

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Warmth melts the ice, and it becomes liquid water. This change from solid to liquid is called melting.

### A6 (Easy)
**Stem:** A book lying on a table keeps its shape. This is because a book is a ______.
- A) Solid
- B) Liquid
- C) Gas
- D) Shadow

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Solids have a fixed shape. A book does not spread out or flow, so it is a solid.

- A) It keeps the shape of the jug
- B) It becomes a square

### A8 (Easy)
**Stem:** What goes into a balloon when you blow into it?
- A) Water
- B) Air
- C) Sand
- D) Milk

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** When we blow, air from our body goes into the balloon. The air spreads out and makes the balloon bigger.

### A9 (Easy)
**Stem:** A tray of water is kept in the freezer for a few hours. What does the water become?
- A) Ice
- B) Steam
- C) Oil
- D) Air

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Strong cold turns liquid water into solid ice. This change is called freezing.

### A10 (Easy)
**Stem:** Which group has ONLY solids?
- A) Chair, milk, stone
- B) Air, coin, water
- C) Coin, chair, stone
- D) Water, juice, oil

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A coin, a chair and a stone all have a fixed shape and size, so they are all solids. The other groups mix in liquids or gases.

### A11 (Medium)
**Stem:** The change of a liquid into a solid by cooling is called ______.
- A) Melting
- B) Boiling
- C) Evaporation
- D) Freezing

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Freezing means a liquid becomes a solid when it is cooled, like water turning into ice. Melting is the opposite change.
