## Interactive lesson outline
- L1: Hello, story spotter! Are you ready to read some fun stories with me?
- L2: When we read, we don't just look at the words. We find out what the story is really about.
- L3: First, ask: what is this story mostly about? That's the main idea.
- L4: Next, look for small details: who, what, where and when.
- L5: (optional) Words like first, next, then and at last tell us the order of things.
- L6: Sometimes a word is new. Don't worry! Read the words around it, and they will help you guess.
- L7: Let's try one together. Listen: "Mohan opened the door. His umbrella was dripping, and his shoes went squish, squish."
- L8: Hmm. A dripping umbrella and squishy shoes. What do you think happened outside?
- L9: Yes! It was raining. The story did not say "rain," but the clues told us.
- L10: And "squish"? Wet shoes make that sound. So squish is a soft, wet sound.
- L11: (optional) See? You are already a clue finder!
- L12: Now it's your turn. Read each story slowly, and then choose the best answer.
- L13: If you are not sure, go back and read that part again. The answer is in the story.
- L14: Well done, story spotter! Every story you read makes you a better reader.

## Passage bank (for quizzes)

### P1 — Chintu and the Red Slipper
Chintu the goat lived on Raju's farm. One morning, Raju's red slipper was missing. He looked under the bed. He looked behind the door. It was not there. Then his little sister Gauri laughed and pointed outside. Chintu was standing near the haystack. Something red was hanging from his mouth! Raju ran after him. Chintu ran around the well, past the hens and into the shed. At last, he dropped the slipper and bleated. The slipper was wet and chewed. Raju sighed, but he could not stop smiling. "Next time, I will keep my slippers on the shelf," he said.

### P2 — The Tiffin Swap
Asha's mother packed upma in her tiffin every Monday. Asha liked upma, but she was tired of it. Her friend Neel always brought aloo paratha. "Let's swap today," said Neel. Asha agreed happily. At lunch, she took a big bite of the paratha. It was very spicy! Her eyes watered, and she quickly drank some water. Neel was enjoying the upma. "This is so soft and tasty," he said. Asha laughed. "Maybe my mother's upma is not so bad after all." From then on, they shared their tiffins every Monday.

### P3 — Diya's Kite
On Makar Sankranti, Diya and her grandfather went up to the terrace. First, they tied the string to the kite. Next, Grandfather held the kite high while Diya held the reel. Then a strong breeze came, and the kite rose into the blue sky. It danced above the rooftops with many other kites. Suddenly, another kite came close. Diya pulled her string gently, and her kite moved away safely. When the sun went down, they wound up the string and went downstairs, tired but happy.
