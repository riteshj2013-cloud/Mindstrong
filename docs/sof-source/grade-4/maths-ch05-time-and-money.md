## Interactive Lesson Outline

### step_1: Reading a Clock to the Minute
- **tts**:
  - The long hand shows minutes. Each big number means five minutes, and each tiny mark is one minute.
  - The short hand shows the hour. When it is between two numbers, the hour is the smaller number.
- **on_screen**: A big clock with tiny minute marks. The long hand sweeps from 12 to 4 and then two marks more while a counter shows 5, 10, 15, 20, 21, 22. The child drags the short hand and sees it creep slowly between 6 and 7.
- **check**: The long hand is two marks past 4 and the short hand is between 6 and 7. What time is it? — Answer: 6:22

### step_2: a.m., p.m. and 24-hour Time
- **tts**:
  - From midnight to noon the time is a.m. From noon to midnight it is p.m.
  - Railways and airports use 24-hour time. For p.m. times, add twelve to the hour: 3 p.m. becomes 15:00.
- **on_screen**: A day-strip from midnight to midnight with a sun rising and setting. Cards 7:30 a.m., 3:15 p.m. and 9:45 p.m. flip to 07:30, 15:15 and 21:45 when tapped.
- **check**: Write 4:20 p.m. in 24-hour time. — Answer: 16:20

### step_3: How Long Did It Take?
- **tts**:
  - To find how long something takes, count up from the start time to the next full hour, then on to the end time.
  - Do not subtract times like ordinary numbers. An hour has sixty minutes, not one hundred!
- **on_screen**: A number-line of time from 9:40 to 10:15 with two hops: a 20-minute hop to 10:00 and a 15-minute hop to 10:15. Then a bus timetable appears and the child taps a row to see its journey time.
- **check**: A film starts at 2:45 p.m. and ends at 4:10 p.m. How long is it? — Answer: 1 hour 25 minutes

### step_4: Units of Time and Leap Years
- **tts**:
  - Sixty seconds make a minute, sixty minutes make an hour, and twenty-four hours make a day.
  - Seven days make a week and twelve months make a year. A leap year has 366 days, because February gets a twenty-ninth day.
- **on_screen**: A staircase of units: seconds → minutes → hours → days → weeks → months → years, with the change number on each step. A February page grows an extra square, 29, in years like 2028.
- **check**: How many days does a leap year have? — Answer: 366 days

### step_5: Calendar Detective
- **tts**:
  - Dates that are seven apart fall on the same day of the week.
  - If the 3rd is a Monday, then the 10th, 17th, 24th and 31st are Mondays too.
- **on_screen**: A calendar page where the child taps a date and the whole column lights up, showing the +7 jumps. Then a torn page shows only the top row and the child works out the 20th.
- **check**: If 5 March is a Friday, what day is 19 March? — Answer: Friday

### step_6: Rupees and Paise
- **tts**:
  - One hundred paise make one rupee. ₹3.25 means three rupees and twenty-five paise.
  - To change rupees to paise, multiply by a hundred. Be careful: fifty paise is .50, but five paise is .05.
- **on_screen**: A coin-and-note tray. The child drags ₹2 coins and ₹10 notes into a box to make ₹26, then a slider turns ₹4.50 into 450 paise.
- **check**: How many paise are there in ₹6.40? — Answer: 640 paise

### step_7: Smart Shopping: Bills, Change and Best Buys
- **tts**:
  - To make a bill, multiply each price by how many you buy, then add.
  - For change, count up from the bill to the money paid. To find the best buy, compare the price of one item.
- **on_screen**: A mini-market: the child fills a basket, the bill builds line by line, and a cashier counts change up from ₹68 to ₹100. Two packs of biscuits appear and the child taps the cheaper one per biscuit.
- **check**: A bill is ₹68 and you pay ₹100. How much change do you get? — Answer: ₹32

- **alt**: An analogue clock. The long minute hand points at 8. The short hour hand is between 7 and 8, closer to 8.
- **options**:
  - A) 8:40
  - B) 8:35
  - C) 7:08
  - D) 7:40
- **answer**: D
- **explanation**: The long minute hand is on 8, which means 8 × 5 = 40 minutes. The short hour hand has passed 7 but has not reached 8, so the time is 7:40. 8:40 is tempting because the hour hand is close to 8, but it has not reached 8 yet.
- **hint**: The short hand sits between two numbers, so use the smaller one. Count the long hand in jumps of five from the top.

### Q03
- **id**: g4-maths-ch05-a-03
- **stem**: A Republic Day quiz on TV starts at quarter past nine at night. How is this time written in 24-hour time?
- **options**:
  - A) 21:15
  - B) 19:15
  - C) 09:15
  - D) 21:45
- **answer**: A
- **explanation**: Quarter past nine is 9:15. At night it is a p.m. time, so add 12 to the hour: 9 + 12 = 21, giving 21:15. 09:15 is the same clock reading in the morning, not at night.
- **hint**: For a p.m. time, add twelve to the hour. 'Quarter past' means fifteen minutes after the hour.
