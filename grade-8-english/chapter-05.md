## Interactive lesson outline
- L1: Welcome. Today we practise the formats readers expect: formal letters, notices, articles and emails.
- L2: Format is not decoration. It tells the reader what kind of message this is and where to find the key facts.
- L3: A notice is brief and public. It needs the issuing body, the word NOTICE, a date, a clear heading, essential details, and a name with designation.
- L4: A formal letter carries sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body paragraphs, complimentary close and signature.
- L5: Remember: Dear Sir/Madam pairs with Yours faithfully; a named reader pairs with Yours sincerely.
- L6: An article for a school magazine needs a catchy heading, a byline, an engaging opening, developed body paragraphs and a satisfying close.
- L7: An email still needs a precise subject line, a proper greeting, clear paragraphs and a suitable close — even though it travels faster than paper.
- L8: (optional) Always ask: Who is my reader? What do I want them to do or understand?
- L9: Weak email subject: "Hello." Strong subject: "Request for permission to use the AV room on 12 November."
- L10: Weak notice: a long story about feelings. Strong notice: what, when, where, who, how — in as few words as honesty allows.
- L11: Articles may use a livelier voice than letters, but they still need facts, structure and a point of view the reader can follow.
- L12: Now it's your turn. For each question, match purpose to format and spot what is missing or misplaced.
- L13: Excellent. The right format makes serious readers take you seriously. Let's begin.

### E1 — Formal email
> **From:** nisha.k@example.com
> **To:** librarian@peakview.edu.in
> **Date:** 8 November 2026
> **Subject:** Request to extend library return date for Class 8 project books
>
> Dear Ms. Iyer,
>
> I am writing to request a one-week extension for returning the Class 8 project reference books issued last Monday. Our group is completing a display on water conservation for the Science Fair on 20 November, and we still need the books for captions and diagrams.
>
> We will ensure that all titles are returned by 22 November and kept in good condition. I would be grateful if you could approve this extension.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> Nisha Kapoor
> Class 8B

### P1 — Formal letter
> 14 Lake Road
> Indore – 452001
> 10 November 2026
>
> The Editor
> The City Herald
> Indore
>
> Subject: Concern over unsafe crossing near Green Park School
>
> Dear Sir/Madam,
>
> I wish to draw your attention to the zebra crossing outside Green Park School, which has faded badly and is ignored by many drivers during morning drop-off. Last week two students had to leap back onto the pavement when a scooter did not slow down.
>
> I request you to publish a report on this hazard and urge the traffic authorities to repaint the crossing and place a temporary marshal during school hours. Prompt action could prevent a serious accident.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Aarav Malhotra
> Resident, Lake Road

### P2 — School magazine article
> **When the Playground Went Quiet**
> By Meera Joseph, Class 8
>
> For years, the scrap of ground behind our canteen rang with cricket scores and laughter. Then the diggers came. In three days the pitch became a trench, and the trench became the skeleton of a new block.
>
> Nobody argues that classrooms matter. What students miss is a place to breathe between bells. During break we now crowd the corridors, and teachers spend precious minutes asking us to keep the way clear.
>
> The Student Council has proposed a simple fix: mark a temporary games zone on the front lawn until the block opens, with soft balls only. Small compromises keep school life human while buildings rise. Progress need not silence play.
